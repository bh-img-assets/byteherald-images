<p>OpenAI has raised $15 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, <a href="https://dealroom.co/news/172395-openai-raises-15b-at-a-1-25t-valuation-half-its-original-target-as-it-br/">CNBC's David Faber reported on Thursday, November 12, 2026</a>, citing people familiar with the matter, in a round that is half the $30 billion the company first sought and below the $1.4 trillion valuation it pitched to investors. The round has not formally closed and is expected to wrap up within the next couple of weeks, and OpenAI itself has not announced it, so the $15 billion figure is a reported total, not a confirmed one. What is confirmed is the price: OpenAI's own chief financial officer said on CNBC on November 6 that the round values the company at $1.25 trillion before the new money, and the people familiar with the matter described the raise as an opportunistic bridge to an initial public offering expected in 2027.</p>

<h2>The raise, in four dates</h2>
<p>The November round is the third act of a funding story that has run all year, and the four dates show a company that keeps raising the price while cutting the size. <a href="https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/">OpenAI announced on March 31, 2026, that it had $122 billion in committed capital at an $852 billion post-money valuation</a>. By late October <a href="https://ktwb.com/2026/10/30/openai-cuts-funding-target-by-10-billion-ft-reports/">the Financial Times reported that the company had cut its new funding target from $30 billion to $20 billion</a>, <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/openai-cut-its-30b-raise-to-20b-what.html">a cut we covered on October 31</a>, and that the valuation it sought was $1.25 trillion, down from the $1.4 trillion first reported in September. On November 6, <a href="https://www.unite.ai/openai-secures-11b-in-initial-funding-at-1-25t-valuation/">Reuters and Bloomberg reported that OpenAI had closed an $11 billion first portion of the round at the $1.25 trillion valuation</a>, and chief financial officer Sarah Friar <a href="https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2302518">told CNBC the same day that the round was ongoing and the valuation was $1.25 trillion before the new money</a>. On November 12, Faber reported the total had reached $15 billion, with the round still open and whether OpenAI would go beyond that figure unclear.</p>

<h2>The valuation math</h2>
<p>The reporting puts the $1.25 trillion valuation at about 47 percent above the $852 billion March valuation, and that comparison needs one correction before it can be used. The March figure was post-money, meaning it included the $122 billion just raised, while the November figure is pre-money, meaning it is the value before the new money. On a post-money basis the step-up is about the same as reported, because adding the $15 billion to the $1.25 trillion gives a November post-money value of about $1.265 trillion, which is about 48 percent above $852 billion, by our arithmetic. On a pre-money basis the step-up is larger: subtracting the $122 billion from the March post-money figure gives a March pre-money value of $730 billion, and $1.25 trillion is about 71 percent above that, also by our arithmetic. Either way the valuation rose by roughly half in about seven months, while the amount raised fell by half from what was first sought, which is the shape of a market that will still pay up for OpenAI but will no longer fund it at any size.</p>
<p>The other numbers in the round tell the same story from the other side. The $15 billion is $4 billion more than the $11 billion first close on November 6, and $5 billion under the $20 billion revised target, which means the company found another $4 billion in a week but is still short of even its reduced goal. Nvidia has put about $30 billion into OpenAI and SoftBank about $65 billion, according to Faber, which makes the two largest backers also the two companies with the most riding on the AI buildout. That concentration is the point of <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/anthropics-644b-ipo-file-every-number.html">the Anthropic IPO filing we audited on November 10</a>, which showed a rival lab choosing the public markets instead: Anthropic lists on Nasdaq on November 19, while OpenAI raises privately to bridge itself to a 2027 listing, so the two labs are taking opposite paths to the same destination, and the market will get to price both.</p>
<h2>What the money is for</h2>
<p>The money is a bridge, and the gap it bridges is visible in OpenAI's own numbers. <a href="https://cryptobriefing.com/openai-revenue-hits-10-billion-q3/">The Information reported on November 10, 2026, that OpenAI generated about $10 billion in third-quarter revenue, nearly 50 percent more than the previous quarter</a>, while <a href="https://startupfortune.com/openais-third-quarter-revenue-hits-10-billion-as-anthropic-prepares-its-ipo/">the Wall Street Journal reported operating losses of about $10.9 billion and an adjusted operating margin of negative 109 percent, improved from roughly negative 150 percent</a>. Revenue is growing by half in a quarter and the company still loses more than a dollar for every dollar it takes in, which is why the raise is opportunistic rather than desperate: the company is selling equity at a rising valuation to fund the compute buildout while the window is open, not because the cash has run out. The $15 billion covers about a quarter and a half of losses at the current quarterly rate, which means it buys time, not profitability, and the 2027 IPO is the event that has to close the gap.</p>
<p>That is also why the round matters beyond OpenAI. The company's funding rounds have set the price for the whole AI market all year: the March round at $852 billion reset every private valuation above it, and the November round at $1.25 trillion does the same, which is why the rest of the month's funding is measured against it in <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/six-ai-raises-added-up-232m-for-ai-that.html">our October funding ledger, which totaled $232 million across six rounds</a>. November's ledger, published with this post, shows one round taking 84 percent of the month's verified total, and that concentration is the real story of the $15 billion: it is not one round among many, it is the round that defines the month, and every other AI company raising money in November is raising it in a market OpenAI has just repriced.</p>
<h2>What is not confirmed</h2>
<p>Three things about the $15 billion are not established, and they should stay unestablished until OpenAI or a filing says otherwise. First, the $15 billion figure traces to one original reporting chain, CNBC's David Faber citing unnamed people familiar with the matter, and OpenAI has not announced the round or confirmed that total; the company declined to comment on the related November reporting. Second, the round has not formally closed, so the total could still move, and it is unclear whether OpenAI will go beyond $15 billion. Third, the reporting does not say whether the $15 billion includes the $11 billion reported on November 6, who invested in the additional $4 billion, or what the final size will be. The valuation, by contrast, is on the record from the company's own chief financial officer, which is why this article states the $1.25 trillion as a confirmed figure and the $15 billion as a reported one. The distinction is the whole story: the price is settled, the size is not, and the market is being asked to believe the first while waiting on the second.</p>
