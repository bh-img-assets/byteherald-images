<p>ByteHerald is an independent tech magazine. This page explains how we check a story before we publish it, so a reader can judge our work and catch our mistakes.</p>
<h2>Primary sources first</h2>
<p>We trace every news claim to the originator where one exists: a company filing, a product document, a research paper, a court record, or a direct statement. We link that first-party source beside the claim. Aggregator rewrites are never our only source for a factual claim. When a number comes from a filing or a study, we name the document and the period it covers.</p>
<h2>Numbers carry their method</h2>
<p>A number in a ByteHerald article should arrive with its unit, its date, and its source in the same passage. Where a sample or a method matters, we state it: how many people, documents, designs, or variants were involved, over what period, and what was measured. Where a figure is an estimate, a tally built by adding separate disclosures, or one analyst's calculation, we say so, and we say what would change it. We do not present a forecast as a fact, and we do not make guaranteed-return or absolute-safety claims.</p>
<h2>Original data, stated limits</h2>
<p>Our articles aim to add something no rewrite has: a comparison table, a reconciliation of conflicting totals, a timeline, a chart built from disclosed figures, or a checklist a reader can apply. Every chart carries its source in its footer. Where sources conflict, we show the conflict instead of picking the tidier number. Where a result is early, preclinical, or unreplicated, we say so in the article, not only in a footnote.</p>
<h2>Images are inspected before publication</h2>
<p>Every chart, diagram, and hero image is opened at full size and inspected before it is published, for overlapping or cut-off text, legibility at the blog's display width, and a clean, professional look. Images that fail are rebuilt and re-inspected. Charts carry ByteHerald branding and a source line only in their footer, never as text overlaid on the data. Charts and diagrams published by ByteHerald may be reused with credit to ByteHerald and a link to the article they came from.</p>
<h2>Developing stories and corrections</h2>
<p>When a covered story develops or a fact changes, we update the original article with a dated update note, or publish a follow-up that links back to the original and add a forward link from the original to the follow-up. We do not publish a competing post on the same angle, and we do not leave a stale fact standing silently. Corrections to published articles are logged publicly on our <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/p/corrections-log.html">Corrections Log</a>, with the article, what changed, and when.</p>
<h2>What this page does not promise</h2>
<p>This methodology describes our process, not a guarantee that any single article is error free. If you find an error, tell us through the Contact page with the article, the statement in dispute, and a reliable source that supports the concern. We will check it, correct it visibly where it is wrong, and log the correction.</p>
