<p>Four flash-tier models now advertise nearly identical prices, and the cheapest one changes with the workload. On ByteHerald's standard batch of 10 million input tokens and 2 million output tokens, GLM-5.3-Flash wins uncached at $2.50, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash wins when 80 percent of input hits the cache at $1.52, and GLM wins again in DeepSeek's peak hours, when DeepSeek's bill doubles to $5.40. The direct answer is that there is no cheapest flash model, only a cheapest model for a given cache share and hour of day, and any ranking that ignores those two variables is wrong for half the workloads it claims to cover.</p>

<p>The uncached result is the closest race in the set: GLM at $2.50, Qwen at $2.54, and DeepSeek and MiniMax tied at $2.70. Four cents separate first from second, which is smaller than any reasonable measurement error in token counting, so a buyer should treat GLM and Qwen as tied here and decide on other grounds, such as license terms or benchmark fit. The cached result flips the order on two cents: DeepSeek at $1.52 against GLM at $1.54, because DeepSeek's $0.003 cache rate overwhelms GLM's cheaper output price once 8 million tokens hit the cache. MiniMax lands third at $1.62, and Qwen cannot be priced at all, which is itself a finding: an unpublished cache price is a missing price, not a zero price, and any comparison that fills it in silently is inventing data.</p>
<p>The peak result is the one that should change buying behavior. DeepSeek's bill doubles to $5.40 in its weekday peak windows, 2.16 times GLM's flat $2.50, while the other three models do not move. An agent fleet that runs on a fixed schedule through 01:00 to 04:00 or 06:00 to 10:00 UTC weekdays pays the peak rate whether it knows the windows exist or not. The fix is operational, not technical: route peak-window traffic to a flat-priced model, or shift batch work outside the windows, and the $2.90 per-batch difference pays for the routing logic many times over. This is also where the expiring-discount story from earlier in October returns, because list prices in this tier move on calendar dates, and a price that wins today can be repriced next quarter without any model change.</p>

<h2>What list prices leave out</h2>
<p>Three gaps keep this ranking honest. First, token counts differ by model for the same task, because reasoning tokens bill as output and models vary in verbosity: Artificial Analysis task-cost figures cited in the comparisons point the same way, with MiniMax near $0.22, GLM near $0.28, and DeepSeek near $0.40 per task at max effort in the snapshots cited, but snapshots and effort settings differ, so those figures support the scenario math rather than replacing it. Second, benchmarks do not settle the choice: Qwen leads the three on SWE-bench Pro at 62.5 against DeepSeek at 56.0 in the comparison cited, with GLM unpublished on that benchmark, while MiniMax reports 73.7 percent on DeepSWE v1.1 as a vendor figure, and those are different tests, not one leaderboard. Third, licenses gate self-hosting: GLM carries an MIT license in the comparison cited, MiniMax ships under a Community License with commercial conditions including an attribution line, a one-time notice, and prior authorization above $20 million in annual revenue, and DeepSeek's and Qwen's licenses are reported as not officially published or not officially specified, so a team planning to serve weights itself must read the actual license file before committing hardware.</p>
<p>MiniMax deserves a separate note on size, because its name misleads. The model totals about 428 billion parameters with 23 billion active per token, ships as roughly 252 gigabytes across 48 shards, and is a multi-GPU serving job even though its API price sits in the flash tier. Flash describes inference economics, not small size, and the same warning applies across this tier: a cheap API price does not mean a cheap self-hosted deployment. DeepSeek totals are undisclosed with 13 billion active, GLM totals 320 billion with active undisclosed, and Qwen totals 125 billion with 6 billion active, so active-parameter comparisons across vendors rest on different disclosure levels and should not be quoted as if every vendor counted the same way.</p>
<h2>How to buy from this table</h2>
<p>Measure your own cache share first, because it decides between DeepSeek and GLM before any other variable matters. If more than half your input repeats across turns, price the cached scenario with your own prefix lengths, not with the 80 percent example here. Then check the clock: if your fleet runs through DeepSeek's weekday peak windows, either route that traffic elsewhere or budget the doubled rate, and do not annualize an off-peak price. Then check the license file if you plan to self-host, and run your own tasks, because benchmark scores in this tier come from different tests and the cheapest model on a benchmark you do not run is not the cheapest model for your work. Finally, calendar the re-check: list prices here are October 2026 figures, and this tier reprices often enough that a quarterly re-run of this exact table is part of the buying process, not an optional extra.</p>
