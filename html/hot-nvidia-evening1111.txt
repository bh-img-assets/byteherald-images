<p><a href="https://www.wsj.com/business/nvidia-is-preparing-to-oust-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-30f421ad">The Wall Street Journal reported</a> on November 11, 2026, that Nvidia's board is preparing to oust chief executive Jensen Huang, and plans to meet as soon as Wednesday to decide his future. The report lands three days after the board opened an investigation into Huang and said it had confidence in him.</p>

<h2>What the Journal reported</h2>
<p>The board is preparing to remove Huang after two extramarital affairs, <a href="https://www.wsj.com/business/nvidia-is-preparing-to-oust-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-30f421ad">the Journal reported</a>, citing people familiar with the matter. One involved Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore data company Megaspeed who is suspected of spying for China. The second, reported by the New York Times on November 11, involved Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reports to Huang. On Sunday, November 8, Huang acknowledged in a letter to staff that he had been unfaithful to his wife and apologized for violating his family's and his employees' trust. The Justice Department is now leading multiple investigations involving Alice Huang, according to the Journal. Nvidia shares fell about 8 percent in after-hours trading on November 11, extending a slide that began when the board probe was announced.</p>

November 10, 2026, 13 to 14 percent. November 11, 2026, 77 percent." /></div>
<h2>What changed since Sunday</h2>
<p>On Sunday, the board said it would investigate reports that Huang was the target of a foreign espionage effort, while adding that it had "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised" and that it had "confidence in Jensen and his leadership team." Three days later, the same board is preparing to meet to decide whether he stays. The market noticed the shift: <a href="https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-11-11-2026/card/nvidia-stock-falls-afterhours-following-report-on-possible-jensen-huang-exit-qpZL3zuTAbNYQZfpIyaU">bettors on Polymarket put the odds of Huang leaving by resignation or removal by year end at 77 percent on November 11</a>, up from 13 to 14 percent on November 10. <a href="https://www.wsj.com/livecoverage/stock-market-today-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-11-11-2026/card/huang-might-need-to-step-down-as-nvidia-s-top-exec-baird-says-Ipn9cQc0rwQqyzTmu1bb">Baird analysts wrote that Huang might need to step down</a>, that the odds of a chief executive transition are increasing heading into 2027, and that an outside hire is more likely than an internal promotion. Baird also downgraded the stock. The stock had already fallen 4.3 percent on November 9, the same day the Nasdaq had its best day since July 30, <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/nvidia-fell-43-on-markets-best-day.html">as we reported then</a>. The board probe itself began November 8, <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/nvidia-board-probes-ceo-huang-after.html">when we first reported the investigation and the board's statement</a>.</p>

<h2>The governance gap the scandal exposed</h2>
<p>Nvidia has no chairman, a trait shared by only about 1 percent of publicly traded companies in the Russell 3000, according to Equilar, <a href="https://www.wsj.com/business/the-spy-scandal-that-lays-bare-nvidias-governance-vacuum-44f8c786">the Journal reported</a>. Huang and the board's lead director, former Cooley chief executive Stephen Neal, together draw up the agenda for board meetings and decide what information directors receive. Three of the board's 10 independent directors have held their seats for 25 years or more, and two others have served more than a decade. Only two directors have joined in the past five years. Huang holds 3.6 percent of Nvidia's shares and runs the company with about 60 direct reports, saying he does not believe in succession planning. Nvidia's latest proxy statement warns the company is "highly dependent on the services of our longstanding executive team" and that a failure to ensure effective succession planning could "hinder our strategic planning, execution, and long-term success." Proxy advisory firm Institutional Shareholder Services assigned Nvidia its riskiest rating for board structure last spring, citing the long-tenured directors and the lack of a chairman or a mechanism to refresh the board.</p>
<h2>What happens next</h2>
<p>The board meets as soon as Wednesday, November 11, to determine Huang's future. The chairmen of three House committees wrote to Huang on November 4, raising national-security concerns about China's access to sensitive United States technology, and requested a closed-door briefing on November 18. The committees asked Huang to respond by November 11. President Trump said he talked to Huang, who told him he had no idea the woman was a spy. Nvidia reports earnings on November 17, which will be the first time Huang faces investors since the board probe began.</p>
