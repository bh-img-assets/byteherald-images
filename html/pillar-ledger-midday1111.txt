<p>Ten companies trying to break Nvidia's grip on AI chips have raised about 15.1 billion dollars in private capital between them, according to ByteHerald's compilation of company announcements and press reporting as of November 11, 2026. One of them is now a public company. One is bankrupt. Four have put hardware into customers' hands. The rest are selling a promise, and the market is pricing the promise as if the hardware already shipped.</p>

<p>The total, about 15.1 billion dollars, is ByteHerald's sum of the table's private-capital figures. It excludes Cerebras's 5.55 billion dollar IPO raise in May 2026 in the base deal, 6.38 billion dollars including the underwriters' over-allotment, which is public capital, and it counts Tenstorrent only at the two large rounds disclosed since December 2024, so the true total is higher. The point of the sum is scale: ten challengers have raised roughly a third of what one hyperscaler spends on data centers in a single quarter, and they have raised it against a company that dominates training with more than 90 percent market share.</p>
<h2>The valuation staircase</h2>
<p>The valuations are rising faster than the money. <a href="https://www.vlsi.kr/en/etched-ai-chip-deep-dive-en/">Etched went from 5 billion dollars in December 2025 to 10.3 billion in July 2026 to a reported 21 billion in August</a>, roughly quadrupling in eight months. <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/with-720m-round-britains-fractile-gets-a-leg-up-in-the-race-for-ai-chips/">Fractile went from about 1.1 billion in May 2026 to about 7 billion in November</a>, more than six times in six months. <a href="https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/21481/release/20260910NE44310?filter=21481">Positron went from over 1 billion in February 2026 to 5 billion in September</a>, more than quadrupling in seven months. The chart below divides each company's latest valuation by the private capital it raised, which is the cleanest way to see how much belief each dollar is carrying.</p>
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bh-img-assets/byteherald-images/main/img/pillar-ledger-midday1111-chart.png" alt="Chart: valuation per dollar raised. Etched 11.1 times, Fractile 6.4 times, SambaNova 4.4 times, d-Matrix 4.4 times, Positron 4.2 times, Tenstorrent 2.8 times." /></div>
<p>Etched turns each dollar raised into 11.1 dollars of valuation, the highest in the field, and it is also the company with the thinnest public proof: one rack delivered to one customer, Jane Street, which also led its latest round, and performance claims of 500,000 tokens per second on Llama 70B that no independent lab has verified. Fractile is next at 6.4 times, with no shipped silicon at all. The two numbers say the same thing: in this market, investors are paying for the next chip, not the current one, and the price assumes the next chip works.</p>
<h2>The proof ladder</h2>
<p>Money is the easy part to count. Proof is harder, and the ledger splits cleanly when you ask what each company has actually put into a customer's hands. Four have shipped hardware: Cerebras, which lists on Nasdaq and publishes audited revenue of 510 million dollars in 2025 with a <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2021728/000162828026056357/cbrs-20260630.htm">25.4 billion dollar order backlog as of June 30, 2026</a>, anchored by a 20 billion dollar OpenAI deal; Tenstorrent, which <a href="https://manufacturing.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/hi-tech/ai-chip-startup-tenstorrent-more-than-doubles-its-valuation-to-6-2-billion-in-funding-round/135050604">closed a 1.5 billion dollar Series E in October 2026 at a 6.2 billion dollar valuation</a> and whose Galaxy servers began shipping in April 2026; Positron, with more than 50 Atlas racks deployed at Oracle Cloud Infrastructure; and Etched, with exactly one rack delivered, to Jane Street. Two are pre-silicon: Fractile, which promises first chips in 2027, and <a href="https://tech.eu/2026/11/11/tendrils-compute-raises-30m-to-redo-the-cpu/">Tendrils, founded in 2026, which has no silicon and no benchmarks</a>. One, Groq, no longer sells chips at all in the old sense: Nvidia licensed its technology for about 20 billion dollars in December 2025 and hired its founder and senior team, while the remaining company sells inference as a cloud operator <a href="https://www.smartbrainsai.com/groq-raises-350-million-at-half-its-old-valuation-as-nvidia-deal-reshapes-ai-inference-race/">and raised 1 billion dollars in 2026 to fund the pivot</a>. One, Untether AI, is bankrupt. The remaining two, <a href="https://aimagazine.com/news/sambanova-systems-fast-ai-inference-chips-at-us-11bn-value">SambaNova</a> and <a href="https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/d-matrix-closes-275m-series-c-funding-bringing-total-raised-to-450m/">d-Matrix</a>, sell enterprise and hyperscale systems, but neither publishes a shipment count, so they sit between the shipped group and the pre-silicon group.</p>

Navy means hardware shipping, gold means pre-silicon, grey means licensed away or bankrupt." /></div>
<p>The gap between the two charts is the story of this ledger. The companies with the most money are not the companies with the most proof, and the company with the highest valuation per dollar raised has the least public evidence of any of them. Only Cerebras, the public one, lets an outsider check its revenue, its backlog, and its customer concentration, and even Cerebras once depended on a single customer, G42, for 87 percent of its revenue in the first half of 2024. The buyers for all of this hardware are the hyperscalers, whose spending we tallied in <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/five-companies-828-billion-2026-ai.html">our 828 billion dollar capex ledger</a>, and the distribution those buyers control is what we mapped in <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/who-controls-open-ai-stack-hugging-face.html">our pillar on who controls the open AI stack</a>. A challenger has to beat Nvidia on the chip and on the channel, and the channel is the harder half.</p>
<h2>The graveyard</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.cbinsights.com/company/untether-ai">Untether AI raised 165 million dollars</a>, including an oversubscribed 125 million dollar round in July 2021 co-led by Intel Capital, and filed for bankruptcy in October 2025, with AMD hiring its engineering team that June. Graphcore, the last British challenger before Fractile, raised about 700 million dollars, peaked near a 3 billion dollar valuation in 2020, and was sold to SoftBank in July 2024 for a price it never disclosed. Both companies had real chips and real engineers. Both ran out of the one thing no valuation provides: a customer willing to pay, at volume, before the money ran out. Every challenger in the ledger depends on the same scarce inputs, and the scarcest is memory: the high-bandwidth memory every one of these chips needs is the bottleneck we tracked in <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/the-memory-wall-how-hbm-stacks-decide.html">our Memory Wall pillar</a>. A funding round buys time. It does not buy tape-out, yield, or a second customer.</p>
<h2>How to read the next raise</h2>
<p>Three questions cut through any future announcement in this field. First, has the chip shipped to a paying customer who is not also an investor? Second, has an independent lab measured it, rather than the company? Third, is there a second customer, so the first is not the whole business? A raise that answers all three is a company. A raise that answers none of them is a price, and valuations in this ledger run 2.8 to 11.1 times the capital each company raised, with the thinnest evidence behind the highest multiple. The next update to this ledger comes when a row changes materially: a shipment, a benchmark, a bankruptcy, or a buyout. Until then, the numbers above are the record as of November 11, 2026.</p>
