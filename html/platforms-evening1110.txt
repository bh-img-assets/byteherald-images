<p>In 22 days, seven enterprise AI moves landed. Cognizant built three frontier delivery groups, one for each of the big model labs. IBM and Microsoft launched a joint agent platform. Commvault, Denodo, and GoodData each announced a data layer for agents, all on the same day. The pattern is the story: the enterprise agent business is no longer about the model. It is about who builds the agents, who runs them in production, and who governs the data they touch. This is the scoreboard of that wave, with every launch in one table, so a buyer can see what each seller is actually offering.</p>

<p>Four of the seven landed on a single day, November 10. That is not a coincidence. It is a land grab: every vendor with a claim on enterprise AI wants its platform named before budgets are set for 2027. The table is the point. No single announcement tells a buyer what the field looks like, and no outlet had put all seven together. The rest of this piece works through the table row by row, then asks the three questions that separate a platform from a press release.</p>

<h2>Cognizant's 68,000-person bet</h2>
<p>The largest number in the wave belongs to one company. Cognizant has now built a frontier delivery group for each of the three big model labs, and the headcount adds up: more than 18,000 associates on Gemini, more than 25,000 professionals on OpenAI, and more than 25,000 professionals on Claude, a total of more than 68,000 people, by ByteHerald's arithmetic from the companies' own announcements. The groups arrived in order. The <a href="https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-19-Cognizant-and-Google-Cloud-Expand-Alliance-to-Accelerate-Agentic-Enterprise-Transformation">Gemini Frontier Capability Group</a> came first, announced October 19, 2026, with Google Cloud, backed by more than 18,000 dedicated associates and focused on legacy modernization, developer experience, autonomous business-process work, and frontier cyber defense. More than 55,000 Cognizant associates have completed the Build with Gemini workshop, and the company is deploying Gemini to 100,000 associates in 2026, with plans to scale to 200,000. The <a href="https://english.kyodonews.net/articles/-/90077">OpenAI Frontier Capability Group</a> followed on October 30, 2026, with more than 25,000 professionals aligned to delivering Codex and ChatGPT, focused on AI-native software engineering with Codex, application modernization, and agentic customer operations. More than 21,000 associates have earned over 40,000 OpenAI badges. The <a href="https://english.kyodonews.net/articles/-/91027">Claude Frontier Capability Group</a> arrived November 10, 2026, with more than 25,000 professionals and a managed services model, under which Cognizant runs Claude-powered systems in production on clients' behalf. The group holds more than 26,000 Claude certifications and ranks first globally for Claude Certified Architect: Foundations.</p>
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bh-img-assets/byteherald-images/main/img/pillar-platforms-evening1110-chart2.png" alt="Chart: professionals aligned to each Cognizant frontier group. Claude group 25,000, OpenAI group 25,000, Gemini group 18,000." /></div>
<p>Two caveats belong with the number. First, the 25,000 figures describe professionals aligned to each practice, not 25,000 new hires. Cognizant has disclosed no revenue target, client count, or investment figure for any of the three groups. Second, the Claude group is the only one of the three that takes responsibility for running the systems it builds. The managed services model gives clients one accountable partner for Claude in production, with more predictable AI spend, and it is the sharpest statement in the wave about where the money moves next: from building agents to operating them. The one client result Cognizant has published is a pharmaceutical manufacturer, where Claude-powered shop-floor agents cut downtime by 35 percent and reduced troubleshooting time from 90 to 120 minutes to 10 to 15 minutes. That is a company-reported result, not an independent measurement, and it is the only production number attached to any of the three groups so far.</p>
<h2>IBM and Microsoft: the platform</h2>
<p><a href="https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-11-10-ibm-and-microsoft-announce-a-new-ai-agent-platform-designed-for-industry-specific-transformation">Imagine Enterprise</a>, announced November 10, 2026, by IBM and Microsoft, is the only joint platform in the wave, and the only one that names both a builder and a cloud. IBM Consulting brings more than 6,000 pre-built AI agents and assets, built on its IBM Consulting Advantage delivery platform, with industry expertise from building and running them. Microsoft provides the AI, data, cloud, and security foundation: Microsoft Foundry, GitHub Copilot, and Copilot Studio to coordinate agents, and Fabric, Microsoft 365 infrastructure, Entra, Purview, Defender, and Azure for data, identity, governance, security, and cloud. Forward-deployed engineers from both companies support qualifying engagements, to move customers from experimentation to production. The industry frameworks start with financial services, insurance, and retail, with manufacturing, energy, public sector, and mobility named as possible additions. The launch customer example is SEI, a financial technology and asset management firm that manages, advises, or administers about 2 trillion dollars in assets as of September 30, 2026, and is exploring agentic technologies with IBM Consulting and Microsoft. Two things are missing, and both matter. No pricing was disclosed. And the 6,000 figure counts agents and other assets together, not 6,000 deployable agents alone. A buyer who reads it as 6,000 ready agents is reading more than the announcement says.</p>
<h2>The data layer: three deals in one day</h2>
<p>The other three November 10 moves share a thesis: agents are only as good as the data they can reach, and enterprise data is still locked in backups, warehouses, and silos. Commvault and Databricks announced <a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/commvault-and-databricks-unlock-trusted-enterprise-data-for-ai-302934181.html">Data Activate for Databricks</a>, which shares enterprise data managed by Commvault into Databricks through OpenSharing as governed tables, managed with Unity Catalog, so analytics, AI applications, and agents can use it without rebuilding connections to every source. Early access is planned for December 2026 in select Azure regions. The same day, Commvault also announced a collaboration with NVIDIA to validate corporate data as current, authorized, and compliant before it feeds training models and real-time inference, and a partnership with Zscaler for shadow AI discovery, runtime governance, and agent detection and recovery, plus Recovery Studio and an AgentRecover update that extends agent-damage reversal to Microsoft Foundry and Amazon Bedrock AgentCore. <a href="https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/10/3399313/0/en/denodo-signs-strategic-collaboration-agreement-with-aws-to-accelerate-trusted-data-access-for-agentic-ai.html">Denodo</a> signed a multi-year strategic collaboration agreement with AWS to give agents live, governed data through a unified AI data layer, with zero-copy delivery that extends Bedrock AgentCore, Amazon Q, and SageMaker to enterprise data without moving or replicating it. GoodData.AI launched <a href="https://www.newswire.com/news/gooddata-ai-launches-the-agentic-serving-plane-a-new-category-of-22874248">the Agentic Serving Plane</a>, a governed execution layer that sits between enterprise data platforms and the dashboards, applications, assistants, and agents that consume them, built on Apache Iceberg and Apache Arrow, with local inference planned to roll out in the coming months so selected AI capabilities can run closer to governed data without a third-party model provider in the path. GoodData was named a Visionary in Gartner's 2026 Magic Quadrant for Analytics and Business Intelligence Platforms.</p>
<h2>Three questions before you buy</h2>
<p>Seven announcements in 22 days is a lot of paper, and most of it is not yet a product a buyer can test. Three questions separate the platforms from the press releases. First, is it a platform or a staffing announcement? Cognizant's three groups are staffing and services: people aligned to a lab's technology, with a managed services model only on the Claude side. Imagine Enterprise is a platform with a consultancy attached. The three data-layer moves are products with dates. Second, does it name a customer and a result? Only Cognizant does, with one pharmaceutical example, and only IBM and Microsoft name a customer at all, with SEI still exploring rather than deployed. Third, can you buy it, or only engage a consultancy? The data-layer products have dates and, in Commvault's case, an early access window. Imagine Enterprise is available by engaging IBM Consulting. The Cognizant groups are engagements, not products. A buyer who asks those three questions will find that the wave is real but uneven: the data layer is shippable, the platforms are engageable, and the headcount is a promise that the work will be staffed.</p>
<h2>What to watch</h2>
<p>The wave's test is production, and the numbers to watch are the ones no announcement contained. No vendor in the table disclosed pricing, a client count, or revenue from these moves. The next evidence will be client names with measured results, the way Cognizant's pharmaceutical example points, and early access reports from Commvault's December window. The governance question runs underneath all of it. Every platform in the table promises governed agents, governed data, or both, and the governance is the product as much as the agents are. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/ai-agents-need-id-agent-identity-stack.html">We compared the agent identity stack in October and found five approaches to the same unsolved problem: proving which agent did what</a>. None of the seven launches answers it. The platform wave is the supply side of a demand story we measured earlier: <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-177-billion-dollar-enterprise-ai.html">enterprise buyers deployed 176.8 billion dollars on AI in our November ledger, with agents taking 80 percent of OpenRouter tokens</a>. The sellers have now answered with platforms, people, and data layers. Whether the buyers get agents that work, or just more announcements, is a question for the first quarter of 2027, when the early access windows close and the forward-deployed engineers either deliver or do not.</p>
