<p>Anthropic has agreed to pay about $3.4 billion for commitments covering 1.7 gigawatts of gas-fired generators, the Financial Times reported on November 8, 2026. The Claude maker did not buy a power company, and it did not order new machines. It took over generator orders a Texas supplier placed with Caterpillar about two years ago, because new orders now face waits of years. The deal is the largest known single purchase of power-generation equipment by an AI lab, the FT said.</p>

<h2>What Anthropic actually bought</h2>
<p>The deal, struck in late September 2026, covers commitments for 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty gas-fired generators from OnePWR Solutions, a San Antonio company that builds microgrids and on-site power plants. Anthropic first planned to become a OnePWR customer, then chose to take over the equipment orders instead, together with the engineering and construction capability needed to install the machines. It did not buy OnePWR itself, and none of OnePWR's employees moved across to Anthropic. The generators are not fresh orders. OnePWR placed them with Caterpillar about two years ago and needed financing to complete the purchase, so Anthropic bought the orders and the financing problem together. A separate disclosure by Piper Sandler, which served as OnePWR's exclusive financial adviser, lists a $3.4 billion sale of OnePWR's power-generation platform to an unnamed AI lab in September 2026. The bank does not name Anthropic as the buyer, but the amount and the month match the FT's report. Reuters carried the FT report and noted it could not immediately verify it, and that Anthropic and OnePWR did not respond to requests for comment.</p>

<h2>Why power is the bottleneck now</h2>
<p>The deal makes sense only against Anthropic's own paperwork. In its amended IPO filing, the S-1/A of November 2, 2026, Anthropic disclosed about $895 billion in commitments, mostly for compute and infrastructure, and said its planned data centers will rely on dedicated on-site natural-gas generation. The same filing warns that turbines, generators, substations, and transmission parts have faced extended delivery timelines, price increases, tariffs, and rationing as data center operators compete for them. That warning is the deal. A lab that needs 1.7 gigawatts cannot wait for Caterpillar to build new machines, because every other lab is in the same queue. Buying orders placed two years ago is buying time, and time is the one input Anthropic cannot manufacture. The $3.4 billion is about 0.38 percent of the $895 billion in commitments, which puts the deal in proportion: it is not the buildout. It is the down payment on being able to build at all. For scale, 1.7 gigawatts is roughly the output of two large nuclear units, and it is power Anthropic will burn whether or not any of it ever reaches a grid connection.</p>
<h2>What we do not know</h2>
<p>Four things about this deal are still undisclosed, and they matter more than the headline number. The FT report does not say where the generators will be installed, which data centers they will serve, what the delivery schedule is, or how the $3.4 billion splits between equipment and installation. Anthropic, OnePWR, and Caterpillar have not commented publicly, and Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report. The $3.4 billion figure and the 1.7 gigawatt capacity are therefore reported, not company-confirmed, and any article that states them as fact without that qualifier is overstating the evidence. The Piper Sandler disclosure corroborates the amount and the month, but it does not name the buyer, so the link between the $3.4 billion sale and Anthropic rests on the FT's sourcing. That is normal for a deal of this kind, and it is also a reason to read the next filing rather than the headline.</p>
<h2>The pattern: power is the new chip</h2>
<p>The generator deal is the third time in six weeks that power, not silicon, has decided an AI story. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/ais-power-and-water-bill-datacenter.html">Our datacenter resource ledger on October 21 mapped the megawatts and water behind every announced campus</a>, and the numbers then were already straining grids. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-32-trillion-dollar-ai-debt-nobody.html">Our AI debt ledger on November 3 added up the off-balance-sheet obligations the labs have signed</a>, about $3.2 trillion across five companies, most of it purchase commitments and leases for exactly this kind of equipment. The $3.4 billion generator deal sits inside both ledgers: it is a power purchase and a signed future obligation at once. The race has moved one layer down the stack. Two years ago the bottleneck was GPUs. Last year it was the data centers to hold them. Now it is the generators to power the data centers, and the companies that ordered early are selling their place in line to the companies that did not. Anthropic just paid $3.4 billion to be early, two years late.</p>
