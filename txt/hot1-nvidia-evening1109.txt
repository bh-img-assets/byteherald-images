<p>Nvidia fell 4.3 percent on Monday, November 9, 2026, closing at 178.67 dollars, on the same day the Nasdaq rose 2.7 percent and the S&P 500 recorded its best day since April. The trigger was the board investigation into Chief Executive Jensen Huang that Nvidia announced on November 8. The market's verdict was narrower than the headlines: the probe hit Nvidia, and it did not hit the AI trade.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/nvidia-board-probes-ceo-huang-after.html">We reported the board's announcement on November 8</a>: an independent investigation into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, overseen by independent directors with outside counsel, with the board saying it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. What changed on Monday is the price, and the price tells a different story from the probe itself. Nvidia lost about 190 billion dollars of market value in one session while the chip index rose 6.9 percent.</p>

<p>The divergence is the story. One stock fell while its sector, its index, and its biggest customers all rose. Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet are three of Nvidia's largest chip buyers, and all three gained on the day. The selling was about Nvidia's chief executive, not about demand for AI hardware.</p>

<h2>What the board actually said</h2>
<p>The board's statement, published November 8, says it is initiating an independent investigation "in light of recent reporting" that Huang "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort," and that a committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee it. The board said it prioritizes United States national and economic security, and added: "While the Board has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, the Board's inquiry will examine all available information." That sentence is a statement of the board's position as the review begins, not a finding from a completed investigation. The board also expressed confidence in Huang and his leadership team, and Huang remains chief executive.</p>
<p>Huang's own message to employees, published the same day, said: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did." He apologized to his family and colleagues, said he supports the investigation, and pledged to cooperate fully. The letter does not name the woman involved. The underlying reporting, from The New York Times as carried by other outlets, is that United States intelligence agencies concluded Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer, was gathering information for Beijing. Those allegations have not been independently verified, no outlet has accused Jensen Huang of wrongdoing, and he has not been charged with any offence. Megaspeed denies any illegal transfer of AI chips to China, and China's foreign ministry rejected the alleged spy plot on November 9. Three House committee chairs have asked Huang to attend a joint closed-door briefing on November 18, and Huang has until November 11 to accept or offer an alternative date. Nvidia had made no filing on the matter with the Securities and Exchange Commission as of November 9.</p>
<p>The legal aftermath began within hours of the close. A proposed class action filed in federal court in San Jose on November 9 accuses Nvidia and Huang of defrauding shareholders by concealing Huang's ties to Alice Huang, covering anyone who bought Nvidia shares between October 9, 2025, and November 8, 2026. The complaint is an allegation, not a finding, and it will be tested in court. It is also the fastest way the probe turns into a number: if the case survives, discovery will put the board's internal record in front of a judge.</p>
<h2>The drawdown in context</h2>
<p>Monday's fall extends a slide that began well before the board statement. Nvidia closed 25 percent below its record close of 239.24 dollars on October 6, and about 17 percent below its close of 215.38 dollars on October 28. Over five sessions the stock is down 12.69 percent, over one month down 22.07 percent, and for the year to date down 4.2 percent. The selling pushed Nvidia's 14-day relative strength index to 24.9 at the open, its lowest reading since 2019, before the shares moved off their lows. RBC Capital Markets maintained its Outperform rating and a 330 dollar price target on Monday. The next scheduled chance for management to address investors is November 17, when Nvidia reports third-quarter results, with analysts expecting revenue of about 111.36 billion dollars and earnings of about 2.53 dollars per share.</p>
<h2>Wall Street's counterpoint</h2>
<p>The banks that cover the sector read Monday as a rotation, not a verdict on AI spending. UBS said in a client note on November 9 that "the recent tech selloff reflects a position reset, not the start of a structural slump in the AI investment cycle," and that it expects broad equity markets to rise further over the next 6 to 12 months, driven by economic activity and improving earnings. Its advice was to use the rebound to trim positions that have grown excessively large in any single stock. Morgan Stanley called the selloff overdone and said the likely outcome is that some data center projects get pushed from 2028 to 2029 rather than canceled, which would slow the pace but extend the spending cycle. Jefferies called the November 5 selloff "reactionary," and HSBC said it is using the correction to increase its exposure to equities again. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-132-trillion-ai-selloff-claim-does.html">We audited the last selloff claim in this market and found the same pattern: a 13.2 trillion dollar figure that did not add up against a verified loss of about 5.3 trillion dollars</a>. The banks' case is that the buildout continues and the prices got ahead of it, not that the buildout stopped.</p>
<h2>The decoupling, or the probe?</h2>
<p>There are two readings of Monday, and they point in opposite directions. The first is that the market separated the man from the machine: the probe is specific to Nvidia's chief executive, the demand for its chips is unchanged, and the stock's fall is a company event inside a healthy sector. The second is that the probe is the excuse and the rotation is the cause: the most crowded trade in the market was due a reset, UBS's note says so in so many words, and a board investigation gave sellers the reason to start it. Both readings agree on the test. On November 17, Nvidia reports data center revenue, and the number either confirms that demand is intact or it does not. The probe's own test is slower: an independent investigation with no published timetable, a House briefing on November 18, and a class action that will ask what the board knew and when. If earnings hold and the probe clears, the decoupling reverses. If either disappoints, the gap between Nvidia and the trade it leads gets wider, and the stock stops being the proxy for AI and becomes a company again.</p>
