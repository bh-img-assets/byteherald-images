<p>Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on November 9, 2026, its most capable models yet, and the release carries a contradiction the company does not hide. The two models are the same underlying system, split by safeguards: Fable is available to all users with cybersecurity and biology safeguards switched on, while Mythos is the same model without those safeguards, available only to vetted organizations. They generate outputs up to 30 percent faster than Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, and they cost 20 percent less. They are also the first frontier models Anthropic has released since its chief executive, Dario Amodei, called in September for the industry to slow down.</p>

<p>Claude Fable 5.2, released November 9, 2026, is Anthropic's most capable generally available model, priced at 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 dollars per million output tokens, 20 percent below Fable 5.1, with an optional fast mode at twice the price that generates tokens up to 2.5 times faster. Claude Mythos 5.2 is the same model without the cybersecurity and biology safeguards, available only to vetted organizations through Anthropic's Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs.</p>

<p>Two rows in that table cut against the launch narrative, and they are worth stating plainly. On AutomationBench, the business-workflow test, Gemini 4 Carbon scores 53.9 percent against Fable 5.2 at 51.4 percent, so the most capable Anthropic model is not the best at business workflows. And on the Elo rating Anthropic reports alongside the table, Opus 5.5 still leads at 1800 against Fable 5.2 at 1785. These are Anthropic's own numbers, measured on its own harness. The numbers show a step up, not a sweep.</p>

<h2>What the model actually did before launch</h2>
<p>The announcement's most striking claims are not the benchmark scores but the work Anthropic says the model did during prerelease testing. Over 90 percent of the changes merged into Anthropic's core codebase are now written by Claude, with human guidance, and a team of agents running an earlier version of Fable 5.2 migrated a critical production service from Python to Rust, a service that now starts about 26 times faster. As an experiment, one engineer gave Fable 5.2 the task of designing a prototype operating system from an empty folder. The model coordinated over 1,000 subagents, working around the clock for eight days, and produced a working operating system that boots on a laptop, with its own core, drivers, file storage, windows, a login screen, an installer, and built-in Claude functionality.</p>
<p>The science claims are similarly concrete, and they are the ones that will be checked first. Fable 5.2 solved two open problems in the physics of black holes, both concerning holographic duality, with the results written up in preprints. It formalized the proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours, using 1,000 agents with minimal human intervention, against 11 days for Anthropic's first formalization, and with one-third as many action tokens and a proof seven times shorter. Opus 5.5, given the same 1,000-agent setup, completed only 26 percent of the formalization in 18 hours and barely progressed after 30. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/ai-proves-70-of-theorems-but-solves.html">Our theorem-proving scoreboard from this morning puts that result in context: GPT-6 Astra proved 70 percent of the theorems on the TCSExam benchmark and solved none of the open conjectures, which is the gap formalization still has to close</a>. In protein work, Mythos 5.2 found a way to predict a whole viral capsid's structure, accurately modeling 28 of the 34 capsids it attempted. In hardware, Fable 5.2 designed three working electronic devices from scratch with the company Diode, a home voice-assistant hub, a USB speakerphone, and a handheld virtual pet, writing specifications, selecting components, routing four-layer circuit boards, and modeling enclosures, with each board ready for manufacturing in under a day and all three devices working without electrical modifications.</p>
<h2>The price cut, and the fast mode</h2>
<p>The pricing is the part of the launch with no ambiguity. Fable 5.2 costs 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 dollars per million output tokens, down from 10 and 50 dollars for Fable 5.1, a 20 percent cut on both rates. The new fast mode speeds up token generation by up to 2.5 times and costs 16 dollars per million input tokens and 80 dollars per million output tokens, exactly twice the standard 5.2 rate. Anthropic also says Fable 5.2 at Medium effort can accomplish challenging tasks with fewer tokens than Fable 5.1, and that on FrontierCode 2.0 it scored about level with Opus 5.5 at Max effort for roughly half the cost per task. The honest caveat is that the bill depends on how many tokens a task uses, and higher effort settings use more, so the 20 percent list-price cut and the per-task cost are two different numbers.</p>
<h2>One model, two doors</h2>
<p>The Fable and Mythos split is the launch's most deliberate design choice. The two are the same underlying model. Fable carries strong cybersecurity and biology safeguards, which Anthropic says are as robust as Fable 5.1's but produce fewer unnecessary blocks, and that is the version available to all users. Mythos carries no such safeguards, so it can do the cybersecurity and biomedical work Fable blocks, and access runs through the recently expanded Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. Anthropic says it ran extensive prerelease safety testing, including novel evaluations for concerning behaviors like sandbox escapes, that high-stakes risks from misalignment remain low, and that the model performed as well as, or better than, recent models on most alignment measures. It also says it has implemented its strongest-ever protections against distillation attacks, the practice of using a model's outputs to train a competing model. The Mythos gate is the control that makes the rest of the launch possible: the most capable version exists, and most users will never touch it.</p>
<h2>The slowdown call, two months later</h2>
<p>That gate is also where the launch meets the slowdown call. On September 12, Amodei published an essay arguing the industry should slow the pace at which it improves model capabilities, with recursive self-improvement, AI building the next AI, as his number one reason. Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 are the first frontier models Anthropic has released since that essay, and they are more capable, faster, and cheaper than the models they replace. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/ai-is-building-ai-twice-as-fast-we.html">We audited the acceleration data behind that tension last week: an independent METR assessment found Anthropic developing its systems about 1.9 times as fast as a year earlier, with about 80 percent of the gain attributed to AI, and Anthropic's own filing says it may imminently meet its threshold for dramatically accelerated progress</a>. The launch also lands days before a possible IPO roadshow, with Anthropic seeking a valuation of 1 trillion to 1.5 trillion dollars, down from the 2 trillion figure floated in recent months, according to CNBC. Anthropic's answer, in the announcement, is that safety testing kept step: the safeguards, the verification programs, and the system card. Whether that answer satisfies the slowdown argument is the question the next two months will settle, and it will be settled by what the embedded evaluators Anthropic promised in September are allowed to see.</p>
<h2>What Fable is, and when to use it</h2>
<p>A frontier model is the most capable system a lab releases, the one it uses to set the ceiling for what its models can do. Fable and Mythos are Anthropic's frontier line, above Opus 5.5, which the company still recommends as the starting point for most coding and knowledge tasks, calling it its strongest daily driver. Fable 5.2 is for the most ambitious, open-ended technical work: the multi-day migrations, the open problems, the hardware designs. It can also play two supporting roles, as Advisor, where smaller models consult it on harder problems, or as Orchestrator, where it delegates simpler tasks to smaller models. The practical reading is that Anthropic now sells a ladder, not a single model: Sonnet and Haiku for daily work, Opus for the hardest daily work, Fable for the work that would otherwise be too slow or too costly to attempt.</p>
<h2>What to watch</h2>
<p>First, independent verification: the benchmark scores, the 30 percent speed gain, and the science results are Anthropic's own, and the first independent runs will either confirm them or narrow them. Second, whether the fast mode's 2.5 times speedup holds on real workloads, and what it does to the per-task bill. Third, whether the Mythos verification programs expand, since the most capable version of the model is also the least accessible. And fourth, whether the embedded evaluators promised in September publish anything about this release, because that was the slowdown essay's only unilateral commitment, and this is the first frontier model it has to cover.</p>
