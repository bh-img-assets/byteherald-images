<p>Anthropic announced Claude Meetings on Tuesday, November 10, 2026, at its "The Briefing: Work at the Frontier" event in San Francisco. Claude joins a Google Meet call as a visible participant and starts working on tasks while people are still talking, instead of waiting until everyone hangs up. It launches in beta for Team and Enterprise plans, with Google Meet first and other meeting platforms to follow.</p>
<h2>What Claude Meetings does</h2>
<p>Teams add Claude to a video call, and it follows the conversation and gets to work in real time. As participants talk, it can draft the plan being discussed, map out a timeline on a whiteboard, answer questions in the meeting chat, and pull information from connected work apps, such as last year's figures. After the call, the meeting notes, action items, and other materials Claude created are available in claude.ai.</p>
<p>Claude identifies itself as a meeting participant and posts a notice in the meeting chat. It will not join calls with external guests unless an admin authorizes it, and it automatically leaves if an external guest joins. Those guardrails matter because the feature puts an agent inside live work conversations, not in a separate chat window after the fact.</p>

<h2>Why acting live changes the meeting</h2>
<p>The meeting agent is not a new category. Engadget frames Claude Meetings as Anthropic's take on a meeting agent like the kind offered by Otter.ai, and Google's Gemini offers its own meeting agent in Meet. The difference is what happens during the call. Transcription and summary tools record the meeting and hand the work back afterward. Claude Meetings is built to do the work while the discussion is still happening, so the plan, the timeline, and the answers exist before anyone hangs up.</p>
<p>The timing is part of a wider race. TechCrunch notes the launch comes a few weeks after OpenAI unveiled workplace tools that let users collaborate with ChatGPT, as both companies try to turn their products into office suites that take on Microsoft and Google. Meta is joining the same contest with its Muse agent, which builds mini-apps on the fly. The contest is no longer about which model answers best in a chat window. It is about which assistant sits inside the tools where work already happens.</p>
<p>That shift is also why the guardrails are the part to watch. An agent that acts inside a live meeting can draft the wrong plan or pull the wrong figures while everyone is still talking, and the error ships before anyone reviews it. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/anthropic-cant-control-its-agents-so-it.html">ByteHerald reported in October that Anthropic pulled an agent product after it could not control it</a>, and the same test applies here: whether Claude's meeting guardrails hold when the agent is acting in real time, in front of a whole team, with no draft stage in between.</p>
