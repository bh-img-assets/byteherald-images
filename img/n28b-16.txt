<p>ByteHerald's valuation ladder finds a pattern the headline misses: LangChain has sold roughly a tenth of itself in each of its last two rounds. The November 13, 2026 Series C of $300 million at a $3 billion valuation implies a 10.0 percent stake, matching the Series B arithmetic of $125 million at $1.25 billion. The valuation multiplied 2.4 times in about thirteen months while the fraction sold stayed flat.</p><p>LangChain announced on November 13, 2026 that it raised $300 million at a $3 billion valuation, led by existing investor CapitalG, with new investors GIC and DFJ Growth and existing investors IVP and Sapphire Ventures participating. The announcement brings total funding to $460 million. The money goes to LangSmith, the company's platform for running, evaluating, and governing AI agents, with new capabilities scheduled for a November 20, 2026 release event.</p><p>The ladder below joins the three rounds. The Series A of $25 million in February 2024 was reported at about a $200 million valuation. The Series B of $125 million in October 2025 priced the company at $1.25 billion, 6.25 times the reported Series A figure. The Series C prices it at $3 billion. The Series C alone is 65.2 percent of all capital the company has raised, by our division of $300 million by the $460 million total.</p><table><tr><th>Round</th><th>Date</th><th>Amount</th><th>Valuation</th><th>Implied stake sold</th></tr>

LangChain does not publish revenue in its announcement, so the $3 billion figure cannot be checked against sales from public documents. What the ladder does establish is the pace of repricing: two rounds in thirteen months, each selling about a tenth of the company, at valuations 2.4 times apart.</p><p>The customer evidence in the announcement is specific. LangChain reports more than 7,000 organizations using LangSmith. Workday uses LangChain's tools to build and implement agents embedded in its platform, according to its chief AI officer, on the logic that engineering effort is better spent customizing the model harness with company data than building agent infrastructure. LangChain employs 47 deployed engineers and 13 deployed architects who work directly with customers, a staffing pattern that exists because enterprise buyers need implementation help, not only licenses. The company plans to share new LangSmith capabilities at a release event on November 20, 2026.</p><p>What to watch is whether the November 20 LangSmith release converts the funding into disclosed enterprise contracts. The ladder prices the company; only customer disclosures can price the business.</p><p>The round's place in the wider market is visible in the same month's ledger of verified AI funding rounds, where the $300 million sits among twelve rounds totalling $17.9 billion dominated by a single $15 billion raise. LangChain's round is large for agent tooling and small against that total, which is the correct scale for reading the $3 billion valuation: a category leader price inside a market where model companies absorb most of the capital.</p><p>Methodology: ByteHerald computed implied stakes by dividing each round amount by its post-money valuation, and the Series C share of total funding by dividing $300 million by $460 million. The Series A valuation is reported, not company-confirmed, and is labeled as such in the table.</p><p>What this does not establish: private valuations are negotiated prices, not market marks.
