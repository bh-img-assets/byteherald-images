<p>ByteHerald's evidence ledger finds that the recursive self-improvement loop is closing on execution and still open on judgment. METR's independent assessment of November 5, 2026, estimates capability progress since October 2025 at 1.9 times the earlier rate, close to the roughly two years of progress in one year that Anthropic itself uses as a threshold. But METR also states that Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative, which caps how firmly any outsider can call the slowdown debate settled.</p>

<h2>What the ledger shows</h2>
<p>On the independent side, METR estimates Anthropic is improving model capabilities on well-specified tasks at around twice the March 2024 to October 2025 rate, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5 to 2.8 times, and guesses that a majority, perhaps about 80%, of the current acceleration is due to AI. METR is explicit that uncertainty is high and that 1.3 times or 3 times remain plausible.</p>
<p>On the company side, the execution numbers are strong. Anthropic reports that more than 80% of merged code was authored by Claude as of May 2026, that the typical engineer merged 8 times as much code per day in the second quarter of 2026 as in 2024, and that Claude's success rate on the most open-ended tasks reached 76% in May 2026, up 50 percentage points in six months. In a fixed-goal experiment optimization test, Claude improved from about a 3 times speedup in May 2025 to about 52 times in April 2026. In an open-ended research project, agents recovered 97% of a performance gap over 800 cumulative hours and about $18,000 in compute, against about 23% for two human researchers over about a week.</p>
<p>The same Anthropic page states the counterweight plainly: large performance gaps persist when Claude must exercise judgment in choosing goals, in both engineering and research. Anthropic also cautions that lines of code measure quantity over quality, so the 8 times figure overstates true productivity gain.</p>
<h2>Methodology and limits</h2>
<p>Methodology: we extracted every dated quantitative claim from METR's November 5, 2026 assessment and Anthropic's When AI builds itself page, then tagged each as independent or company reported. Limits: METR's access was high but not complete, and the redaction of most informative evidence means the independent estimate rests on partial data. Company figures come from internal instrumentation that outsiders cannot audit, and several measure activity rather than research quality. The evidence does not establish that full recursive self-improvement, where a system designs its own successor, has begun; Anthropic states it has not.</p>
<h2>Analysis</h2>
<p>Analysis: the ledger supports a narrower claim than either camp usually makes. AI assistance is measurably accelerating well-specified execution, and an independent evaluator puts the acceleration near Anthropic's own two-years-in-one threshold. The slowdown call fails on execution evidence. It survives only as a judgment claim: choosing what to build next remains human-led, and that is precisely the capability a closed loop would require.</p>
