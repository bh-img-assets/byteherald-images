<p>ByteHerald's claim-evidence ledger finds that the newest x86 lead in AI host CPUs rests on two different kinds of evidence. AMD's November 10, 2026 general-availability release for 6th Gen EPYC (Zen 6, Venice) compares published SPECrate2017 integer scores against Intel, but its headline comparisons against Nvidia's Vera and Arm's AGI rest on AMD internal estimates, by AMD's own footnotes. Normalized per core, the published-score gap against Intel narrows from 2.2x per system to about 1.09x per core.</p>

<h2>What the ledger shows</h2>
<p>AMD announced general availability on November 10, 2026, with systems shipping from server OEMs and ODMs and Microsoft Azure and Oracle Cloud instances to follow. Against Intel's Xeon 6980P, AMD claims up to 2.2x performance and 2.0x performance per dollar using published scores: 5,470 against 2,510. Per core, that is 10.7 against 9.8, a gap of about 9 percent, because the EPYC system carries twice the cores. Against Vera and Arm AGI, AMD claims up to 3.7x and 2.8x respectively, but both comparator scores are AMD internal estimates rather than measured systems, and the Vera figure derives from published third-party testing rather than an AMD lab run, per the footnotes.</p>
<h2>What this does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: SPECrate2017 integer throughput measures general integer work, not agent-sandbox latency or tool-execution behavior, and no independent results on production Venice silicon had been published as of November 27, 2026. The ledger therefore supports a narrower claim than the launch headline: x86 now has a shipping contender for AI host work, its published-score lead over Intel is real but mostly a core-count lead, and its lead over Arm-based rivals remains an estimate until independent systems are measured.</p>
<h2>How to use this ledger</h2><p>Read the evidence-class column before the ratio column. A published SPECrate score can be checked against the SPEC results database; an internal estimate cannot, and AMD's footnotes are explicit that the Vera and Arm AGI figures are projections subject to change. Read the per-core column before the system ratio: agent hosts are bought by the rack, so system throughput matters, but per-core figures show how much of a lead is architecture and how much is simply more cores at more watts. The flagship EPYC 9996 carries a 600W default power rating per socket against 500W for the Xeon 6980P, so the 2.2x system lead is bought with twice the cores and 20 percent more power per socket. None of that makes the lead unreal. It makes it a purchasing calculation rather than an architecture verdict, which is what the ledger is for.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
