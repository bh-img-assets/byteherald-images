<p>ByteHerald's concentration ledger puts the two halves of the AI market story on one page. On the spending side, concentration is extreme: JPMorgan Private Bank's 42-stock AI basket accounts for 81 percent of S&amp;P 500 capital expenditure and research and development growth since January 2024, 67 percent of price returns and 58 percent of earnings growth, per Michael Cembalest's October 2026 analysis. On the earnings side, the third quarter of 2026 moved the other way. FactSet's November 20, 2026 Earnings Insight puts actual Magnificent 7 earnings growth at 27.0 percent, below the blended 34.7 percent for the other 493 index companies. The ledger below is our compilation of those dated readings; the spread row is our arithmetic.</p><table><thead><tr><th>Measure</th><th>Value</th><th>Source and date</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>AI basket share of capex and R&amp;D growth since January 2024</td><td>81%</td><td>JPMorgan Private Bank, October 2026</td></tr><tr><td>AI basket share of price returns since January 2024</td><td>67%</td><td>JPMorgan Private Bank, October 2026</td></tr><tr><td>AI basket share of earnings growth since January 2024</td><td>58%</td><td>JPMorgan Private Bank, October 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Magnificent 7 Q3 earnings growth, estimate</td><td>33.6%</td><td>FactSet, September 30, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Magnificent 7 Q3 earnings growth, blended</td><td>22.6%</td><td>FactSet, October 30, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Other 493 Q3 earnings growth, blended</td><td>32.6%</td><td>FactSet, October 30, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Magnificent 7 Q3 earnings growth, actual</td><td>27.0%</td><td>FactSet, November 20, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Other 493 Q3 earnings growth, blended</td><td>34.7%</td><td>FactSet, November 20, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Spread, other 493 minus Magnificent 7 (our arithmetic)</td><td>7.7 points</td><td>ByteHerald, on FactSet November 20, 2026</td></tr></tbody></table><h2>Methodology</h2><p>The basket rows are JPMorgan's published shares for its 42-stock AI basket over the window since January 2024. The earnings rows are FactSet's year-over-year earnings growth rates for Q3 2026 at three checkpoints: the September 30, 2026 estimate, the October 30, 2026 blended reading (actual results for companies that had reported, estimates for the rest), and the November 20, 2026 reading after NVIDIA reported on November 17, 2026. FactSet uses GAAP earnings per share for Alphabet, Amazon and Meta in these calculations. The spread is simple subtraction of the two November 20, 2026 rates. The two datasets measure different things: the basket measures where spending and returns concentrated over almost three years; the FactSet rows measure one quarter of earnings growth. They belong in one ledger because the investment question joins them: whether the companies doing the spending are still the companies producing the earnings growth.</p><h2>What the ledger shows</h2><p>Three findings follow from the rows. First, the spending concentration did not dilute in the quarter when earnings breadth improved. Nothing in the FactSet readings changes the basket's 81 percent share of capex and R&amp;D growth; that share is a cumulative measure, and the buildout continued through Q3 2026. Second, the earnings leadership inverted within one quarter. On September 30, 2026 the Magnificent 7 were expected to grow earnings at 33.6 percent, ahead of the other 493. By October 30, 2026 their blended rate had fallen to 22.6 percent against 32.6 percent for the rest, and the final actual reading of 27.0 percent still trailed the other 493 at 34.7 percent. FactSet notes this would be the first quarter since Q4 2022 in which the Magnificent 7 grew earnings more slowly than the rest of the index. Third, the index-level growth rate stayed high throughout: FactSet put blended S&amp;P 500 earnings growth at 32.9 percent on November 20, 2026, with 84 percent of reporting companies beating earnings estimates. The quarter was not weak; it was broad.</p><h2>What the ledger does not establish</h2><p>Analysis, labeled as analysis: the ledger supports a fragility claim about prices more than a claim about earnings. A basket producing 67 percent of price returns means index holders carry AI exposure whether they chose it or not, and a repricing of that basket moves the whole index mechanically. The earnings rows cut against the stronger claim that only AI names can grow profits; in Q3 2026 the other 493 grew faster. What neither dataset establishes is whether the capex earns its return. The basket rows measure spending share, not returns on that spending, and one quarter of earnings breadth does not settle a multi-year investment cycle. The ledger also cannot price the basket: concentration can persist for years while underlying businesses grow, and the November 2026 correction, in which the technology sector fell more than 20 percent while the index fell 10 percent from its high, is consistent with the fragility reading without proving valuations wrong. The two series to watch are the basket's capex share in JPMorgan's next update and whether FactSet's Q4 2026 estimates, which put Magnificent 7 growth at 22.6 percent against 30.8 percent for the other 493, survive contact with reported results.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
