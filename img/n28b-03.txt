<p>ByteHerald's layer scorecard finds that five of the six layers an agent identity stack needs shipped as named products within 17 days, from October 27 to November 13, 2026. Detection, trust scoring, registration, credential isolation, and a merchant authorization protocol all reached the market in that window. The missing layer is the one no vendor can sell: a shared registry that lets one company's verification travel to another company's checkout.</p>

Each layer is marked only where a primary product document or release supports it.</p>

<h2>What the scorecard shows</h2>
<p>Socure's October 27, 2026 launch covers three layers at once. Agent Detection classifies agentic traffic, Agent Trust Score evaluates identity, authorization, environment, behavior, and risk, and Agent Registration binds an agent to a KYC or KYB verified person or business with portable verifiable evidence and delegation chains. The same day, Infisical announced a $40 million Series B at a $600 million valuation to build Agent Vault, which brokers access so agents never hold the underlying credentials, according to its PR Newswire release of October 27, 2026.</p>
<p>Seventeen days later, Sierra's Poppy v1.0 announcement of November 13, 2026, supplied the merchant side. Shopify enabled Poppy for every merchant, more than 100 design partners are live, and the protocol uses OAuth authentication with MCP, OpenAPI, and UCP standards. Sierra states its commerce extension lets an agent shop as a guest or sign in with a customer account and asks the customer before certain actions. Demand signals arrived in the same week: Bank of America warned on agent identity risk in reporting dated October 27, 2026, and Home Depot's CIO raised the same concern in reporting dated October 28, 2026.</p>
<h2>Methodology and limits</h2>
<p>Methodology: we required a primary product page or company release for each layer and recorded the earliest dated evidence. Limits: vendor pages describe intended capability, not measured performance. No layer publishes false-positive rates for detection, accuracy for trust scores, or adoption counts beyond Sierra's partner figure. The Bank of America and Home Depot signals rest on contemporaneous reporting rather than primary transcripts, so we mark them as demand signals, not product evidence. The evidence does not establish that any layer interoperates with another vendor's layer today.</p>
<h2>Analysis</h2>
<p>Analysis: the stack is assembling bottom-up, with each vendor owning one layer and no neutral registry connecting them. That structure mirrors early web identity, where every site ran its own login until shared standards consolidated the market. Merchants enabling Poppy today are betting that the authorization layer consolidates first, because checkout is where agent identity turns into revenue. Until registration evidence becomes portable across vendors, each merchant still verifies agents largely on its own.</p>
