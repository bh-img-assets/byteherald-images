<h2>What the dataset shows</h2>
<p>Arena relaunched the board on November 18, 2026 with a headline Agent Index, rescaled so the average model of July 2026 sits near 1,000. On that scale Fable 5.2 leads at 1,449, ahead of Opus 5.5 at 1,342 and Gemini 4 Neon (xHigh) at 1,331 on a preliminary rating. The underlying net-effect estimates tell a tighter story: Fable's +27.44 percent against Opus 5.5's +19.12 percent is a gap of about 8.3 points, narrower than the roughly 11-point gap on November 11. Cost is where the frontier separates: Arena's November 22 board puts Fable's median cost per task at $17.48, while BenchLeader's November 27 reading puts Carbon at $1.35 with a net effect of +16.1 percent.</p>
<h2>What this does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: Arena scores are causal estimates from real sessions in which users set and judge their own tasks, so session mix differs across models and preliminary ratings rest on thinner samples. The dataset supports two claims only: the leader held across 353,321 additional sessions, and the cost of the lead is now measurable. Whether the premium buys proportionate reliability is the question the next board should answer.</p>
<h2>How Arena grades, in brief</h2><p>Arena routes each real Agent Mode session to a randomly chosen model and records whether the user confirmed the task was done, praise against complaints, how much steering the user had to do, and how often the model's own shell commands failed. Each signal is estimated as the model's effect against the average model, then averaged. The November 18, 2026 relaunch rescaled that average into the Agent Index, where the average model of July 2026 sits near 1,000 points and one point is a thousandth of a standard deviation. That rescaling is why the November 11 percentages and the November 22 index points cannot be compared directly, and why this dataset keeps both scales visible instead of converting one into the other. Cost per task is the median over the prior 14 days, so it moves with session mix as well as with model behavior.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
