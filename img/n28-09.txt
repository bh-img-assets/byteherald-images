<p>ByteHerald's verification ledger finds that OpenAI's three science claims do not stand on equal evidence. Two of the three arrived with public natural language proofs and Lean formalizations that outside researchers can inspect. The physics claim did not arrive with a full public artifact set, because the collaboration handling it chose to write up the work itself. Counting public artifact sets gives two auditable claims out of three, by our count of the release decisions OpenAI described on October 26, 2026. Reporting the announcement as three equal breakthroughs would erase that difference.</p>
<p>The method grades each claim by artifact, not by headline. A claim is auditable in this ledger only when the natural language argument and the Lean formalization are public. Lean checking is treated as machine verification of the formal argument as encoded. It is not treated as proof that the formal statement captures the intended mathematical claim in every semantic detail, and it is not treated as peer review. Dates are taken from OpenAI's announcement, which is the primary source for what the company says its systems produced and when.</p>

<p>The mathematics row is the clearest release. OpenAI said its models produced a proof of the local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, based on a novel auxiliary partial differential equation, and that the result also resolves the Kakeya conjecture and Stein's restriction conjecture in all dimensions. OpenAI stated that it obtained the AI output on October 18, 2026, formalized it in Lean the following day, and released both the natural language writeup and the Lean proof in its public GitHub repository. The historical frame in the announcement is specific: Sogge proposed the conjecture in 1991, Guth, Wang and Zhang proved the two-dimensional case in 2019, and Wang and Zahl proved the three-dimensional Kakeya conjecture in 2025. Those prior results are context, not verification of the new claim.</p>
<p>The computer science row has the same artifact strength and a different review path. OpenAI said its system proved that the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false, with the natural language disproof produced on October 16, 2026 and verified in Lean the next day. The collaboration decided to make the natural language proof and Lean formalization public immediately through OpenAI's GitHub repository. The announcement also formed an external group of collaborators to explain the result. Analysis: public artifacts make the claim examinable now, while the collaborator process addresses explanation and scrutiny. Neither step, by itself, completes independent acceptance by the field.</p>
<p>The physics row is where the ledger refuses to upgrade language. OpenAI announced rigorous existence proofs for quantum field theories in four dimensions, including quantum chromodynamics and pure Yang-Mills theory. It also said materials and artifacts related to the results would be turned over to the collaborations, and reporting of the announcement described the physics materials as held back while the review group writes up the work. Without the full public artifacts, outsiders cannot run the same checks available for the other two rows. The correct status is announced and under collaboration review, not verified.</p>
<p>What this asset does not establish matters for readers. It does not establish that any of the three results is correct. It does not establish that Lean formalization removes the need for human review of definitions, assumptions and interpretation. It also does not establish practical consequences for algorithms or physics until the relevant communities examine the arguments. The citable contribution is the evidence map: which claims can be checked from public artifacts today, which dates OpenAI itself assigned to production and formalization, and which claim remains outside public audit.</p>
