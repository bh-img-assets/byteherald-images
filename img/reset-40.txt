<p>ByteHerald's threshold audit finds two different claims wearing one headline. METR published its independent assessment on November 5, 2026, reporting that Anthropic's internal capabilities index rose 1.9 times as fast from October 2025 as in the earlier period, with a 95 percent confidence interval of 1.5 to 2.8 times, and that Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative. Five days later, Anthropic's Amendment No. 2 filed November 10, 2026 stated that the company had met its Responsible Scaling Policy threshold based on what it expects from future models and its overall rate of progress, rather than on what it has observed from current models.</p>
<p>The asset is a claim-separation audit. It places the measured estimate, the attribution judgment and the policy determination in separate rows, because each rests on different evidence. METR measured a pace. METR judged, with high uncertainty, that AI accounts for most of the speed-up. Anthropic declared a policy status. Only the first of those is a measurement an outsider can inspect, and even it depends on modeling choices that move the point estimate.</p>

<p>Methodology: ByteHerald extracted each claim with its date and evidence class from the METR assessment and the filing sequence. We did not re-fit METR's benchmark model, because the underlying internal index and redacted evidence are not public. The audit therefore tests consistency, not the fit itself: does the filing language claim the same thing the measurement shows? It does not. The filing moved from imminent to met between November 2 and November 10, 2026, while the measured estimate stayed at 1.9 times with an interval that includes values well below and above two.</p>
<p>The policy definition explains part of the gap. Anthropic's threshold can be met by an expected doubling in the pace of progress, not only an observed one. A determination based on future models and overall pace can therefore be consistent with the policy while remaining unproven as a measurement. That is not a contradiction. It is a warning against reading a policy declaration as independent confirmation of the 1.9 times estimate, or reading the estimate as proof that the policy threshold was crossed by observation.</p>
<p>What this does not establish: it does not show that AI caused the acceleration, because METR's attribution rests substantially on redacted evidence. It does not show recursive self-improvement, autonomous research replacement, or any specific capability in a named current model. It does not validate the superexponential extrapolations discussed around the report, which METR treats as uncertain. And it does not tell outsiders which safeguards changed in practice after the determination, because the filings declare a status more clearly than they enumerate operational consequences.</p>
<p>Analysis: the citable ByteHerald finding is the separation itself. As of November 18, 2026, the public record contains a measured pace of 1.9 times with wide uncertainty, an attribution judgment outsiders cannot fully inspect, and a policy determination explicitly based on expectations. Any sentence that merges those three into one proven fact overstates the record.</p>
