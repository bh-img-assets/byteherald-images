<p>ByteHerald's PERM pipeline dataset measures the labor-certification system Brad Smith was defending: nearly one fiscal year 2026 certification in three sat in software, systems, data, or software testing work. The US Department of Labor reported 159,277 PERM certifications in fiscal year 2026, with data as of September 30, 2026. Software developers accounted for 36,471 certifications. Computer systems analysts accounted for 7,046. Data scientists accounted for 3,968. Software quality assurance analysts and testers accounted for 2,891. The PERM dataset gives that warning a measurable denominator. Software developers alone were 22.9 percent of certifications. The four software, systems, data and testing groups together were about 31.6 percent by ByteHerald arithmetic.</p><p>Analysis: a PERM pause binds first where certifications concentrate. The dataset does not prove that any named employer would lose a specific number of workers. It does show that the occupation groups closest to AI engineering and software delivery form a large share of the permanent labor certification pipeline.</p><h2>What the dataset does not establish</h2><p>The Department of Labor table does not name the suspended employers in the selected statistics used here. It also does not show how many certified workers later received green cards, how many changed employers, or how many students chose other countries. Those outcomes require employer-level and immigration-outcome records beyond the selected statistics table.</p><p>Analysis: Smith's warning is a forecast about talent location over admission cycles. The PERM dataset supports the exposure part of that forecast: the pipeline is heavily weighted toward software and data occupations. It does not by itself prove the generational outcome Smith predicted.</p><p>How to cite this dataset: cite 110,809 applications received, 171,079 determinations, 159,277 certifications and the occupation counts to the Department of Labor fiscal year 2026 selected statistics with data as of September 30, 2026.
