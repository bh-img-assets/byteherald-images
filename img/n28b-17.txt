<p>ByteHerald's two-bid ledger, built from the Delaware docket, corrects the story this page first told. Character.AI is being sold in two pieces, and Disney's $20 million buys the piece without the users. The platform, accounts, and chat content sit in a separate $10 million stalking-horse bid from a company formed one day before its designation, and 41 states and the District of Columbia have objected to selling the chats at all. The two opening bids total $30 million in cash.</p><p>Character Technologies, Inc. filed for Chapter 11 on October 12, 2026 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, case No. 26-11603, before Judge Brendan Linehan Shannon. The court approved bidding procedures on November 4, 2026. Disney Worldwide Services is the stalking-horse bidder at $20 million cash for a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive license to specified technology and patents, plus claims against former employees who join Disney. The license excludes the consumer app, user data, personal information, user inputs and outputs, training data, and finished models. Disney paid $7.5 million before the filing, of which $2 million is a refundable deposit.</p><p>The second piece emerged after this article's first version. On November 14, 2026 the debtor designated Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC as stalking-horse bidder for the remaining assets at $10 million plus specified liabilities. The company was formed on November 13, 2026. On November 24, 2026 the debtor disclosed its backer as Octave Fund I LLC, and the judge said the court was prepared to approve the designation. Bid protections in both packages cap break-up fees at 3.5 percent, $700,000 on the Disney bid and $350,000 on the Smirks bid, by the percentages stated in the filings.</p><table><tr><th>Bidder</th><th>Assets</th><th>Cash</th><th>User data included</th></tr>

Bids were due November 24, 2026 at 11 a.m. Central. An auction is set for December 1, 2026, and the sale hearing for December 3 and 4, 2026. Attorneys general from 41 states and the District of Columbia objected on November 20, 2026, asking the court to stop the sale of private chats, including more than 2.1 million accounts registered to minors. The sale itself has not been approved.</p><p>Analysis: the split is the finding. Disney's license is structured to leave personal information out, while the platform bid carries the data the states are fighting over. The $20 million headline therefore prices technology rights, and the contested $10 million prices the operating platform and its data. Readers who treat the Disney figure as the value of the whole company will misread both numbers.</p><p>The human thread runs through the filings as well. Disney sent Character.AI cease-and-desist letters late in 2025 over unauthorized impersonations of its characters, then hired chief executive Karandeep Anand as its first chief technology officer in September 2026 along with several technical staff. The transition services terms in the sale documents run 60 days after closing or 75 days after the auction, with the winner paying cost plus a 10 percent margin, and Disney may retain up to 20 percent of former employees it hired away from a winner's recruiting. The first-day declaration states the platform will be wound down if no higher or better bid supports continued operations.</p><p>What to watch is the December 1, 2026 auction and the December 3 and 4 sale hearing. Rival bids above the two floors, and the court's treatment of the states' objection, will decide whether the platform and its chats change hands at all.</p><p>Methodology: ByteHerald compiled this ledger from docket entries in case No. 26-11603 as reported from the filings: the petition of October 12, 2026, the sale motion and license agreement of October 13, 2026, the bidding procedures order of November 4, 2026, the Smirks designation of November 14, 2026, and the states' objection of November 20, 2026. Cash totals are our addition of the two opening bids.</p><p>What this does not establish: stalking-horse bids are floors, not outcomes.
