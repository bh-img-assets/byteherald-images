<p>ByteHerald's evidence ledger finds that the October open-weight contest had only one crown resting on an independent score of shipped weights. Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro scored 46 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2 after its MIT licensed weights were released on September 22, 2026. Meta's claim rests on its own model card tables. Mistral's later independent score uses Index v5, a different ruler, so it cannot be ranked directly against Xiaomi's v4.3.2 result.</p>
<p>That last point is the one most comparisons miss. An index score is only comparable inside the same index version, with the same task set and weighting. Placing a v5 score beside a v4.3.2 score produces a ranking the underlying measurements do not support.</p>
<h2>The ByteHerald evidence ledger</h2>
<p>We graded each contender on four documented facts: release date, license, active parameter share, and the strongest benchmark evidence available for the shipped weights. Active shares are our division of the published active and total parameter counts.</p>

<p>All three are sparse mixture-of-experts models, so the active share is the honest size comparison. Xiaomi activates about 4.1 percent of its parameters per token, Meta about 5.1 percent, and Mistral about 5.0 percent. Headline trillion parameter counts describe stored capacity, not per-token compute.</p>
<p>The licenses also differ in kind. Xiaomi ships under MIT. Meta ships under its custom Muse Model License, which carries attribution and a model-as-a-service revenue condition. Mistral ships under its ecosystem open-weight license. Calling all three equally open compresses three different legal positions into one word.</p>
<h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: the documents opened do not establish a single best open model across all uses. They establish that Xiaomi held the strongest independently scored position on the v4.3.2 ruler, that Meta's tables are vendor reported, and that Mistral's weights arrived on October 29, 2026 with a later v5 score of 26. A fair rematch requires all three on one index version.</p>
<p>Until then, the defensible statement is procedural, not promotional: one independent crown, one vendor claim, and one later measurement on a changed ruler.</p>
<p>Context windows do not separate these models either. All three document a 1M token context, so long-context marketing cannot rank them. What separates them in the documents is evidence quality and legal terms, which is why this ledger grades those two properties instead of repeating launch superlatives.</p>
<p>Release timing also changes what a fair comparison can claim. Xiaomi's weights were public from September 22, 2026, Meta's from October 15, 2026, and Mistral's from October 29, 2026, after a preview that began on October 6. A comparison written in mid-October could not have tested Mistral's shipped weights, because they did not exist publicly yet. Any ranking from that window that treated Mistral as an open-weight contender was ranking a promise.</p>
<p>Hardware cost is the second practical separator the score tables hide. Trillion parameter checkpoints require multi-GPU serving even when only about 5 percent of parameters activate per token, because the full weight set must be stored and routed. A buyer comparing these models on score alone, without memory, throughput, and license terms, is comparing the cheapest part of deployment.</p>
