By ByteHerald's arithmetic, Anthropic's pre-expense proceeds are about 96.9 percent of the gross offering total. The $26.0 billion concurrent private placement is separate money at the same per-share price, and the prospectus states the underwriters receive no discount or commission on it. Adding the two figures into one raise number without saying so would misstate the document.</p><h2>The 94 pages, checked against the table of contents</h2><p>The risk warning in this article's title is measurable in the filing itself. The final prospectus table of contents lists Risk Factors beginning on page 30 and the Special Note Regarding Forward-Looking Statements on page 124. By ByteHerald's subtraction, the risk section spans 94 pages before the forward-looking statements note begins. That is a page-count fact about the document, not a judgment about how likely any single risk is.</p><h2>Control does not follow the money</h2><p>The same prospectus describes a governance structure in which public buyers receive economics without control. A single Class F share held by a founder limited liability company carries votes equal to 50.1 percent of total voting power on matters submitted to stockholders generally, with no economic rights. The Long-Term Benefit Trust, through Class T stock, generally elects and removes the Class T directors, who together constitute a majority of the board. Class A shares carry one vote each. Anthropic states in the prospectus that it is a controlled company under Nasdaq governance requirements.</p><p>Analysis: the filing record supports two conclusions and blocks a third. It supports that pricing moved above the marketed range, because $83.00 is $5.00 above the $78.00 top of the November 17 range by our subtraction. It supports that control sits outside the public float. It does not, on its own, establish a market valuation, because proceeds raised and shares sold are not a valuation until a share count and a market price are combined.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
