<p>ByteHerald's three-survey ledger separates the enterprise AI scale trap into three different constraints that headline percentages blur together. Cloudera measures unit cost: 89 percent of 1,500 IT leaders limited or redesigned AI use cases because of token consumption. Capgemini measures forecastability: 59 percent of 500 large-company executives spent significantly more on AI than expected, with affected organizations averaging 24 percent above expectation or budget. SnapLogic measures value proof: 54 percent of 1,000 IT decision-makers abandoned agentic AI projects because business value was unclear. The percentages cannot be averaged, because the samples and questions differ. Read as a ledger, they show three separate failure points.</p>

<h2>Three constraints, not one</h2>
<p>The first constraint is unit cost. Cloudera's 89 percent figure answers a specific question: whether rising token consumption forced the organization to limit or redesign AI use cases. The same survey reports 45 percent moved AI workloads in response to higher or more volatile costs. That is a workload-economics finding, not a general AI failure rate.</p>
<p>The second constraint is forecastability. Capgemini's 59 percent figure answers a budget question: whether AI expenditure over the past 12 months was significantly higher than expected or budgeted. Among affected organizations, expenditure averaged 24 percent above expectation or budget, and 21 percent overshot by more than 30 percent. A team can know its token price and still fail this test if usage grows faster than the forecast.</p>
<p>The third constraint is value proof. SnapLogic reports 85 percent deploying agentic AI at scale or fully prepared for large-scale deployment, while 54 percent abandoned projects because business value was unclear and 48 percent struggle or expect to struggle to measure ROI. Analysis: readiness and abandonment can coexist in the same survey because they measure different things. Deployment confidence is not value evidence.</p>
<p>What the ledger does not establish is a single enterprise failure rate. The surveys cover different populations: global IT leaders, executives at organizations above $1 billion in revenue, and IT decision-makers in three countries. All three are vendor-commissioned or vendor-published surveys with self-reported answers. The citable finding is the separation: token cost, budget forecastability and value proof are three distinct gates, and an AI program can pass one while failing the next.</p>
