<p>ByteHerald separated the Wyoming campus into what is contracted, what is announced, and what is merely ambition. The contracted piece is 702 megawatts of fuel cells. Against the campus's announced initial scale of 1.8 gigawatts, that covers 39 percent of the power needed, by our arithmetic. Against the 10 gigawatt expansion ambition, it covers 7 percent. Google bought a site and a power plan, not a powered campus, and the $17 billion headline is planned construction investment, not the undisclosed purchase price.</p>

<h2>What Google actually bought</h2>
<p>Crusoe's November 6, 2026 release says the acquisition completes its pre-development work: site selection, governmental approvals and power strategy, developed with Tallgrass Energy. Google's November 5 announcement names Tallgrass, Black Hills Energy, American Electric Power and Related Digital as partners, describes an air-cooled design to limit water strain, and calls the 702 megawatt fuel-cell development with carbon capture the largest planned of its kind. Reporting on the deal adds that Crusoe had tried and failed to land Google as a customer and that Google raised concerns about cost and construction timeline; those reasons come from reporting, not from either company's release, and neither company disclosed the price.</p>
<p>The power gap is the project. Tallgrass is developing a natural gas hub beside the site, and Black Hills holds service agreements reported to run through 2048, but the only contracted generation named in Google's own announcement is the 702 megawatt fuel-cell phase. Everything beyond 39 percent of the initial scale depends on power arrangements still being built.</p>
<h2>What the ledger does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: Google has not published a gigawatt capacity for its own campus plan, a construction schedule in its announcement, or a breakdown of the $17 billion across buildings, power and chips. The 1.8 and 10 gigawatt figures are Crusoe-era development ambitions, so our coverage percentages measure the contracted phase against the seller's old scale, not against a Google-confirmed target. Local reporting puts construction completion around 2031. Until Google states its own capacity, the firm facts are the site transfer, the partners, and 702 contracted megawatts.</p>
<p>The transaction also marks a change in who carries development risk. Crusoe's model was to assemble land, approvals and power, then sell capacity to a hyperscaler. Here the hyperscaler bought the assembly itself and took construction in-house, after reportedly losing confidence in the developer's cost and timeline. Analysis: that pattern, buying the project rather than the output, is what a power-constrained market produces when the scarce asset is a permitted site with a credible power plan. The ledger's contracted row is the evidence: Google paid an undisclosed sum for groundwork whose only firm generation number is 702 megawatts, because the groundwork, not the megawatts, was the scarce part. Future announcements can be graded against this ledger row by row as contracts replace ambitions over the coming construction years still ahead.</p>
