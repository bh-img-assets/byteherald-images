<p>ByteHerald's allowance ledger shows the December ChatGPT and Codex change is two changes at once: the pool merges, and the meter becomes model-specific without a published token weighting. OpenAI's Help Center guidance says Plus, Pro, and Business plans will share one token-measured allowance across ChatGPT and Codex from December 2026, while Instant chat does not draw from it. The citable fact is the missing denominator: subscribers are told the pool is shared, but not how many tokens each plan contains or how cached, new, and output tokens are weighted.</p>

<h2>What changed before the merge</h2>
<p>OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on November 17, 2026 for Work and Codex users on Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu plans, according to OpenAI release reporting that day. The frontier model's list price fell: Astra input moved from $10.00 to $8.00 per million tokens and output from $50.00 to $40.00, a 20 percent cut on both by our arithmetic. Cached input fell from $1.00 to $0.40, a 60 percent cut. Sol's list price did not move.</p>
<p>The billing correction then moved effective consumption in the opposite direction. OpenAI said on November 26, 2026 that a launch billing mistake had undercounted GPT-6.2 usage, and that after the fix users would reach limits about 40 percent faster on Astra and about 25 percent faster on Sol than the prior week. OpenAI added one banked reset for Go, Plus, Pro, and Business accounts. Those percentages are OpenAI's comparison to the discounted launch week, not a permanent price table.</p>
<h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: a lower API list price does not prove a larger subscription allowance. The December pool could be generous or tight depending on the unpublished token weighting and plan size. Likewise, the November 26 billing fix does not prove GPT-6.2 became more expensive than GPT-6; it proves the launch week was undercounted. Confusing those baselines would turn a correction into a price rise.</p>
<p>The shared pool also does not make every ChatGPT action equal. Instant chat is excluded from the allowance in OpenAI's guidance. Agent work, coding runs, and advanced-model tasks are the activities that compete for the shared meter.</p>
<h2>The citable point</h2>
<p>Our ledger separates three numbers readers often merge: list price, billing correction, and allowance size. List price is published. The correction percentages are published. Allowance size and token weighting are not. Until OpenAI publishes those, the December change should be described as a shared meter with an undisclosed denominator.</p>
<p>For heavy Codex users, the practical test is simpler than the pricing table. If a morning of agent work reduces afternoon ChatGPT capacity, the shared pool is binding. If Instant chat remains available after the allowance is exhausted, the exclusion is doing real work.
