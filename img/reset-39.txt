<p>ByteHerald's stack-height ledger finds buyers trading capacity per stack for accelerators shipped. Samsung's published specifications put HBM3 at 819 gigabytes per second per stack, HBM3E at 1,180, HBM4 at up to 3,300 and the sampled 12-high HBM4E at up to 3,600. Those are vendor specification ceilings. The demand signal arrived on October 28, 2026, when SK hynix said on its third-quarter earnings call that DRAM and HBM output was effectively sold out through 2029 when secured long-term agreements are combined with demand under discussion, that signed agreements cover more than 50 percent of 2027 and 2028 production capacity, and that customers are requesting more than twice the volume it can supply.</p>
<p>The asset separates three kinds of evidence that earnings coverage usually blends: published stack specifications, contracted supply, and demand still under discussion. Keeping them separate is the point. A specification says what a stack can move. A signed agreement says what a buyer has committed to take. Demand under discussion says what buyers say they want. Only the first two are hard numbers, and the sold-out claim depends on the third.</p>

<p>Methodology: ByteHerald classified each row by evidence class before drawing any conclusion. Specification rows are ceilings from Samsung's published specifications, not measured system bandwidth. Earnings-call rows are SK hynix statements, not audited contract totals. The sold-out row is explicitly a combination: secured long-term agreement volume plus demand under discussion that the company described as concrete purchasing plans. We did not convert the more-than-twice request figure into a shortage tonnage, because the call does not publish the underlying volume.</p>
<p>Stack height is where the trade becomes visible. More layers add capacity in the same footprint, but each layer complicates the thermal path and adds a bonding interface where a defect can spoil the stack. An 8-layer stack carries less capacity than a 12-layer stack. When supply is the binding constraint, a buyer can rationally prefer more accelerators with less memory each over fewer accelerators with more memory each. SK hynix read the 8-layer requests the same way: as an effort to build more AI accelerators from limited memory supply.</p>
<p>What this does not establish: it does not prove that 8-layer HBM4 will outsell 12-layer HBM4, because the call reports requests, not shipment shares. It does not prove the sold-out horizon, because part of that horizon rests on demand under discussion rather than signed agreements. It does not establish system-level performance for any accelerator, because per-stack bandwidth ceilings do not include packaging, thermals, yields or the number of stacks a given product carries.</p>
<p>Analysis: the citable finding is the qualifier structure. Signed agreements cover more than half of two future years of capacity, by SK hynix's October 28, 2026 statement, while the 2029 sold-out claim requires adding unsigned demand. A reader who tracks only the headline will overstate contracted certainty. A reader who tracks the three evidence classes separately can see both the real commitment and the negotiating position built on top of it.</p>
