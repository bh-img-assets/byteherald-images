<p>Tencent launched the full version of Hy4 on November 4, 2026, with vision and computer use, and called it one of the strongest open-weight models available. The full model weights are not yet downloadable, so today Hy4 is an API product with an open-weight promise, not an open model a developer can run. That single distinction changes every practical decision about Hy4, from where data goes to whether a team can fine-tune it at all.</p>

<h2>What changed since the August preview</h2>
<p>Hy4 succeeds a preview released on August 28, 2026, which was text only and shipped with downloadable weights under an Apache 2.0 license. The full release, 68 days later, adds the two capabilities that matter most for agent work. First, vision: fine-grained image understanding, complex chart analysis, and mathematical reasoning from visual information. Second, computer use: operating software through command-line and graphical interfaces, with training focused on office tasks, financial analysis, and game development, including operating tools such as Blender and computer-aided design software. Hy4 is a 770-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, with 49 billion parameters active per token, figures from the preview model card that the full release carries over, and a context window of about one million tokens.</p>
<p>Tencent's own reported improvements over the preview are large, and they are labeled here as Tencent-reported because no independent reproduction exists yet. Tencent reported a 19.1 percent improvement on GameDev and a 9.8 percent improvement on GameLogic benchmarks, and said its internal search evaluations showed a hallucination rate falling from 15.76 percent in the August preview to 5.45 percent in the full release. Tencent also acknowledged, in its launch post, a large gap between Hy4 and the top closed models, a caveat that should travel with every headline calling Hy4 a top-tier model. A model can be first among open-weight models and still trail the closed frontier, and Tencent said both things.</p>
<h2>What you can use today, and what is only promised</h2>
<p>Today, Hy4 is reachable as a hosted service through Tencent Cloud TokenHub, WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, Yuanbao, ima, the WeCom intelligent assistant Dayuan, and through APIs on OpenRouter, Hermes, and OpenCode. Free usage in WorkBuddy and CodeBuddy runs from November 4 to November 15, 2026, and TokenHub carries a 50 percent discount through December 31, 2026. Reported API pricing after the free window is 0.834 dollars per million input tokens and 2.501 dollars per million output tokens, with cached input at 0.042 dollars, figures consistent with the 6 yuan input and 18 yuan output prices in Tencent's launch materials. A lighter Hy4 Flash version focused on speed and cost is also announced as coming soon, with no specifications, date, or price.</p>
<p>What is not available today is the part that makes a model open in the practical sense. Tencent says it plans to release the full model weights and system cards in the coming days, with support lined up from the vLLM and SGLang inference communities, but it gives no date, and it has not published a license or a model card for the final model. Until those files appear, nobody outside Tencent can download, run, fine-tune, or independently inspect Hy4 itself. Every request goes through Tencent's API, which means data residency, compliance, and cost questions are answered by a vendor service agreement, not by self-hosting. The preview weights remain downloadable, but they are the August model, not the November model with vision and computer use.</p>

<h2>The vendor benchmark, read with its label on</h2>
<p>Tencent published a head-to-head computer-use result on OSWorld 2.1, and the chart below reproduces it with its label intact: these are Tencent-reported scores, not independently reproduced results. Hy4 scored 78.9, behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 81.8 and Kimi K3.1 Pro at 79.6, and ahead of GPT-6.1 Sol at 75.2. The gap to the leader is 2.9 points, and the gap to Kimi is 0.7 points, which is small enough that a different benchmark, a different task mix, or an independent rerun could reorder the middle of the table. That is not a reason to dismiss the result, and it is a reason not to treat a vendor table as a leaderboard. Until the weights land and someone outside Tencent runs OSWorld on this exact model, the honest description is that Tencent reports Hy4 close to, but not ahead of, the two models it chose for comparison.</p>

<h2>The Kimi control case: a promise kept on the same week</h2>
<p>The cleanest way to judge Hy4's missing weights is to look at the model that shipped them on schedule. Moonshot launched Kimi K3.1 Pro on October 23, 2026, promised its full weights by November 4, 2026, and published them on Hugging Face as moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro by that date. Kimi K3.1 Pro is a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 104 billion parameters active per token, a one-million-token context, and API pricing of 2 dollars per million input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens. Its custom Kimi K3.1 Pro license is MIT-style with extra conditions that apply only to the largest API businesses and products, so teams must read it before commercial self-hosting, but the files exist, which is the point. Moonshot's own reported Terminal-Bench 4.0 score of 65.7 percent sits 0.7 points behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 66.4 percent, again a vendor figure, not an independent result.</p>
<p>The contrast is not that Tencent broke a promise, because Tencent promised weights in coming days, not on November 4, and the preview was already open. The contrast is that two Chinese labs chose opposite launch orders in the same week: Moonshot shipped the model, then the weights, on a dated promise; Tencent shipped the hosted service first and left the weights as an undated next step. For a developer, that order decides what is possible this month. With Kimi, a team can download and run the model, subject to its license. With Hy4, a team can only call it, and must wait to find out under which license, and with which safety evaluations, the weights will eventually arrive.</p>
<h2>How to tell an open model from an open announcement</h2>
<p>Five checks separate the two, and they are worth running on every launch that calls itself open. First, can you download the weights for this exact version, not a preview? Second, is there a license that states what commercial use allows? Third, is there a system card with safety evaluations for this version? Fourth, can an independent party rerun the headline benchmark on the downloaded weights? Fifth, does the vendor state a date for anything still missing, or only a direction? Hy4 today passes none of the first four for the full model, and only the direction, not a date, for the fifth. Kimi K3.1 Pro passes the first four, with its license as the reading homework. How future leaderboards judge Hy4 will depend on the same discipline that rebuilt the last one, <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/how-intelligence-index-v5-rewrote-ai.html">when Intelligence Index v5 moved to mostly private tests and widened the open-weights gap</a>, because a model that cannot be independently run cannot be independently ranked.</p>
