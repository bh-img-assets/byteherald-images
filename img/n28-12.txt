<p>ByteHerald's weight audit finds that Intelligence Index v5 is a new ruler, not a refreshed ranking on the old one. The v5 design uses nine evaluations across four categories weighted at 25 percent each, and the nine evaluation weights sum to 100 percent by our addition. Artificial Analysis put 60 percent of the index weight on private test sets or held-out answers in v5, compared with 45 percent in the v4.3 design described in methodology summaries. The increase is 15 percentage points, by our subtraction. A model score can therefore move between versions because the test mixture changed, even before any model change is considered.</p>
<p>The method audits design weights rather than republishing model scores. Category weights and evaluation weights are taken from the v5 methodology descriptions. The audit checks two things: whether category weights sum to the stated category total, and whether all evaluation weights sum to 100 percent. It does not recompute model rankings, because the underlying private and held-out items are not public and the official total also freezes an Elo component for AA-Briefcase in the methodology description. Cross-version score differences are treated as design breaks unless Artificial Analysis states a bridge, which the summaries used here do not provide.</p>

<p>The category arithmetic is exact. Knowledge Work sums to 25 percent from 15 plus 5 plus 5. Coding sums to 25 percent from 15 plus 10. Science sums to 25 percent from 15 plus 10. General sums to 25 percent from 15 plus 10, with AA-Omniscience split into 10 percent accuracy and 5 percent non-hallucination. Adding all nine weights gives 100 percent. That audit sounds basic, but it is the difference between citing a composite and repeating a leaderboard graphic. A composite with equal category weights makes a value judgment: knowledge work, coding, science and general performance count equally in the total, regardless of a reader's actual workload.</p>
<p>The version break is larger than the category labels suggest. Methodology summaries describe v4.3 as ten evaluations with category weights of 30, 20, 30 and 20 percent, while v5 uses nine evaluations at 25 percent each. GDPval-AA was a constituent through v4.3.2 and was removed in v5.0. The private or held-out share rose from 45 percent to 60 percent. Analysis: those three changes together mean a v5 score and a v4 score are not two readings of one instrument. They are readings from related instruments with different mixtures, different category priorities and different exposure to memorized public items.</p>
<p>The private share cuts both ways. Held-out items reduce the value of training on public benchmark questions, which is the stated purpose of moving weight toward private sets. The same move reduces outside reproducibility, because readers cannot inspect every item or rerun the full composite from public materials. The methodology summaries also describe pass at 1 style scoring, fixed temperature settings by model type, repeated API attempts for failures and manual review for persistent failures. Those procedures support consistency inside Artificial Analysis runs. They do not make the private portion independently auditable by readers.</p>
<p>What this asset does not establish is which model is best for a specific buyer. It does not convert the composite into a percentage of general intelligence, and it does not supply cost, speed or reliability for a workload. It establishes how to cite v5 honestly: name the version, name the equal category weights, note the 60 percent private or held-out share, and avoid comparing a v5 total with a v4 total as if the ruler had stayed still. The leaderboard changed because the measurement changed first.</p>
