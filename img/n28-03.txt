The developer often does not keep the token revenue.</p><p>The updated filing rows widen the caution. Anthropic later reported an $81 billion run rate as of October 31, 2026. ByteDance is $4.0 billion of the $11.0 billion Chinese sum, or 36.4 percent. The four model-focused firms in the table, Z.ai, Moonshot, DeepSeek, and MiniMax, sum to $4.6 billion, or 41.8 percent of the Chinese total. Anthropic alone, at $65.0 billion in the Epoch AI table, is 5.9 times the six-firm Chinese sum. OpenAI alone, at $40.0 billion, is 3.6 times that sum. Those ratios are arithmetic on mixed-month run rates, so they should be cited with the basis column attached.</p><p>Epoch AI gives the mechanism. Z.ai listed GLM 5.3 Flash on OpenRouter on August 26, 2026 and released weights at the same time. By September 15, 2026 there were 26 rival hosts, and Z.ai share of the model tokens fell from 88 percent to 22 percent over 20 days. That is a 66 percentage point fall and a 75.0 percent relative decline, by ByteHerald arithmetic. Epoch AI also reports that only about 25 percent of GLM-5 tokens were served through Z.ai own API as of September 16, 2026, and only 28.4 percent of DeepSeek V4 Pro tokens were served by DeepSeek. A model can be popular while the originating lab captures a minority of serving revenue.</p><p>Consumer apps show the same capture problem from the other side. Epoch AI reports MiniMax AI-native apps had a 4.7 percent gross margin in the first nine months of 2025, while Open Platform and other enterprise services had a 69.4 percent gross margin in the same period. The gap is 64.7 percentage points. MiniMax apps averaged 27.6 million monthly active users and 1.77 million paid users in the first nine months of 2025, about 6.4 percent paid conversion by ByteHerald arithmetic. Low price and low paid conversion can coexist with high usage.</p><p>Analysis: the evidence supports a pricing and capture explanation more than a simple demand explanation. It does not prove that Chinese firms cannot close the gap. Enterprise and government deployments can carry better margins, and large conglomerates can monetize AI indirectly through cloud and advertising. Epoch AI explicitly warns that Alibaba and ByteDance may earn more through complementary product lines, and the revenue table excludes much of that indirect value. The citable conclusion is therefore bounded: in direct AI revenue rows collected from June to September 2026, two US labs captured about $105 billion against about $11 billion for six Chinese firms, and the OpenRouter and margin rows show a concrete reason direct capture can lag usage.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
