<p>ByteHerald finding: Fractile sold one round at three prices. Its own Companies House filings record about 577.4 million pounds of Series C shares issued in six weeks, with the top class priced at 1,452.35 pounds a share, about 1.8 times the 801.54 pounds paid for the first class, by our arithmetic. The headline valuation depends on which price a reader picks.</p>
<p>That is the fact the announcement coverage flattened. The UK government put the round at more than 500 million pounds and the valuation at over 5 billion pounds on November 11, 2026. Reuters put the round at 720 million dollars and the valuation at 7 billion dollars the same day. The filings, made weeks earlier, show what was actually issued, class by class.</p>

<p>Methodology: we summed the nine SH01 allotment returns by share class, multiplying shares issued by the stated price per share. Class totals and the 1.8 times price ratio are our arithmetic. The filings name no investors and state no valuation, so we do not assign any class to any named investor. Currency conversions in other coverage vary with the rate used, so this ledger stays in pounds, the currency of the filings.</p>
<h2>What the ledger shows</h2>
<p>Analysis: three prices in one round usually mean three different bargains. The C-3 class, about 392.2 million pounds at the highest price, looks like the headline new money. The C-1 and C-2 classes, issued on September 11, 2026 at lower prices, look like earlier commitments or conversions settled into the round. The filings do not say which, and Fractile has not explained the tiers. Any valuation built by multiplying the top price across all shares would overstate what earlier money paid.</p>
<p>The filed total also matters. About 577.4 million pounds of shares were allotted between September 9 and October 23, 2026, before the November 11 announcement. That is above the government floor of more than 500 million pounds, and it means the financing was substantially executed before it was public. The government release does not disclose how much public money went in. It says the Sovereign AI Venture Fund typically invests around 1 million to 10 million pounds per company, so the state share of a round this size is likely small, though the exact figure is undisclosed.</p>
<h2>The unshipped chip</h2>
<p>Neither the filings nor the government release records a shipped product, a customer deployment or an independent benchmark. The government describes hardware that is faster, more energy efficient and less costly as the aim. Fractile chief executive Walter Goodwin said the funding will let the company ship its first chips and systems in 2027, according to reporting of the announcement. Performance figures in coverage, including claims of up to 100 times better performance, are company claims, not independent measurements.</p>
<p>Analysis: the price step up is the risk in one number. The C-3 price of 1,452.35 pounds is about 1.8 times the C-1 price in the same round, and the round follows a 220 million dollar Series B in May 2026. Investors are paying for inference hardware that does not exist yet, in a market where the last British AI chip company to raise at this scale, Graphcore, was sold to SoftBank in July 2024 for an undisclosed price. That history does not decide Fractile outcome. It does define what the 7 billion dollar figure has to survive: shipment, independent benchmarks, and customers beyond reported early deals.</p>
<h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2>
<p>The filings do not establish a single valuation, because they record three prices and no investor names. They do not show how much of the 577.4 million pounds was new cash versus converted earlier instruments. The government release does not disclose the public stake or any shipment date. Until Fractile ships silicon and independent benchmarks exist, the citable record is a financing record: 507,334 Series C shares, three prices, and a valuation that changes with the class a reader chooses.</p>
