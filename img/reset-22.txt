<p>ByteHerald's verification ledger of 2026 cryptanalysis results separates what AI systems have actually broken from what they have merely weakened. Three RSA challenge numbers were factored in public this year, RSA-260 on September 3, RSA-896 on September 19 and RSA-270 on November 12, 2026. The public record stands at 896 bits. No production key, and no 1024-bit or 2048-bit RSA key, has been factored in public.</p><h2>The ledger: every 2026 result, graded by verification</h2><table><thead><tr><th>Result</th><th>Date</th><th>Verification status</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>RSA-260 factored (862 bits)</td><td>September 3, 2026</td><td>Factors published; multiplying them reproduces the challenge number</td></tr><tr><td>RSA-896 factored (896 bits)</td><td>September 19, 2026</td><td>Factors published; current public record</td></tr><tr><td>RSA-270 factored (895 bits)</td><td>November 12, 2026</td><td>Independent factorization completed November 16, 2026 produced a matching factor</td></tr><tr><td>HAWK expected key-recovery cost cut from 2^64 to 2^38</td><td>July 28, 2026</td><td>Candidate withdrawn from the NIST process the next day</td></tr><tr><td>Two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA</td><td>November 6, 2026</td><td>Theoretical improvements announced; no 1024-bit key factored</td></tr></tbody></table><p>Methodology: ByteHerald compiled this ledger from the primary publications: Eric Lu's September 3, 2026 factorization announcement, Stephen Weis's September 19, 2026 RSA-896 publication, Adithya Balaji's November 18, 2026 independent RSA-270 post, Anthropic's July 28, 2026 HAWK report and Google DeepMind's November 6, 2026 announcement. The public record had not moved since RSA-250 in February 2020; in 2026 it moved three times in ten weeks, each time through GPU implementations of the general number field sieve, a method known for decades. Lu's own write-up reports essentially no algorithmic advancement for RSA-260, which took about 4,900 GPU-days. What AI changed is the cost of building and tuning the software that runs the old method on AI hardware.</p><p>What the evidence does not establish: that RSA-1024 is broken, that any lab holds a polynomial-time factoring method, or that deployed keys are at immediate risk. Rumors of an unpublished factoring breakthrough remained unverified as of November 26, 2026, with no factors, code or checked proof published. The ledger's rule holds: a claim that publishes factors is a result; a claim that does not is a rumor.</p><h2>How the ledger was built</h2><p>The ledger admits a result only when a primary publication states it: a factorization announcement with factors, a vendor research report, or a lab announcement with named methods. Verification status is graded separately from the claim. A factorization is verified when its factors multiply to the challenge number. An attack improvement is verified when the target's custodians act on it, as NIST's process did after the HAWK result. A theoretical improvement stays theoretical until a key falls.</p><p>Analysis: graded this way, 2026 shows a cost curve bending, not a cipher breaking. Three factorizations in ten weeks after six static years is a real change in the economics of the general number field sieve on AI hardware. It is also bounded: 896 bits is the record, RSA-1024 remains unfactored in public, and the distance to 2048 bits is measured in orders of magnitude, not in months. The ledger keeps both facts in the same table so neither can be quoted without the other.</p><p>The ledger also records what did not happen in 2026. No 1024-bit RSA key was factored in public, no production cryptosystem was broken, and no peer-reviewed polynomial-time factoring method was published. Recording the absences matters because rumor fills gaps that a ledger leaves blank; the November 2026 factoring rumor circulated for days with no factors, code or proof behind it, and it stays in the rumor column until one of those appears.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
