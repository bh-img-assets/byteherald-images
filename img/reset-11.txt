<p>ByteHerald's re-run of the open speech scorecard finds a new leader and a clearer buying rule. On Artificial Analysis's AA-WER board as of November 12, 2026, Salesforce's SOV-Transcribe-1 leads open-weights models at 2.28 percent word error rate, ahead of Mistral's Voxtral Small at 2.77 percent and Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe at 2.81 percent. The accuracy gap to the previous leader is 0.49 points by our subtraction, and SOV-Transcribe-1 reaches it with 4.6 billion active parameters against Voxtral Small's roughly 24 billion. The catch sits in the model card: about 53 GB of BF16 weights, non-streaming transcription of recordings up to 30 minutes, and seven languages. Lower is better. Rankings and inclusion are as of November 12, 2026, when Salesforce published SOV-Transcribe-1 under the Apache 2.0 license; the board then ranked it first among open-weights models and seventh overall. Deployment facts come from the Salesforce model card: 26.6 billion total parameters with 4.6 billion active including the audio encoder, a Whisper large-v3 encoder with a Gemma 4 26B A4B decoder, testing on audio up to 30 minutes, and seven supported languages. Streaming and recorded-audio results measure different tasks and are not merged in this scorecard. The gap row is our subtraction of published AA-WER figures.</p><h2>What the scorecard shows</h2><p>Three findings follow. First, the October tie is gone: the previous board separated Voxtral Small and Nemotron 3.5 by four hundredths of a point, inside rounding; the November board separates first from second by 0.49 points, which is a real accuracy lead on this test. Second, accuracy per active parameter moved sharply: SOV-Transcribe-1 beats Nemotron 3.5 at a similar active size (2.28 against 2.81 percent) and Voxtral Small with about six times fewer active parameters, per Salesforce's comparison of the board figures. Third, the hardware bill did not shrink with the active count: 26.6 billion total parameters at BF16 is about 53 GB of weights before runtime overhead, so the most accurate open model is also the heaviest of the three to serve, and its non-streaming design excludes live captioning and real-time agents.</p><h2>What the scorecard does not establish</h2><p>Analysis, labeled as analysis: AA-WER measures recorded speech in three datasets; it does not establish accuracy on accented call-center audio, noisy field recordings or languages outside the model card's seven. Salesforce reports 8.21 percent average word error rate on AppTek call-center dialogues across 14 English accents, a reminder that the 2.28 percent headline does not travel to harder audio. The scorecard also says nothing about price per minute, latency or diarization quality. The buying rule the data supports is conditional: for recorded audio in supported languages where a 53 GB serving footprint is acceptable, SOV-Transcribe-1 is the open accuracy leader as of November 12, 2026; for streaming or small-footprint deployment, the board does not pick a winner.</p><p>One further check belongs in any citation of this scorecard: the board is a moving target. Artificial Analysis updates AA-WER as models are added, and the November 12, 2026 reading already differs from the October board this article originally used, which listed 62 models with 15 open-weights entries. Any reuse of these figures should carry the board date, because a ranking without a date will silently expire.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
