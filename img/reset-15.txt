<p>ByteHerald re-ran the November ARC-AGI-3 verified results at matched reasoning effort, and the harness gap survives the correction. Across seven matched configurations from ARC Prize results dated November 9 to November 17, 2026, the Provider Adapter score exceeded the Standard harness score by 16.1 to 43.5 percentage points. The finding is not that one model is best. It is that a single ARC-AGI-3 number is incomplete unless the harness and effort travel with it.</p>

<p>Methodology: we used only ARC Prize verified semi-private ARC-AGI-3 rows where the same model and same reasoning effort had both a Standard harness score and a Provider Adapter score. <h2>What the scorecard establishes</h2>
<p>The Standard harness leader in this matched set is Claude Fable 5.2 Xhigh at 81.2 percent, from ARC Prize results dated November 9, 2026. The Provider Adapter leader is GPT-6.2 Astra XHigh at 100.0 percent, from ARC Prize results dated November 17, 2026. Those are different winners under different context-carrying rules, so a ranking that mixes harnesses is not a model ranking. It is a ranking of model plus test rig.</p>
<p>Effort also does not move scores in a clean staircase. GPT-6.2 Astra scored 56.5 percent on the Standard harness at XHigh and 58.0 percent at High in ARC Prize results dated November 17, 2026. More reasoning effort produced a lower Standard score in that pair, while the Provider Adapter score stayed near the ceiling. Any claim that higher effort simply means higher ARC-AGI-3 performance fails on those rows.</p>
<h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: the scorecard does not prove the Provider Adapter is cheating, and it does not prove the Standard harness is the only fair test. ARC Prize reports the harnesses separately because they measure different context-management setups. The adapter can be a legitimate product-like condition. The problem begins when a score is quoted without its harness, effort, date, and verification status.</p>
<p>The scorecard also does not settle general intelligence. ARC-AGI-3 is an interactive benchmark with specific games, actions, and scoring. A harness gap inside that benchmark is evidence about measurement sensitivity, not a complete theory of model capability.</p>
<h2>The citable rule</h2>
<p>Our matched-effort table gives readers a rule they can apply to the next leaderboard: demand model, effort, harness, date, and verifier before comparing two percentages. If any one of those five fields is missing, the score is marketing material until proven otherwise.</p>
<p>The cost dimension reinforces the same caution. ARC Prize reported GPT-6.2 Astra at $24.2K for the Standard harness headline result and $4.1K for the Provider Adapter headline result on November 17, 2026. Those totals are evaluation costs, not per-task prices, and they come from different effort settings in the headline comparison.
