<p>Starting in December 2026, ChatGPT and Codex will share one usage allowance for Plus, Pro, and Business subscribers, measured in tokens, according to OpenAI's Help Center article on ChatGPT and Codex usage limits, updated October 31, 2026. Instant chat will not draw from that allowance. The change turns two budgets into one, and for subscribers who code heavily in Codex, it means an afternoon of agent work now competes directly with every question asked in ChatGPT.</p>

<h2>What changes in December, exactly as OpenAI states it</h2>
<p>The Help Center sentence is short, and its limits are part of the story: "Starting in December, Plus, Pro, and Business plans' usage allowance will be shared across ChatGPT and Codex as we bring the agentic capabilities of Work into the main ChatGPT experience." Usage will be measured in tokens, the units a model reads and writes, and more advanced models and harder tasks generally use more of the allowance. Combined remaining usage and reset times will appear in Settings, under Usage. When a subscriber hits the limit, the documented options are to wait for the reset, upgrade the plan, or buy credits for eligible features. OpenAI says it expects most users will not need to wait for a reset more often than they do now, which is a forecast about typical use, not a guarantee about heavy use.</p>
<p>What OpenAI has not published is just as important for planning. The article gives no exact December date, no token allowance for any plan, and no reset cadence. That means no subscriber can calculate, today, how many Codex sessions their December allowance covers, and no reviewer can verify a plan's size against a published number. The only reliable planning input before December is a subscriber's own measured burn: how much Codex work they do in a week, and how much chat work sits alongside it.</p>

<h2>How December follows the October reversal</h2>
<p>December's merge only makes sense against what happened on October 29, 2026. OpenAI had emailed Pro 200 subscribers on September 29, 2026, saying their included allowance would be reduced after October 29, with the $200 monthly price unchanged. After subscriber backlash, the company reversed course on the day the cut was due: it doubled the shared Work and Codex allowance for Pro 100 and Pro 500 plans, cancelled the Pro 200 reduction, and levelled Pro 200 subscriptions to the same allowance. Thibault Sottiaux, who speaks for ChatGPT and Codex for OpenAI, wrote that limits were doubling for all Pro 100, 200, and 500 tiers and Business Premium, effective that day, and that OpenAI had heard subscribers' feedback and would not reduce Pro 200 limits. Dots usage stayed separate from default usage.</p>
<p>Read together, the two moves tell a coherent story, and it is worth stating as a reading rather than an OpenAI claim. The October doubling gave Pro subscribers more headroom, and the December merge then spends that headroom from a single pool. A subscriber who welcomed the doubling as extra capacity should not assume it stays extra: if Codex and chat previously drew on separate budgets, merging them means the doubled allowance is now shared across both jobs instead of added to either one alone. OpenAI has not said the merge reduces anyone's total, and it has not published numbers that would let anyone prove otherwise, which is exactly why the December wording deserves a careful read rather than a celebratory one.</p>

<h2>What to do before December</h2>
<p>First, measure. A subscriber who runs Codex daily and chats daily should watch their current usage across a normal week and note which job dominates, because the December pool punishes the heavier job first. Second, route trivial prompts to Instant chat where the answer allows it, since Instant does not draw from the allowance, and keep advanced-model chat for questions that need it. Third, expect a rollout, not a switch flip: OpenAI plans to test the unified Chat and Work experience on the desktop app with a small group of users before expanding it widely, and Codex remains a separate desktop application view, so the interface change and the billing change may not arrive on the same day for every subscriber.</p>
<p>One more November change belongs in the same planning picture. OpenAI's Help Center now states limits on how many paid accounts can share one payment card or device, across its Go, Plus, and Pro tiers, and it publishes no thresholds for either limit. That is a separate control from the December merge, but it lands on the same subscribers: teams sharing cards or devices across accounts should check their own setup rather than assuming a shared arrangement still passes.</p>
<p>The pricing history behind all of this is why subscribers distrust limit announcements in the first place. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/gpt-6-vs-claude-vs-gemini-october-2026.html">Our October 2026 price tracker across 10 frontier models</a> shows how often list prices and allowances moved in a single month, and the lesson carries over: treat every announced multiplier as provisional until the Help Center states a number. And the shared pool matters most for agent work, which is also where spending surprises live: <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/ai-agents-in-production-what-breaks-and.html">our study of what breaks when agents run in production</a> found that agent runs, not chat messages, are the cost that escapes budgets, and December makes that escape route draw on the same pool as ordinary chat.</p>
