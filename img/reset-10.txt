<p>ByteHerald's impairment ledger joins three primary records that are usually quoted separately. Gartner's October 14, 2026 arithmetic says data-center owners need $8.5 trillion of cumulative revenue through 2030 to cover planned spending, financing costs and a 12 percent return, against at most $3.6 trillion of attributable AI revenue, a gap of $4.9 trillion by our subtraction. Gartner's November 12, 2026 forecast then raised the spending side: worldwide AI spending of $4,050 billion in 2027, with infrastructure at $2,397 billion. The Bank for International Settlements measured how the buildout is financed: $222 billion of gross bond issuance by five hyperscalers through September 2026 and $1.2 trillion of data centre lease commitments by mid-2026. The November 12, 2026 rows are Gartner's revised worldwide spending forecast by market. The financing rows are as stated in BIS Bulletin No 140 (October 27, 2026): gross bond issuance by Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle, and Moody's Ratings lease-commitment figures cited there. The gap is simple subtraction of Gartner's two October figures. The three records have different scopes (a hurdle model, a spending forecast, and financing flows) and are placed in one ledger only to test whether the spending path and the financing path are consistent with the revenue hurdle.</p><h2>What the ledger shows</h2><p>Three findings follow. First, the November forecast moved the spending side up, not down: infrastructure alone is forecast at $2,397 billion in 2027, and AI-optimized servers nearly double from $527.6 billion to $957.2 billion, partly on memory-price inflation inside server prices. Second, the revenue side of the hurdle is structurally mismatched: Gartner notes that most AI-native providers are asset-light and predicts less than half of AI revenue will be earned by providers owning significant infrastructure, so the revenue and the assets sit in different hands. Third, the financing rows show the buildout increasingly carried by obligations with fixed payment schedules: $222 billion of bonds issued by September 2026 and $1.2 trillion of lease commitments, about $820 billion of them not yet started, fall due whether or not forecast revenue arrives.</p><h2>What the ledger does not establish</h2><p>Analysis, labeled as analysis: the ledger does not prove impairment will occur. Gartner's hurdle depends on its revenue ceiling and its 12 percent return assumption; faster AI revenue growth, longer asset lives or lower financing costs would narrow the gap, and Gartner itself does not expect the 12 percent return before the end of 2032 at the earliest. The BIS bulletin also reports that spillovers to bond markets on AI-financing news days in 2026 were small and short-lived, which cuts against an imminent financing-stress reading. What the ledger establishes is narrower: as of the November 12, 2026 forecast, planned spending rose while the only published revenue ceiling against it remained the October figure, and a growing share of the spending is debt and leases. The impairment question will be settled by attributable revenue against the $8.5 trillion hurdle, not by spending announcements.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
