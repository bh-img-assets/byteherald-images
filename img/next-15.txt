<p>ByteHerald finding: Microsoft shipped the containment layer three days before its chief executive described the architecture. Our ledger maps Satya Nadella seven trust principles of October 10, 2026 against the Microsoft Execution Containers release of October 7, 2026. One principle is documented product. The rest are partial or principle only.</p>
<p>Nadella essay, titled Models as Insider Risks in the Super Intelligence Era, argues that companies should assume a model is compromised and contain it from the start, separate the supply of intelligence from authority over it, and keep an authorized person able to pause or shut down a model mid-task. The question our ledger answers is what an enterprise could actually buy or deploy in October 2026.</p>

<p>Methodology: we graded each principle against Microsoft own October 2026 documents only. Documented means the control is described as shipping. Partial means a mechanism exists with stated limits. Principle only means the essay asserts the requirement without naming a shipped control. We did not grade other vendors products, and we did not treat roadmap statements as shipped capability.</p>
<h2>What the ledger shows</h2>
<p>Analysis: containment is real. MXC lets a developer or administrator declare what an agent can reach, and the operating system enforces that boundary at runtime, so neither the agent nor code it generates can grant itself more access. That is the load bearing part of Nadella argument, and it shipped first. The essay arrived after the product, which reverses the usual order of framework then implementation.</p>
<p>The gaps sit exactly where enterprises will feel them. Organization wide management through Intune was described as coming soon without a date, so in October 2026 the boundary was written by the agent developer more than by the deploying organization. Observability is mode dependent: enforcement mode blocks ungranted access without producing a report, while learning and permissive modes record activity, and activity reporting is limited to Windows process containers. Nadella principle asks for tamper proof evidence of every meaningful action. A mode that enforces silently does not meet that bar on its own.</p>
<h2>The brake itself</h2>
<p>The emergency brake, a human pause or shutdown mid-task, is stated as a requirement in the essay. The October documents we compiled do not name a shipped kill switch product, a certification, or a contract term that guarantees one. Analysis: containment limits what an agent can touch, but it is not the same as a tested interruption path with defined recovery after a pause. Enterprises reading the essay as a procurement checklist would find the fence documented and the brake still a design obligation.</p>
<p>The remaining principles, model diversity, continuous verifiability, independent auditability and incident disclosure, appear in the essay without corresponding shipped controls in the October documents. That does not make them wrong. It makes them requirements the industry has not yet productized, including, on this record, the company proposing them.</p>
<h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2>
<p>This ledger does not establish that MXC stops a determined attacker, and Microsoft own backend list includes experimental isolation options alongside generally available ones. It does not measure how many enterprises deployed MXC in October 2026. It establishes a narrower, citable point: on Microsoft own documents, containment shipped, management and full observability were partial, and the emergency brake remained a principle in search of a product.</p>
