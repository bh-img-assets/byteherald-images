<p>ByteHerald's allowance ledger finds that OpenAI has published the structure of its December metering change without publishing its size. Between October 16 and November 26, 2026, OpenAI launched parallel agent crews, doubled some Pro allowances, announced one shared token-measured allowance across ChatGPT and Codex, and corrected a billing error that made limits arrive up to 40 percent sooner. At no point in that record did it publish a numeric cap on parallel agents, per-plan token allowances, or the weighting that converts a task into allowance use.</p>

<h2>What the ledger shows</h2>
<p>The October 16, 2026 upgrade let multiple agents split one task in parallel for Pro users, with visible progress and reviewable work, but attached no benchmark to its speed claim and no cap to its agent count. The October 29, 2026 notes doubled Work and Codex allowances for Pro 100 and Pro 500 and reversed an announced Pro 200 cut. The November 24, 2026 guidance then merged the pools: from December 2026, Plus, Pro and Business share one token-measured allowance across ChatGPT and Codex, ordinary Instant chat does not draw from it, and combined remaining usage appears in Settings. The November 26, 2026 billing fix showed the meter already binds: correcting undercounted GPT-6.2 usage made limits arrive about 40 percent sooner on Astra and 25 percent sooner on Sol, with one banked reset added to Go, Plus, Pro and Business accounts.</p>
<h2>What this does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: parallel agents no longer spend from a separate pool, so a crew's cost now competes directly with the same user's chat and coding work. The ledger cannot say how much work a plan buys, because OpenAI states only that use depends on the model and the task. Until the token weighting is published, the shared meter is a structure without a size, and any precise task count claimed elsewhere is unsupported by OpenAI's documents.</p>
<h2>Why the undisclosed column matters</h2><p>A token-measured allowance is only plannable if the conversion is known. OpenAI's November 24, 2026 guidance says use depends on the model and the task and that more advanced models and complex tasks generally use more, but it publishes no weighting for cached input, fresh input and output, and no per-plan token totals. The November 26, 2026 billing correction proves the weighting is material: fixing an undercount moved real limits by 25 to 40 percent overnight. For agent crews the stakes are higher, because parallel agents multiply token use per task while drawing on the same pool as chat. Until OpenAI publishes the weighting, the honest planning unit is the Settings usage screen after the fact, not a budget set before it. That is a disclosure gap, not a pricing dispute, and it is the gap this ledger tracks.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
