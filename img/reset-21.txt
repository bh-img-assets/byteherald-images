The Intelligence Index ranks current models on today's tasks; the Progress Index places models released years apart on one capability ruler. Analysis: the November board shows the ruler working as designed for ordering, because a November 2026 model and the 320 anchor can be compared on one scale even though the underlying evaluations changed. It does not show that a 3.5-point gap between neighboring models is a measured capability difference, because Artificial Analysis publishes no uncertainty estimates for these scores, a gap noted in coverage on October 28, 2026.</p><p>What the evidence does not establish: that the top-ranked configuration is the best choice for any specific workload, that scores will hold after the next refit (refits can move every score at once), or that effort settings cost the same across labs. The citable unit is model plus effort plus date, not the model name alone.</p><h2>How the re-run was built</h2><p>The re-run takes the published board as its dataset and adds two computations: points above the 320 anchor, and within-model effort spread. Both are subtractions on published scores, stated so any reader can reproduce them from the same board. The re-run does not re-estimate the index, does not merge it with the Intelligence Index, and does not treat score gaps as statistically significant, because the publisher releases no uncertainty estimates.</p><p>Analysis: the effort-spread finding is the citable part. Benchmark coverage usually reports the maximum-effort score as the model's score. On this board, the same model's low-effort configuration sits 14.6 points lower, below configurations from two other labs. A procurement decision, a research comparison or a news claim that cites 359.1 without the effort setting is citing a configuration most deployments will never run. The ledger format, model plus effort plus date, is the minimum honest citation.</p><p>The board's size matters for how much weight the top rows can bear. With 697 configurations published, the top of the board is a dense region where several labs and several effort settings of the same model sit within a few points.
