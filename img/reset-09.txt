<p>ByteHerald's expectations-versus-outcomes ledger sets what Anthropic's employees predicted in October 2026 against what employers reported doing in November 2026. The prediction side is extreme: in Anthropic's October 16, 2026 survey, most of 1,334 staff expected AI to handle at least 80 percent of their jobs by 2030, against about 5 percent of 10,980 US adults, and staff forecast US unemployment above 12 percent. The outcome side is quieter: in Anthropic's November 23, 2026 employer study, 39 of 58 companies reported hiring less because of AI, while only 2 reported a job eliminated by automation. The October 16, 2026 expectations survey polled 1,334 Anthropic employees (unweighted, August 9 to 12, 2026) and 10,980 US adults through Morning Consult (weighted, August 11 to 23, 2026). The November 23, 2026 outcome study rests on 66 face-to-face interviews at 58 US companies that use Claude, chosen for range across industry and size; its authors state the companies are Anthropic customers, not a representative sample of the US economy, and that value claims are qualitative interview accounts rather than A/B tests. Our percentages divide each company count by 58. A company can appear in more than one outcome row, so the rows do not sum to 100 percent.</p><h2>What the ledger shows</h2><p>Three findings follow. First, the measured effect so far is the hire that never happens: hiring less (67.2 percent of companies) outnumbers job elimination (3.4 percent) by roughly twenty to one in this sample, and about a third of the hiring reductions came from attrition without backfills. Second, the displacement that did occur concentrated in contracted and offshore work: 9 companies cut contracted developers, support, legal or back-office staff, many already offshore. Third, the effect is not one-directional: 10 companies hired more because of AI, mostly into implementation and IT support, and six startups estimated they would need about four times as many employees without AI, an interviewee estimate rather than a measurement.</p><h2>What the ledger does not establish</h2><p>Analysis, labeled as analysis: the ledger does not refute the staff forecast, which concerns 2030 and rests on access to unreleased models; it shows that the 2026 mechanism is hiring restraint, not layoffs. It also cannot generalize beyond Claude customers: a vendor studying its own customers will over-sample successful deployments, and companies where AI failed are less likely to be in the frame. What the ledger does establish is the correct early-warning indicator. If displacement follows the pattern in these 58 companies, it will appear first in vacancy postings, contractor budgets and entry-level hiring, not in layoff headlines, and the staff forecast of unemployment above 12 percent would need that quiet mechanism to scale far beyond this sample.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
