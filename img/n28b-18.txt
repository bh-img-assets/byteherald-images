<p>ByteHerald's segment ledger, computed from SpaceX's October 20, 2026 earnings release, separates the headline from the profit. Super Intelligence produced 51.9 percent of third-quarter revenue and 5.7 percent operating margin. Connectivity, the Starlink business, produced 36.0 percent of revenue at a 45.1 percent margin and supplied $2,367 million of the $2,791 million in operating income. The AI segment is the growth engine. The satellite segment is the profit engine.</p><p>SpaceX reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $14,575 million, up 161 percent from $5,576 million a year earlier, with net income of $4.4 billion against a $1.1 billion loss in the year-ago quarter. Adjusted EBITDA was $8,809 million, up 306 percent. Super Intelligence revenue reached $7,560 million, up from $813 million a year earlier and $2,561 million in the second quarter, driven by cloud services agreements, the Cursor acquisition, and the company's models and software including Grok. Nameplate compute reached 1.7 GW as of September 30, 2026.</p><p>The ledger below divides each segment's operating income by its revenue. Super Intelligence earned $431 million on $7,560 million. The release attributes Super Intelligence growth to cloud agreements and an acquisition rather than to model sales alone, and the margin gap shows where the cash to fund compute buildout is generated. Whether cloud tenants renew at current rates is the open question the release does not answer.</p><p>The quarter also carried operational milestones the ledger does not price. Starship Flight 14 delivered 26 Starlink V3 satellites, which SpaceX describes as roughly ten times the downlink capacity added by a Falcon 9 Starlink launch, and management outlined plans to attempt recovery of both Starship stages on Flight 15 with second-stage reflight targeted for early 2027. After the quarter closed, SpaceX agreed to buy up to 14 MHz of nationwide 800 MHz spectrum for Starlink Mobile, with closing expected in the first half of 2027. Third-quarter capital expenditures reached $19.6 billion across compute infrastructure, Starship development, and satellite production.</p><p>What to watch is the Super Intelligence margin as nameplate compute grows past 1.7 GW. If cloud agreement revenue scales faster than the depreciation on $16.4 billion of quarterly capex, the segment margin expands. If tenants build their own capacity, the landlord model reprices.</p><p>The earnings call added one forward item the ledger records without pricing it. Management said a merger between SpaceX and Tesla is a good idea and confirmed both companies have formed special committees to discuss it. Any deal would need shareholder approval, with a high bar on the Tesla side.
