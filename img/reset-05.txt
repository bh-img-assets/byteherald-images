<p>ByteHerald's control ledger finds that Nvidia's leadership question was settled on paper within eleven days while the control question underneath the Hugging Face deal did not move at all. Nvidia's Form 8-K filed November 12, 2026 records the board accepting Jen-Hsun Huang's resignation effective November 11, and its Form 8-K filed November 23, 2026 records his unanimous reinstatement effective November 22, with the independent-director investigation still to proceed to conclusion. Neither filing mentions the Hugging Face transaction, whose $12.93 billion price and first-half-2027 closing window remain as announced on September 3, 2026.</p>

<h2>What the ledger shows</h2>
<p>The deal track is unchanged since October: a $12.93 billion purchase, about $150 million in annualized Hugging Face revenue as reported, a formal Justice Department and Federal Trade Commission review period before completion, and closing expected in the first half of 2027. The leadership track reversed twice: resignation effective November 11, 2026 with Colette Kress as interim chief executive and Harvey C. Jones as chair; a November 17, 2026 earnings-call statement that the board did not intend to reinstate Huang; then unanimous reinstatement effective November 22, 2026. The November 23, 2026 filing adds governance machinery, a National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors, while stating the investigation into reporting that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort will proceed to conclusion.</p>
<h2>What this does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: reinstatement is not exoneration, and the filings do not claim it is. The ledger supports one conclusion: control of Nvidia itself was contested and restored within eleven days, while the distribution-point question the Justice Department is reported to be asking about Hugging Face remains open on its own track. The leadership question now reads as settled unless the committee's findings say otherwise.</p>
<h2>The layers underneath the deal</h2><p>The control question is about layers, not logos. Hugging Face is a distribution point: models, datasets and tooling pass through it to developers who may run them on any hardware. Nvidia controls the accelerator layer those workloads usually run on. A single owner across both layers could, in principle, shape defaults, discovery and terms at the point where open models meet their users, which is the capture concern Reuters reported the Justice Department asking about on October 28, 2026. Nvidia's September 3, 2026 statement that the platform would remain open to third-party processors, frameworks and cloud services addresses conduct after closing, not structure before it. That distinction is why the review period matters: before completion, regulators can condition or block; after completion, the remedy left is conduct enforcement, which is slower and harder.
