<p>ByteHerald built a dated disclosure ledger from the Fireworks bulletins and Notion's advisory, and the ledger shows the breach widening in two steps, not one. The first bulletin said no customer models, datasets, or training data had been touched. Five weeks later, the updated bulletin confirmed billing data, secrets, datasets, and incomplete model weights. The widening, and the four days between Notion disconnecting and learning customer data was involved, is the story.</p><h2>The disclosure ledger</h2><p>Fireworks AI told customers on November 9, 2026, that the October intrusion reached further than first disclosed. The first used internal Fireworks credentials and exposed secrets stored for evaluation jobs. The second entered through a third-party cloud provider that hosted Fireworks research machines, then reached development systems. Treating them as one incident understates the problem: the second path bypassed Fireworks' own perimeter entirely.</p><h2>What changed between the bulletins</h2><p>The October 9 bulletin stated that Fireworks had not detected unauthorized access to customer models, datasets, training data, or inference traffic. The November 9 update replaced that position: account and billing information for affected accounts, and for a limited set of accounts, secrets, datasets, and incomplete model weights. Fireworks says payment card numbers are not stored and were not impacted, and that its inference, Training SDK, and Nexus services, which are zero data retention by default, showed no impact. Fireworks has not published a count of affected customers, so no count can be reported.</p><p>Notion's side of the ledger is narrower and better documented. Fewer than 1 percent of Notion workspaces were affected, and the data was Notion AI chat transcripts used for the Insights feature. Notion states the transcripts held model inputs and outputs but no tool call results, and that in most affected workspaces the chats were not labeled with author or workspace. Notion disconnected on November 3, deleted its Fireworks data on November 4, and disabled Insights while the investigation continues.</p><h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2><p>Analysis: the ledger establishes sequence and scope as the companies state them. It does not establish how data covered by zero-retention commitments came to be held in a dataset, and neither company has explained that path. Fireworks' November 17 bulletin says forensic work on the first incident is complete while the second investigation continues, so the final scope is not yet fixed. Customers should treat the November 9 categories as the current floor, not the ceiling, until the second investigation closes.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
