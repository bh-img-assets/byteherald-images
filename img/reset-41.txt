<p>ByteHerald's terms-status ledger finds the liability clause is paused, not withdrawn, and the older responsibility language never left. Meta's updated Commercial Terms were first due to take effect on October 30, 2026. Meta moved the date to November 20, 2026 on October 23, 2026. On November 5, 2026, Meta's Business Help Center stated that the updates are paused and will not take effect on November 20, 2026, with existing terms remaining in effect. The pause covers four documents: the Commercial Terms, the Product Catalog Terms, the Self-Serve Ad Terms and the Ad Creative Generative AI Terms.</p>
<p>The asset is a status ledger for advertisers and publishers who need to know which text governs today. It separates three states that coverage often merges: proposed language, paused language, and language already in force. The proposed AI Features section would make businesses solely responsible for risks from Meta's AI features and resulting content. The terms already in effect tie responsibility to the advertiser's own use: an advertiser that uses or posts AI-generated content is responsible for determining whether the output is correct and suitable for public display.</p>

<p>Methodology: ByteHerald built the ledger from dated events and recorded the governing text after each event, rather than treating the pause as a single headline. Where a date comes from reporting rather than a Meta or regulator document, the row says so. The ledger does not give legal advice and does not predict whether a revised proposal returns. It records status: proposed, paused, in force, under investigation.</p>
<p>The practical distinction is narrow and important. The paused AI Features section would assign responsibility for AI features and resulting content to the business, including, on the reported reading, features Meta enables. The Ad Creative Generative AI Terms already in effect assign responsibility when the advertiser uses or posts the output. Those are different triggers. The first follows the feature. The second follows the advertiser's act of use or publication. An advertiser auditing its workflow today should map which of its AI uses are chosen, which are default-on, and who reviews output before it runs.</p>
<p>What this does not establish: the pause does not withdraw the proposal, and Meta can return with revised text after consultation. The CMA investigation does not establish wrongdoing; the authority opened an investigation and made no finding. The Norwegian suspension does not settle the terms in other jurisdictions. And the ledger cannot tell any individual advertiser its legal exposure, which depends on its contracts, jurisdiction and actual use of Meta's AI features.</p>
<p>Analysis: the citable ByteHerald contribution is the status separation. As of the November 5, 2026 pause, the October 30 and November 20 deadlines are gone, the four updated documents are not in force, the existing terms govern, and the regulator track runs to January 2027. Any account that says advertisers accepted the new liability clause in October or November 2026 is wrong on the dates.</p>
