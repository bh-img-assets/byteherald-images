The formalization rows keep two different measures separate: the README's percentage of top-line results, a term the repository does not define, and a count of catalogue entries with a formalized main result. Lean formalization means a proof expressed in a form the Lean kernel can check step by step; it verifies that a proof follows from its stated assumptions, not that the formal statement matches the theorem readers assume, and not that the result is new. Confirmation status for the October 26, 2026 claims (a Kakeya extension, an Exponential Time Hypothesis refutation and a Yang-Mills result) is taken from reporting dated November 11, 2026, which described external shepherd reviewers, a Yang-Mills working group, and no independent confirmation of the three claims at that date.</p><h2>What the ledger shows</h2><p>Three findings follow. First, the release was raw research data by its publisher's own description, and its error rate was visible immediately: a sign error invalidated one argument within 48 hours and two dependent manuscripts fell with it. Second, the checkable share is smaller than the headline share under any catalogue-based count: 162 formalized main results against a catalogue of more than 700 manuscripts is roughly one in five, while the README's 42 percent uses an undefined top-line denominator. Third, the institutional response changed process before it changed verdicts: the October 15, 2026 funding announcement and the October 26, 2026 release practice, announcing named external reviewers alongside claims, both postdate the revolt, while none of the October 26 claims had independent confirmation in the November 11, 2026 reporting.</p><h2>What the ledger does not establish</h2><p>Analysis, labeled as analysis: the ledger does not show that the manuscripts are wrong; most have simply not been independently checked, and absence of confirmation is not refutation. It also does not measure novelty, which was the community's sharpest complaint: researchers catalogued cases where humans had already proved the same results, a defect no formalization percentage can detect. What the ledger does establish is the correct unit of account for AI mathematics claims in 2026: not manuscripts published, but results formalized, statements matched to the claimed theorem, and confirmations by people outside the releasing lab.
