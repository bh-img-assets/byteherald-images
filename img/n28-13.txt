Meta's own chart puts Ember at 35.8 and Haiku at 36.2 unrounded, so Haiku holds a small capability edge that rounding hides. Ember's list prices are $0.10 per million input tokens, $0.004 per million cached input tokens, and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 200,000 input tokens, per Meta's launch pricing. Above 200,000 input tokens every rate triples, to $0.30 input, $0.012 cached, and $1.50 output. Haiku's long-prompt rates rise earlier, above 100,000 tokens. Long-document workloads therefore need their own arithmetic; the short-prompt ledger does not transfer unchanged.</p><p>Why does Ember cost less per task at the same list price? Artificial Analysis attributes the saving to two factors: cached input, which it calls most of the cost in its agentic evaluations, is about 60 percent cheaper for Ember, and Ember uses fewer output tokens to finish the same tasks. That mechanism matters more than the headline, because it means the saving scales with how much of a buyer's workload is repeated context and verbose output.</p><h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2><p>Analysis: the ledger ranks models on one index and one task-cost measure. Cost per task also moves with reasoning settings and prompt length, so a buyer with long prompts or heavy tool use should rerun the division on their own traffic before switching.
