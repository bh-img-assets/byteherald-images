<p>ByteHerald's discipline-gap ledger shows the enterprise AI problem as a stopping-rule shortage, not only a model shortage. In Gartner's survey of 2,002 CIOs and technology executives, only 13 percent said their enterprise had realized significant or transformational value from AI. The practices associated with Gartner's vanguard group are much rarer than the spending pressure around them: 31 percent reported pilot stopping guidelines, 17 percent reported production abandonment guidelines, and 4 percent reported explicit plans to redesign roles for a human-agent workforce.</p>

<h2>Analysis</h2>
<p>Analysis: the sharpest gap is workforce redesign. Only 4 percent of CIOs reported explicit plans to redesign roles for a human-agent workforce, while organizations with those plans were 2.4 times more likely to be vanguards in Gartner's reporting. That combination is more informative than the headline value figure alone. It suggests the scarce asset is not access to models. It is an operating model that states which tasks move to agents, which tasks remain human, who owns exceptions, and how headcount demand changes when production cost falls.</p>
<p>The stopping-rule rows show a second pattern. Pilot gates are more common than production kill rules by 14 percentage points, using ByteHerald arithmetic from 31 percent and 17 percent. That ordering matters because pilots are easier to stop before users, budgets, and workflows depend on them. A production system creates switching costs and political protection. Gartner's reported association is stronger for production abandonment guidelines, at 2.6 times, than for pilot guidelines, at 2.3 times. The ledger therefore points to the harder discipline as the rarer one.</p>
<p>Cost pressure makes the gap harder to close. Gartner reported that 58 percent of CIOs had increased pressure to deliver cost improvement from AI, while 51 percent expected AI to increase total cost of ownership. Those two figures can coexist because AI spending can rise while finance still demands savings elsewhere. The risk is a portfolio that expands use cases faster than it writes exit criteria. Gartner also reported that the likelihood of use case success remains one in five, which is 20 percent when expressed as a percentage. That success rate should be read beside the 13 percent value figure, not as a substitute for it.</p>
<h2>What the evidence does not establish</h2>
<p>The evidence does not establish that stopping rules cause better outcomes, that vanguard status can be bought by copying a checklist, or that the survey percentages describe any single company. It also does not publish the full questionnaire, response weighting, industry mix, or company-size mix in the press release. The ledger is therefore a governance signal from a large CIO survey, not an audit of enterprise AI returns.</p>
<p>What to watch next is whether later Gartner releases report movement in the same three practices: pilot gates, production abandonment rules, and human-agent workforce redesign. If value realization rises while those practices stay rare, the explanation will need to come from somewhere else. If the practices spread and value remains flat, the association should be treated more cautiously.</p>
