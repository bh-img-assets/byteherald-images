<p>ByteHerald's concentration analysis of Censys scan data finds that the exposed ARTEX footprint is far more concentrated than the headline count suggests. The top three countries hold 88.1% of the 1,465 exposed hosts Censys observed as of November 4, 2026, and two hosting networks alone account for 44.4%. The widely quoted figure of 815 distinct deployments is what remains after those two hosting blocks are removed, which means a small number of infrastructure decisions created most of the exposure.</p>

<h2>What the concentration shows</h2>
<p>Geography is lopsided. The United States holds 726 hosts, Hong Kong 387, and China 177, which sums to 1,290 of 1,465 hosts, or 88.1%. Japan, Singapore, and South Korea together add only 110 hosts. Hosting is equally concentrated: PEG Tech accounts for 493 hosts and Yancy Limited for 157, and their combined 650 matches the two identically configured hosting blocks Censys identified. Removing those blocks leaves roughly 815 distinct-looking deployments, the figure that best represents individual operators.</p>
<p>Risk markers overlap on a minority of hosts. Censys found 155 hosts, or 10.6%, also running another threat detection, and 32 hosts exposing open directories. Seventy hosts run CyberStrikeAI alongside ARTEX, and 43 run LLM gateway or reseller software, which suggests some operators are stacking multiple AI offensive tools on the same machines. The most common console port, 8787, shows 1,931 endpoints, more than the host count, because single hosts can expose the console on several addresses.</p>
<p>Context from the same report matters for severity. Censys cites CrowdStrike reporting of October 7, 2026, and Microsoft reporting of October 21, 2026, including a Microsoft summary stating that Storm-0940 attempted sign-ins to more than 2,250 tenants, peaking at 2,900 attempts per minute. An exposed console for an AI hacking tool is therefore not a theoretical risk; it is infrastructure pointed at identity systems at scale.</p>
<h2>Methodology and limits</h2>
<p>Methodology: we used the host counts Censys published from its November 4, 2026 observation and computed shares against the 1,465-host denominator. Limits: internet-wide scans see only hosts that respond on scanned ports, so the true deployment count is higher. Hosting-block hosts may be resold or shared, so host counts overstate distinct operators, which is why Censys separates the 815 distinct-looking deployments. Country attribution follows IP geolocation and can misplace cloud-hosted machines. The evidence does not establish how many consoles were actively used for attacks.</p>
<h2>Analysis</h2>
<p>Analysis: concentration cuts both ways. Defenders can prioritize two hosting networks and three countries to cover most of the visible footprint, but operators can also relocate quickly once a block is burned. The 10.6% overlap with other threat detections is the figure to watch: if that share rises, ARTEX is moving from experimentation into layered toolkits.</p>
