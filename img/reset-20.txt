<p>ByteHerald's ledger of the two documented Cairn campaigns shows they should not be merged into one story. The volume campaign converted cheap tokens into persistence against weakly defended sites, at roughly $50 per breach by NorForge's estimate. The zero-day campaign converted agent exploration into a novel flaw in a specific product, which Amazon reconstructed from the operator's exposed Cairn panel. Amazon attributed the second campaign to a China-nexus actor with moderate confidence; no public record attributes the first campaign to the same operator.</p><p>What the evidence does not establish: how much data either campaign took, whether the named companies in the volume campaign lost sensitive data (Deere said no sensitive data was accessed, as Reuters reported), or whether the 11-minute figure Amazon reported for reaching file writes would reproduce outside that one deployment. The defensible claim is narrower: one framework has now been documented in two distinct operations, and patch status, not attacker sophistication, decided the second one's outcome.</p><h2>How the ledger was built</h2><p>The ledger admits a campaign only when a primary record describes it: a named research firm's findings, a vendor disclosure, a CVE record or a regulator catalog entry. Where the two campaigns share a framework name, the ledger records the framework as the connection and nothing more, because shared tooling is not shared attribution.</p><p>Analysis: the comparison also exposes a disclosure asymmetry.
