<p>ByteHerald's deployment stage ladder finds one customer-confirmed warehouse deployment with a counted workload, one contracted expansion that has not started, and one production ramp with no public unit count. That is stronger evidence than a prediction, and weaker evidence than a general robotics breakthrough. GXO and Agility Robotics announced on October 26, 2026 that Digit had moved more than 100,000 totes at GXO's Flowery Branch, Georgia facility. The same announcement said deployments at Statesville, North Carolina and Lebanon, Tennessee were expected to begin in early 2027. Tesla's Q3 2026 update on October 21, 2026 listed California Optimus as Early Ramp and Texas as Construction, with no capacity figure in the update summaries reviewed.</p><h2>The ByteHerald stage ladder</h2><p>We classified each claim by evidence stage. A completed customer workload ranks above a contracted future deployment. A contracted deployment ranks above a factory status label. More than 100,000 totes is not a fleet size, a price, or a profit figure. It does show repeated material handling in a live logistics environment, which is the kind of evidence a warehouse buyer can inspect.</p><p>The expansion row is weaker by design. Statesville and Lebanon are future deployments with an expected early 2027 start. They show customer intent after the Georgia deployment. They do not show robots operating at those sites today.</p><p>The Tesla row is a production status, not an external deployment count. Early Ramp and Construction describe factory state. They do not disclose how many Optimus units were built, what they cost, or whether outside customers received any.</p><h2>Capability is not cost parity</h2><p>Anthropic's research explains why deployment evidence remains narrow. It estimates that robots can perform 74 percent of physical tasks in the US, but mostly in limited settings, and that robots are cost-competitive for only 0.3 percent of job tasks. Analysis: those two figures separate technical possibility from economic substitution. A robot can be capable in a structured setting and still lose to human labor once purchase, integration, maintenance, oversight and reliability are priced.</p><p>Analysis: the stage ladder therefore rejects both extremes. It rejects the claim that nothing real has deployed, because GXO reports a counted workload. It also rejects the claim that a general ChatGPT moment has arrived, because the cost-competitiveness estimate remains near zero and the largest named ramp still lacks a public unit count.</p><p>How to cite this ladder: cite the more than 100,000 totes figure to the GXO and Agility Robotics announcement dated October 26, 2026, cite Statesville and Lebanon only as expected early 2027 deployments, and cite Tesla only for Early Ramp and Construction status in the October 21, 2026 update. The ladder should be revised when a customer reports a new completed workload, a contracted site begins operating, or Tesla publishes a unit count. Until one of those events occurs, forecast market size should not be placed on the deployment rungs.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
