<p>ByteHerald's filing ledger finds a split inside ServiceNow's third quarter release: reported growth stayed above 20 percent on the main lines, while the forward contract growth guide stepped down. ServiceNow reported total revenue of $4,106 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up 20.5 percent year over year, and current remaining performance obligations of $13.65 billion, also up 20.5 percent. For the fourth quarter, the company guided current remaining performance obligation growth to 18.5 percent. The gap between the reported third quarter growth rate and the fourth quarter guide is 2.0 percentage points, by our subtraction. That single gap is the cleanest filing based explanation for why a beat and a selloff could coexist, without treating market commentary as the primary evidence.</p>
<p>The method uses only the October 28, 2026 earnings release filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission. Reported figures and guidance figures are kept in separate rows because they answer different questions. Reported figures describe the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Guidance describes management expectations for the fourth quarter and full year. Analyst expectations are excluded from the asset because they are not company filings, and share price reaction is excluded from the calculation because the release does not establish market causation.</p>

<p>The reported quarter was not weak on the release's own terms. Subscription revenue reached $3,988 million, up 21 percent year over year, and total remaining performance obligations reached $29.7 billion, up 22 percent. ServiceNow said it beat the high end of guidance across third quarter topline growth and profitability metrics and raised the full year subscription revenue and free cash flow margin outlook. The free cash flow margin target rose by 100 basis points to 36 percent. Those statements are management characterizations inside the release, and the ledger records them as release statements rather than independent verification.</p>
<p>The guidance rows point in two directions. Fourth quarter subscription revenue guidance of $4,245 million to $4,255 million implies growth of 22.5 percent to 23 percent, above the third quarter subscription growth rate of 21 percent. Current remaining performance obligation growth guidance of 18.5 percent sits below the third quarter rate of 20.5 percent. Analysis: the release therefore does not show uniform deceleration. It shows faster guided subscription revenue growth alongside slower guided growth in contracted revenue due within the next 12 months. Readers who collapse those rows into one verdict miss the filing's actual tension.</p>
<p>The AI contract line is a target, not recognized revenue. ServiceNow raised its 2026 AI annual contract value target to $1.7 billion, described as up 70 percent from the beginning of the year. Annual contract value measures contracted annual value under the company's definition in the release context; it should not be added to quarterly revenue or treated as cash collected. The ledger keeps it visible because it is part of the release's forward story, and labels it as a target so the distinction survives citation.</p>
<p>What this asset does not establish is why investors repriced the shares on any specific day, whether the guidance will be met, or whether the contract growth guide signals demand loss rather than timing, mix or conservatism. The filing establishes the reported base, the guided rates and the arithmetic gap between them. That is enough to correct the simplest reading of the quarter. ServiceNow did not report a revenue contraction. It reported growth above 20 percent and guided one forward contract measure to slower growth while guiding subscription revenue to faster growth.</p>
