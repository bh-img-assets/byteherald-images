<p>ByteHerald's registry ledger separates what China's company register proves about DeepSeek's second round from what the headline round size claims. The register proves a new holding partnership, Hangzhou Chengyan, took 17.2650 percent of DeepSeek's operating entity on November 17, 2026, with 31 new partners and about 3.439 billion yuan of registered contribution. It does not print the roughly 100 billion yuan round size reported by Caixin Global on November 20, 2026. The gap between those two numbers is the story: the filing proves ownership changed, while cash received remains unconfirmed in the public record we reviewed.</p>

<h2>What the register does and does not prove</h2>
<p>The register proves structure. A single partnership now holds 17.2650 percent of the operating entity, and the largest named blocs inside that partnership are Tencent's affiliate and three CATL funds at 9.5653 percent each. Multiplied through, each bloc equals about 1.65 percent of DeepSeek. China Mobile's investment arm and CITIC Securities Investment each hold 7.6522 percent of Chengyan, equal to about 1.32 percent of DeepSeek. Those are ownership shares, not control rights, board seats, or cash amounts.</p>
<p>The register does not prove the round size. Caixin Global reported about 100 billion yuan on November 20, 2026. The Chengyan registered contribution is about 3.439 billion yuan. Dividing 100 billion by 3.439 billion gives about 29, so the reported round is roughly twenty-nine times the registered contribution in the excerpts. That ratio does not prove the report wrong, because subscribed capital and paid-in funding can differ. It does prove that the public filing, by itself, cannot confirm the headline figure.</p>
<p>Analysis: the sequence still matters. The Department of Homeland Security published its exclusion and removal order on October 20, 2026, covering DeepSeek products and third-party products developed using DeepSeek implementations in federal civilian systems. Twenty-eight days later, the registry change was completed. The ledger supports a narrower claim than a verdict on chip restrictions: after the US order, DeepSeek's ownership register added a broad domestic investor platform, while the exact cash paid in stayed outside the filing.</p>
<p>What remains unestablished is the paid-in total, the valuation DeepSeek itself accepts, and whether the new capital changes compute access. Caixin Global also reported on November 20, 2026 that DeepSeek restricted some long conversations and document reading from November 15, 2026, citing insufficient computing capacity. That operating limit is separate evidence from the registry. It suggests capital and compute are different constraints, and the filing answers only the first.</p>
