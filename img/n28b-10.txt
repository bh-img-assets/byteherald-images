<p>ByteHerald's attempts-versus-breaches ledger finds that the ConnectWise headline number counts attempts, not confirmed breaches. Microsoft recorded sign-in attempts against more than 2,250 customer tenants after application credentials for a ConnectWise multi-tenant application were compromised. The same reporting documents privileged access in several intrusions, not in 2,250 of them.</p>
<p>The distinction changes the defensive lesson. A stolen application credential turns one vendor compromise into a tenant-wide authentication problem. The count that measures exposure is the attempt count. The count that measures harm is the smaller, harder number: tenants where access succeeded and data was reached.</p>

<p>The password spray figures show the machine pace. Microsoft recorded more than 151,000 failed logons against 327 accounts at eight organizations between October 10 and 14, 2026, peaking at 2,900 failures per minute. Forty-eight accounts received more than 1,000 attempts each, which is 14.7 percent of the targeted accounts by our arithmetic. Microsoft observed no successful authentications in that specific spray campaign.</p>
<p>ConnectWise published its initial Cloud Backup advisory on October 16, 2026, disabled seven affected app identities on October 17, and completed its restoration phases on November 19. That is 33 days from disablement to completed restoration, a measure of vendor recovery time rather than attacker dwell time.</p>
<h2>What remains unestablished</h2>
<p>Analysis: the documents opened do not establish a final count of tenants breached, the volume of data taken, or how the application credentials were first stolen. They establish a supply chain entry point, machine-speed attempts at scale, and privileged access in several cases. Reporting the attempt count as a breach count overstates the proven harm and understates the proven exposure.</p>
<p>The citable summary is therefore two numbers, not one: more than 2,250 tenants attempted, and several intrusions reaching privileged access in under three hours.</p>
<p>Restoration timing should not be confused with tenant recovery. ConnectWise completing its service restoration on November 19, 2026 closes the vendor side of the incident. Each affected customer still had to review its own application grants, credentials, and sign-in logs, because persistence planted inside a tenant survives the vendor fixing its own service.</p>
<p>The defensive implication follows from the entry point. Application credentials and service principal sign-ins do not look like user logins, so monitoring built around employee authentication can miss the exact path this campaign used. Microsoft's guidance to review service principal sign-ins and unexpected application credential or privilege changes addresses that blind spot directly, because the abuse traveled through access customers had already granted.</p>
<p>Attribution should be read at the strength Microsoft states it. Microsoft assesses Storm-0940 responsibility for the ConnectWise compromise and describes the actor as China-state aligned and active since 2021. That is a vendor assessment from telemetry, not a court finding, and the documents opened do not name individual operators or a directing organization beyond that assessment.</p>
