<p>Palantir reported third quarter 2026 revenue of 2.31 billion dollars on November 2, 2026, up 96 percent from a year earlier, and for the first time in the company's history its United States commercial revenue passed its United States government revenue. Commercial sales reached 984 million dollars, up 148 percent year over year, against 953 million dollars from United States government customers, up 96 percent. The 31 million dollar gap is small, but the crossing is the point: Palantir, built on defense and intelligence contracts, now earns more from American companies than from the American government.</p>

<p>Chief executive Alex Karp highlighted the crossover in the <a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261101396214/en/Palantir-Reports-Q3-2026-U.S.-Comm-Revenue-Growth-of-148-YY-and-Revenue-Growth-of-96-YY-Raises-FY-2026-Revenue-Guidance-to-91-YY-Growth-and-U.S.-Comm-Revenue-Guidance-to-143-YY-Crushing-Consensus-Expectations">company's release</a>, writing that United States commercial revenue had surpassed United States government revenue for the first time in company history. The release also raised full year guidance: revenue of 8.53 billion dollars, up 91 percent, with United States commercial revenue above 3.56 billion dollars, growth of at least 143 percent. Fourth quarter revenue is guided at 2.65 billion dollars, above the 2.46 billion dollar analyst estimate reported by Reuters. Adjusted earnings per share came in at 44 cents, against a 41 cent consensus, and revenue beat the 2.18 billion dollar consensus.</p>
<h2>Why the crossover matters more than the beat</h2>
<p>Palantir's origin story is government work, and for years the question hanging over the stock was whether its Artificial Intelligence Platform could sell at the same scale to companies that buy software on commercial terms, with procurement cycles, price sensitivity, and competitors that government contracts do not have. A quarter in which commercial revenue grows 148 percent while government revenue also grows 96 percent answers the demand part of that question: both engines are running, and the commercial one is now slightly larger. Readers tracking the wider enterprise pullback should read this against ByteHerald's <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/the-enterprise-ai-reckoning-what.html">enterprise AI reckoning</a>, which found companies cutting AI budgets where value was unproven; Palantir's quarter is the counter case, a vendor whose customers are expanding spend rather than trimming it.</p>
<h2>The catch the headline hides</h2>
<p>The number that tempers the celebration is contract value. Total contract value closed in the quarter was 2.92 billion dollars, up only 6 percent from a year earlier, and United States commercial contract value rose 11 percent to 1.46 billion dollars, far below the 148 percent revenue growth in the same segment. Revenue records work delivered and billed in the quarter; contract value records new deals signed. When revenue grows much faster than new signings, a company is converting its existing backlog, and the backlog eventually needs refilling. The Motley Fool's rapid reaction flagged the same gap, noting that United States commercial remaining deal value rose just 4 percent from the prior quarter while commercial revenue rose 29 percent sequentially.</p>
<p>That distinction does not make the quarter weak. It makes the next two quarters the test. If new commercial signings accelerate, the crossover becomes a durable mix shift, and the full year commercial guidance above 3.56 billion dollars looks conservative. If signings stay near flat while revenue keeps converting backlog, growth must slow, no matter how strong this quarter looks. The market's first reaction leaned toward the cautious reading: shares fell about 3 percent in after hours trading despite the beat and the raised guidance, according to reports of the extended session.</p>
<h2>What to watch next</h2>
<p>Three numbers decide whether this crossover holds. First, United States commercial contract value in the fourth quarter, against the 1.46 billion dollars signed in the third. Second, whether government revenue keeps growing near its 96 percent pace, because a crossover built on a shrinking government business would be a different story from one built on both sides growing. Third, the fourth quarter revenue guide of 2.65 billion dollars, which the company must hit while converting backlog at the current pace. Palantir also reported 1.22 billion dollars in adjusted free cash flow and 10.4 billion dollars in cash, cash equivalents, and Treasury securities, so the balance sheet gives it room to wait for signings to catch up. The crossover is real, it is dated November 2, 2026, and it is the first time the commercial side has led. Whether it leads by more next quarter is the story now.</p>
