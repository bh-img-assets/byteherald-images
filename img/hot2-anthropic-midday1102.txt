<p>Anthropic filed a $16.96 billion third quarter on November 2, 2026, and in the same amendment disclosed about $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and lease agreements, substantially all of it for compute and infrastructure. The quarter is a genuine record and the bill is a genuine liability, and neither number cancels the other. The filing's own timing makes the point: about $69.8 billion of those commitments fall due in the 12 months after September 30, 2026, which is more than the quarter's entire annualized revenue of about $67.8 billion.</p>

<h2>The quarter, in the filing's own numbers</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026068676/anthropicpbc-sx1ax1.htm">Anthropic's Amendment No. 1 to its Form S-1, filed November 2, 2026</a>, fills in the third quarter that the October 26, 2026 filing left blank. Revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was $16.956 billion, up 47 percent from $11.555 billion in the second quarter and up 1,127 percent from $1.382 billion a year earlier. Revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was $33.242 billion, against $2.55 billion a year earlier. The price range, share count, and deal size remain blank, so this amendment still does not start the roadshow, and the company has applied to list Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT.</p>
<p>The profit line did not repeat. Operating income of $974 million in the second quarter became an operating loss of $567 million in the third quarter. Frenzy Capital's analysis of the filing points to $5.0 billion of short-term compute in the quarter and a $2.0 billion non-cash charge for matching employee donations. Net loss for the quarter was $23.291 billion, against $12.1 billion a year earlier, a figure dominated by non-cash charges rather than cash operating costs, which is why the operating line, not the net line, is the better guide to the quarter's trading. ByteHerald's <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/anthropic-filed-its-ipo-94-page-risk.html">anatomy of the original October filing</a> laid out the same warning about the first filing's $80.7 billion first-half net loss, most of it non-cash, and the same reading applies here.</p>

<p>That table is the quarter's real story. Revenue grew by $5.401 billion from the second to the third quarter, the first smaller quarterly dollar add in the seven quarters the filing shows, after a $6.824 billion add in the second quarter. Compute grew faster, by $4.305 billion, and took 58 cents of every revenue dollar, up from 48 cents in the second quarter. Growth also came with a price cut: revenue growth in the quarter was partly offset by a decrease of about $600 million from price reductions introduced with new model versions and contractual discounts, as reported from the filing. A company can grow revenue 47 percent in a quarter and still lose operating leverage when its largest cost grows faster and its prices fall at the same time.</p>
<h2>The $895.4 billion, broken into its parts</h2>
<p>The commitment total is not one bill, and treating it as one is the second mistake. The amendment states the aggregate as of and subsequent to September 30, 2026, combining the formal contractual obligations table with short-term commitments and leases not yet commenced. Its parts are:</p>

<tr><td>Leases not yet commenced</td><td>451.2</td><td>Mostly due to begin between 2026 and 2028, running about 10 years on average</td></tr>
<tr><td>Deals signed since September 30, 2026</td><td>100.2</td><td>Mainly data center leases and cloud hosting</td></tr>

</table>
<p>Those four parts sum to $895.4 billion, by ByteHerald addition, which is a useful check that the total is a sum of categories with different legal force, not a single invoice. About $213.8 billion of the total relates to arrangements with exercisable termination rights or contingent conditions before they begin, such as a counterparty securing financing, facilities, or regulatory approvals. And about $150 billion to $175 billion of additional compute arrangements that do not state a fixed dollar commitment sit outside the total entirely, estimated by the company for payments after capacity is delivered. The honest range for what Anthropic has signed or estimated is therefore wider than the headline: $895.4 billion counted, $213.8 billion of it conditional or terminable, and another $150 billion to $175 billion uncounted because no fixed amount is stated.</p>
<h2>When the money falls due</h2>
<p>Timing, not the total, is what an IPO buyer should price first. About $447.5 billion is expected to be incurred before December 31, 2030, and substantially all of the remainder between 2031 and 2045. The near-term slice, $69.8 billion due in the 12 months after September 30, 2026, sits against $95.6 billion of cash and $36.3 billion of short-term investments at September 30, a combined $131.9 billion by ByteHerald addition, close to the $131.8 billion cash and short-term investments figure reported from the filing. The next 12 months of commitments alone are about 53 percent of that combined pile, and they exceed the quarter's annualized revenue. That does not mean Anthropic cannot pay: revenue is still growing, cash flow from operations for the first nine months was about $6.6 billion by filing-derived reporting, and commitments are incurred as capacity is delivered, not all at once. It does mean the company has converted a growth story into a fixed-cost story, and fixed costs do not fall if revenue growth slows.</p>
<p>Named counterparties show where the money goes. The filing's named minimum commitments include at least about $121.3 billion to Google from April 2026 to July 2033, at least $110.0 billion to Amazon from May 2026 to April 2036, and at least $44.9 billion to Microsoft from November 2026 to June 2033, alongside $161.2 billion of Broadcom equipment lease rent and up to $120.5 billion to SpaceXAI, as tallied from the filing. Readers comparing this total with other big AI numbers should keep the universes separate. ByteHerald's <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/five-companies-828-billion-2026-ai.html">five-company $828 billion capex ledger</a> counts money companies plan to spend building capacity in a single year, while Anthropic's $895.4 billion counts purchase commitments and leases stretching to 2045. One is a year's building budget, the other is a two-decade capacity mortgage, and adding them together would double-count the same capacity.</p>
<h2>What to watch next, without a recommendation</h2>
<p>Three filing facts, not market sentiment, will decide whether this quarter reads as a turning point or a warning. First, whether compute's share of revenue falls back toward the second quarter's 48 percent or stays near 58 percent as short-term buying continues, which the filing says it expects to compress margins in the near term. Second, whether the next quarterly dollar add re-accelerates or shrinks again, after the first smaller add in seven quarters. Third, whether the price range and share count appear in a later amendment, which is the step that actually starts the roadshow. Until then, the filing is a prospectus, not a price, and no reader should treat any of its figures as a valuation: the document itself leaves the offering price blank.</p>
