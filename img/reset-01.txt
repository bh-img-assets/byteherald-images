<p>ByteHerald's provider ledger finds a split the launch headlines missed: text watermark coverage widened through November 2026, while public verification stayed gated at every provider that shipped one. Anthropic completed its model-line retrofit on November 16, 2026, and xAI documented marking for seven Grok models, yet neither gives the public a free detector today. The first dated commitment that changes that is February 2, 2027, when EU Code of Practice signatories must make detection interoperable across providers.</p>

<h2>What the ledger shows</h2>
<p>Three findings. First, defaults diverged: Anthropic applies its mark globally with no opt-out described in its documentation, OpenAI defaults on only in the EU with API opt-in elsewhere, and xAI reversed its own default within three days, from on by default on November 7, 2026 to off by default in documentation recorded by November 10, 2026. Second, Anthropic's coverage carries a technical condition: on six older models, requests that set temperature, top_p or top_k to non-default values might not carry the mark, per its November 2026 documentation. Third, detection is the bottleneck: Anthropic's detector is in private preview, xAI grants detection access by application, and only the EU Code of Practice, published June 10, 2026, requires signatories to offer a free detection tool and cross-provider interoperability by February 2, 2027.</p>
<h2>What this does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: the ledger measures documented coverage and access, not detection accuracy. OpenAI's own testing, reported October 5, 2026, showed detection falling sharply after light editing, and neither Anthropic nor xAI had published false-positive rates in the documents reviewed. A missing watermark therefore does not prove a human wrote a passage. What the ledger does establish is narrower and citable: as of November 27, 2026, marking is widespread and public verification is not.</p>
<h2>How to read this ledger</h2><p>Three terms decide what a row means. Coverage is which models mark their text. Default is whether marking happens without the customer acting. Detection access is who can test a passage. A provider can score well on the first two and still leave the public unable to verify anything, which is the position Anthropic and xAI occupy in their November 2026 documents: Anthropic's detector is in private preview for eligible organizations, and xAI grants detection by application while giving its own API customers an x-watermark header as per-request proof. OpenAI's October 5, 2026 announcement is the only one of the three to pair marking with a public detector, and it limits default marking to the European Union. The ledger will be updated when any provider publishes accuracy figures or opens detection, because those are the two facts that would change its conclusion.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
