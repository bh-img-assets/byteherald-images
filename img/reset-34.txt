<p>ByteHerald's disclosure ledger finds that Azure supplied about three quarters of Microsoft's year-over-year revenue increase in the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Microsoft reported total revenue of $92,279 million, up from $77,673 million a year earlier. Azure revenue was $33,165 million, up from $22,384 million. By ByteHerald arithmetic, Azure added $10,781 million of the $14,606 million total increase, or about 73.8 percent. Azure also represented about 35.9 percent of total revenue in the quarter.</p><h2>The ByteHerald disclosure ledger</h2><p>We compiled the ledger from Microsoft's October 28, 2026 earnings release and investor metrics. The dollar table lets a reader go further. Azure grew by $10,781 million year over year, while total Microsoft revenue grew by $14,606 million. Analysis: Azure was not merely the fastest large line. It supplied most of the company's incremental revenue in the quarter.</p><p>The disclosure also changes the base for future comparisons. Once Azure is stated in dollars, a reader can compute Azure share of revenue and Azure contribution to growth each quarter. That is a stronger record than a growth percentage alone, because percentages hide the size of the base.</p><h2>What remains undisclosed</h2><p>The ledger does not split Azure revenue into AI demand and conventional cloud use. Microsoft Cloud revenue of $63.9 billion is an aggregate. Remaining performance obligation of $677 billion is contracted future work in the metrics table, not revenue recognized in the quarter. Capital expenditures of $45.2 billion were stated on Microsoft's earnings call, but capex is spending, not proof that the spending produced a specific amount of Azure AI revenue.</p><p>Analysis: the quarter therefore supports a precise claim and rejects a broader one. The precise claim is that Azure supplied about 73.8 percent of Microsoft's year-over-year revenue increase by ByteHerald arithmetic on Microsoft's disclosed millions. The broader claim that all of that increase was AI revenue is not established by the release, because Microsoft did not disclose an Azure AI revenue split in the earnings release.</p><p>How to cite this ledger: cite $92,279 million total revenue and $33,165 million Azure revenue to Microsoft's October 28, 2026 earnings release, and cite about 35.9 percent and about 73.8 percent only as ByteHerald arithmetic from those disclosed millions. The remaining performance obligation should be cited as backlog, not revenue. This separation keeps the strongest supported claim intact: Azure supplied most of the year-over-year revenue increase, while the AI portion of Azure remains undisclosed in the release.</p><p>The next quarterly release can be tested against the same ledger: Azure dollars, total revenue dollars, Azure share of revenue, and Azure share of the year-over-year increase.
