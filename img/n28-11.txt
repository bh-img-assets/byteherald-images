<p>ByteHerald's commitment timing ledger finds the sharpest fact in Anthropic's November 2, 2026 filing amendment in the next twelve months, not in the distant total. Anthropic reported third quarter revenue of $16.956 billion for the three months ended September 30, 2026. Annualizing that quarter gives $67.824 billion, by our multiplication. The same amendment disclosed about $69.8 billion of purchase commitments and lease obligations due in the 12 months after September 30, 2026. That near-term amount is 102.9 percent of annualized third quarter revenue, by our division, and exceeds it by about $2.0 billion. The comparison is a timing measure, not a solvency verdict, because commitments are future obligations and revenue after September is not in the quarter.</p>
<p>The method separates filed results from commitment stock. Revenue, operating result and net result are interim filed figures for periods ended September 30, 2026. Commitments are aggregate purchase agreements and leases disclosed as of and after September 30, including leases not yet commenced. The ledger does not treat commitments as expenses already incurred. Percentages and annualized figures are our arithmetic from filed inputs and are labeled as such in the table.</p>

<p>The growth sequence is steep even before commitments enter. Revenue rose from $1.382 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to $11.555 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and $16.956 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The quarter over quarter increase from the second to third quarter is 46.7 percent, by our division. The nine month total reached $33.242 billion. Those are filed interim figures in a registration statement amendment, not audited annual results, and the amendment still left the offering price range, share count and deal size blank. A reader should therefore separate operating scale, which the amendment documents, from public market value, which it does not set.</p>
<p>The profit lines require the same separation. The amendment reported a $567 million operating loss for the third quarter and a $23.291 billion net loss. The operating line is closer to current operations than the net line, which filing summaries describe as dominated by non-cash charges. Analysis: citing only the net loss overstates cash operating pressure, while citing only revenue understates the cost of serving growth. The ledger keeps both lines because the filing's story is scale with losses, not scale instead of losses.</p>
<p>The commitment total extends far beyond the next year. About $447.5 billion is expected to be incurred before 2031, which is 50.0 percent of the $895.4 billion aggregate by our division, with substantially all of the remainder between 2031 and 2045 in filing summaries. That maturity split matters because a single total invites a single reaction. Half of the disclosed stock sits before 2031 and about $69.8 billion sits in the first twelve months. The near-term slice is the part that must be read against cash resources, future revenue and contract terms, none of which this ledger forecasts.</p>
<p>What this asset does not establish is whether Anthropic can fund the commitments, whether counterparties can enforce every amount on the same terms, or what valuation public investors will assign. It establishes the filed sequence and the timing arithmetic: a $16.956 billion quarter, a $567 million operating loss, and about $69.8 billion of commitments due in the following twelve months. That is the citable core. The $895.4 billion total is real as a disclosure, but the first year slice is where the filing becomes a near-term financing question.</p>
