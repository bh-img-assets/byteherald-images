<h2>The re-run: method and table</h2>
<p>Methodology: we took BenchLM's November 23, 2026 board of 117 models, kept models with both a score and a recorded catalog price, and computed blended cost per million tokens at a 3:1 input-to-output mix. Points per dollar is score divided by blended cost. BenchLM states that none of the 117 results rests on a documented independent run: 111 were reported by the labs that built the models. The table therefore measures claimed performance per catalog dollar, not independently verified performance.</p>

<h2>What the re-run shows</h2>
<p>Three findings. First, the value frontier is entirely open: the top five value rows are open models, and the best closed value in the table, Opus 5 at 9.6 points per dollar, sits about 59 times below Laguna XS 2.1. Second, the capability gap did not move: the best priced open score remains 80.6 percent against 96 percent closed, a gap of 15.4 points. <h2>What this does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: points per dollar answers one question only, how much claimed coding performance a catalog dollar buys at a 3:1 token mix. It does not measure reliability, latency, or cost per solved task, and unpriced open models such as Ornith-1.5-397B at 86 percent cannot enter the value table at all. Within those limits, the conclusion is stable across both readings: open models win on value by orders of magnitude, closed models keep the absolute crown, and six weeks of new releases widened neither gap.</p>
<h2>How to read the value table</h2><p>Points per dollar is a ratio, and ratios hide levels. Laguna XS 2.1 tops the value table at 567 points per dollar with a score of 70.9 percent, while DeepSeek V4 Pro scores 80.6 percent at 40.7 points per dollar. A team that needs the highest claimed solve rate buys a different row from a team that needs the cheapest acceptable one, and the table supports both readings because it keeps score and price visible beside the ratio. Two further cautions apply to every row. Catalog prices are list rates, not negotiated or cached-token costs, so real spend per solved task will differ. And because BenchLM records no independently documented run for any of the 117 models, the score column is best read as what each lab claims under its own harness. The value conclusion survives those cautions because the gaps are orders of magnitude, not margins.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
