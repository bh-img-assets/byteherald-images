<h2>What the numbers mean</h2>
<p>Our arithmetic on the Divergence row shows why headline counts mislead. Three binding designs out of about 35,000 generated designs is 0.0086 percent. Twenty dislodging designs is 0.057 percent. Those rates do not make the work useless, because hit generation is a funnel. They do mean the citable result is the funnel output, not the 35,000 input count. The same discipline applies to BigHat: about 1,010 variants in about 10 weeks is a throughput result, and binders over 10 times tighter is a lead-optimization result. Neither declares a development candidate.</p>
<p>Enveda is the clearest measured gain and the easiest to misread. Reviewing 1,061 pages, catching all 11 known issues plus 7 missed, and cutting review time by 89 percent is a real document-work result. It is not evidence that a molecule works. In the ledger it belongs under manufacturing and filings, where Anthropic placed it, not under drug discovery output.</p>
<p>The Mithril row needs the staffing correction. Anthropic described Mithril as a one-person company coordinating 11 external labs. Nature reported on November 11, 2026 that Mithril had one full-time employee, hired part-time consultants, and paid at least five contract research organizations. Analysis: the operating model is coordination of rented laboratory capacity with AI assistance. That is a real model. It is not solo drug discovery, and Nature reported the two antibodies remain in early development.</p>
<p>What the ledger does not establish is clinical value. No row names a formal candidate, a first-in-human trial, or an independent reproduction outside the partnerships. The next citable event is therefore specific: a named candidate, a trial start, or a partner result reproduced by a group that did not run the program. Until one of those appears, the accurate description remains Anthropic's own: early progress, mostly preclinical, with no drug yet.</p>
