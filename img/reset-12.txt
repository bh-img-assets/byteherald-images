Submissions are checked by the Lean kernel or an executable task checker, so scores carry no partial credit from a human grader. The TCSExam Index weights theorem proving, solver engineering and constructions at 30 points each and open conjectures at 10; construction scores above 100 percent are not capped, so the Index can exceed 100. Eight agents ran the Full suite; Opus 5.5 ran only the 20-task Lite suite, where it leads at 72.3, and is excluded from this ledger's Full comparison. Moonshot AI, maker of the Kimi models, co-authored the paper, a conflict the paper's affiliation list makes visible.</p><h2>What the ledger shows</h2><p>Three findings follow. First, theorem proving is a one-model result: Astra's 70.0 percent is nearly double Kimi K3.1 Pro's 38.3 percent, and the other six agents reached at most 11.7 percent, so the 70 percent headline describes one system, not the field. Second, constructions produced verified records: a 630-edge girth-5 graph against a prior best of 629 edges, and a [[25,7,6]] quantum code raising minimum distance from 5 to 6, both independently verified per the paper. Third, the open-conjecture track produced no outright resolution: the accepted submissions adapted counterexamples and proofs OpenAI had already published, while Astra proved a special case of the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture over the field of five elements for n up to 9, extending prior results, which is progress on a conjecture rather than its settlement.</p><h2>What the ledger does not establish</h2><p>Analysis, labeled as analysis: the ledger does not show that AI cannot do original mathematics; it shows that on 30 formalized open conjectures, no agent produced a complete proof or refutation in this evaluation, under this attempt budget. Construction records improve known objects by one edge or one distance step, which is verifiable progress of a different kind from resolving an open problem. The Index itself should be read with its weights attached: a 71.9 built from 30-point tracks and a 10-point conjecture track will always flatter proving over discovering. The honest summary of the November 5, 2026 record is that machine-checked proof production is now strong in one system, record-beating construction is real, and open-conjecture settlement remains at zero.</p><p>The Lite suite adds one more check on the ledger's reading. Opus 5.5, run only on the 20-task Lite set, leads it at 72.3 Index points, and the paper reports a Spearman correlation of 0.93 between Full and Lite rankings across the eight shared agents. That alignment supports using Lite for cheaper screening, but it does not change the Full-suite conjecture result: no agent resolved an open conjecture outright on either suite in the November 5, 2026 evaluation.</p><p>By ByteHerald Staff.
