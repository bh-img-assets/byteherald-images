<p>ByteHerald's confirmation ledger separates the 2026 AI valuation headlines into two groups that should never be averaged together: rounds a company announced as closed, and talks that no company has confirmed. Across the five confirmed closes in the ledger, companies raised $8.6 billion in total, by our addition of the announced round sizes. The share of each company implied by its headline valuation ranged from 2.6 percent at Databricks to 11.5 percent at Baseten, using the simple division of raise by valuation. The reported OpenAI round sits outside those totals because OpenAI declined to comment and no close was announced in the October 22, 2026 reporting.</p>
<p>The method is narrow by design. A round enters the confirmed group only when the company or a same-day report of the company's announcement states the amount and valuation as a completed financing. The implied share sold is our arithmetic, not a cap table disclosure, and it assumes the headline valuation is the denominator the announcement intends readers to use. Where pricing was reported as split across tranches, the ledger keeps the highest announced valuation visible and labels the split as reported rather than confirmed. Revenue figures are included only where the announcement or same-day reporting tied them to the round, and they are not audited financial statements.</p>

<p>Databricks is the anchor because it pairs the largest confirmed raise with the smallest implied share sold. Reuters reported on August 13, 2026 that Databricks raised $5 billion at a $190 billion valuation, and the same reporting tied the round to a revenue run rate above $7 billion growing more than 80 percent year over year. Dividing $190 billion by $7 billion gives about 27 times the stated run rate, our calculation. That multiple is a pricing fact about the round, not a claim that the valuation is justified. It does show why the Databricks headline differs from a pre-revenue mark: the denominator in the multiple is a disclosed operating figure, even though the figure itself is company reported.</p>
<p>Legal AI shows the opposite pattern in the same ledger. Harvey announced $550 million at $15.5 billion on September 9, 2026, an implied 3.5 percent share. Legora announced $550 million at an $8.8 billion post-money valuation on October 21, 2026, an implied 6.3 percent share, with annual recurring revenue above $200 million in its announcement. The two rounds raised the same cash amount at valuations $6.7 billion apart, our subtraction. Analysis: the gap cannot be read from the raise size alone. It reflects different investor pricing of two companies in the same category, and the ledger does not establish which price public markets would support.</p>
<p>Cerebras and Baseten mark the limits of headline reading. Cerebras announced the closing of a $1 billion Series H at a post-money valuation of approximately $23 billion on February 3, 2026, an implied 4.3 percent share. Baseten said in June 2026 that it raised $1.5 billion at a $13 billion valuation, while June 18, 2026 reporting described split pricing with some investors at $11 billion and others at $13 billion. The ledger therefore records Baseten as a confirmed raise with reported split pricing. Treating $13 billion as the price every investor paid would go beyond the evidence.</p>
<p>The OpenAI row is the absorption point for the separate $30 billion raise article, and it stays unconfirmed. TechCrunch reported on October 22, 2026 that OpenAI was working on raising up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, that terms were still being negotiated, and that OpenAI declined to comment. A pre-money figure also cannot be compared directly with post-money marks without adjustment. Adding the full $30 billion would imply about $1.28 trillion post-money, our addition, but that arithmetic describes a reported scenario, not a closed round. Until a close is announced, the correct ledger entry is talks, not valuation history.</p>
<p>What this asset does not establish is equally important. It does not rank business quality, forecast exits, or prove that any private mark would clear in public markets. It establishes a cleaner reading rule: confirmed cash, confirmed valuation basis, implied share sold, and evidence status belong in the same row. Headlines that omit any one of those four items are incomplete, and talks that omit confirmation belong in a separate row until the company closes them.</p>
