<p>ByteHerald bridged Alphabet's third quarter from its SEC earnings release, and one line explains the miss. Google Cloud supplied about 62 percent of Alphabet's total revenue growth, by our arithmetic from the release figures, while an $8.7 billion equity-securities loss in the filing turned a 30 percent rise in operating income into a fall in net income. The quarter missed on paper, not on sales.</p>
<h2>The ByteHerald earnings bridge</h2>
<p>Methodology: we took the quarter-ended September 30 figures from Alphabet's Q3 2026 earnings release furnished with its Form 8-K on October 28, 2026, and computed growth contributions ourselves. Cloud's prior-year base is implied by the release's reported 103 percent growth rate, so the 61.8 percent share is an approximation, labeled as our arithmetic.</p>

<h2>Where the bridge breaks</h2>
<p>The operating business strengthened. Revenue rose 25 percent, Google Services reached $96.3 billion, Search and other grew 12 percent to about $63.4 billion, and Cloud operating income more than tripled to $11.1 billion. Operating margin held near 32 percent. None of that produced a higher bottom line, because other income and expenses swung to negative $8.2 billion. The chief financial officer attributed the swing primarily to an unrealized loss on the SpaceX investment; the filing puts the equity-securities loss at $8.7 billion, cutting net income by $6.7 billion and diluted EPS by $0.55. The release itself does not name the investee, so the SpaceX attribution rests on the CFO's remarks, not the release text.</p>
<p>The second break is cash. Capital expenditure of $51.4 billion exceeded operating cash flow of $42.3 billion, leaving free cash flow at negative $9.07 billion. Alphabet kept its full-year capex guide at $195 billion to $205 billion and said 2027 spending would increase significantly. The quarter therefore shows two different companies at once: an operating business growing at record Cloud rates, and a balance sheet absorbing investment marks and infrastructure spending faster than operations fund them.</p>
<h2>What the bridge does not establish</h2>
<p>Analysis: an unrealized equity loss can reverse if the underlying valuation recovers, and one quarter of negative free cash flow does not establish a trend. Consensus comparisons in market coverage use estimates Alphabet does not publish, so the size of the miss against expectations is a market construct, not a filing fact. What the filing establishes is narrower and firmer: growth concentrated in Cloud, operating profit up, net income down on investment marks, and capex above operating cash flow.</p>
<p>The mix shift inside revenue is the second firm fact. Cloud rose from about 15 percent of revenue a year earlier to about 24 percent this quarter, on the release figures, while Search growth slowed to 12 percent from faster rates earlier in 2026. Management's own framing on the call, a half-trillion-dollar annual run rate with first-party model APIs near 32 billion tokens per minute, describes a company whose growth engine is moving from advertising toward AI infrastructure and enterprise AI services. The bridge quantifies that move instead of asserting it: roughly three-fifths of the quarter's revenue growth came from one segment that was one-seventh of the business a year ago.</p>
<p>Three series decide whether this quarter was an event or a pattern, and all three are checkable in the next filing: whether Cloud keeps supplying the majority of growth, whether equity marks swing back, and whether capex stays above operating cash flow as the 2026 guide is spent. None of those checks requires a share-price prediction. They require reading the same bridge again next quarter, which is what makes the ledger, rather than the headline miss, the durable record of this quarter.</p>
