Anthropic unveiled an office suite inside Claude on November 10, 2026, at an event in San Francisco: Sheets, Meetings, Whiteboards, and Data, plus a way to open full apps such as Adobe and HubSpot inside a conversation. The same morning, Microsoft shares fell as much as 3.5 percent before recovering part of the drop. Anthropic is now selling the whole office, and the market priced the threat the same day.

Anthropic launched Claude Sheets, Claude Meetings, Claude Whiteboards, and Claude Data on November 10, 2026, putting Claude in direct competition with Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace, and Microsoft shares fell as much as 3.5 percent intraday, to 526.37 dollars, before standing 1.9 percent lower at 534.63 dollars in mid-afternoon trading in New York.

The stock move, measured

Microsoft closed at 545.18 dollars on November 9. On November 10 it opened at 542.98 dollars, touched a high of 544.19 dollars, and fell to an intraday low of 526.37 dollars, a decline of 3.5 percent from the previous close, before recovering to 534.63 dollars at 2:55 p.m. Eastern, down 1.9 percent on the day. The move was real but partial: the market reacted to the headline, then bought back about half the drop within hours. One morning does not price a moat, and a stock that falls 3.5 percent intraday and stands 1.9 percent lower in mid-afternoon trading is a market that is unsure, not one that has decided.

Software stocks have sold off on AI threats several times this year, and the pattern is now familiar enough to name: an AI lab announces a product that touches an incumbent's core business, the incumbent's stock drops, and the question becomes whether the product can actually do the work. We mapped that pattern on October 30, when four software stocks fell in a single day on the same fear, and the lesson from that day was that the selloff is the easy part to measure and the product is the hard part to judge. The honest way to read November 10 is the same way: the stock move is a signal that the threat is taken seriously, not evidence that the threat has landed.

What the suite is

Claude Sheets is the headline product: a spreadsheet service where Claude generates and edits spreadsheets, with tabs, formulas, dropdown menus, and formatting, and files that export to Excel. Anthropic is billing it as a collaborative tool, where everyone with editing access can make changes and Claude's tweaks are tracked for coworkers to see, and it supports the company's Google Workspace plugin. Sheets is in beta for Claude subscribers across the web, desktop, and mobile.

Claude Meetings puts Claude into video calls as a visible participant, starting with Google Meet, where it takes notes while people talk and can later be asked about the plans and action items that came up. It is available only to Team and Enterprise customers, and it will not join a call with outside guests unless an admin allows it, and it leaves if one joins anyway. We covered Meetings in detail earlier today, when it launched: Claude joins the call, works on tasks live, and drafts plans, whiteboard timelines, and meeting-chat answers from connected apps. Claude Whiteboards is a shared, real-time board where teams brainstorm with Claude, which groups ideas by theme and drafts action items for each person, and it is in beta for all paid users. Claude Data, renamed from Claude Dashboards, connects to a company's CRM and data warehouses so Claude can answer questions about what is inside them, produce reports and charts, and narrate video summaries, and it is rolling out to all paid users.

The fifth piece is the one with the longest tail: interactive connectors that open full versions of third-party apps inside a Claude conversation, so the chatbot can act on what it sees in the window. Anthropic is starting with a small group of partners that includes Adobe, Atlassian, and HubSpot, and it already lists more than 9,000 connectors in the Claude Marketplace. The suite follows Claude Docs and Slides, which arrived in September, and Claude Dashboards, which launched on October 8 and has now been renamed Data. Anthropic is building the office one application at a time, and November 10 is the day the collection started to look like a suite.

What Sheets cannot do yet

The most important sentence in the launch coverage did not come from a critic. Anthropic's product lead, Nicholas Lin, said Claude Sheets is too basic for advanced financial modeling. That is the honest limitation, and it sets the moat test precisely: the suite threatens the middle of the office, the trackers, budgets, and shared sheets that most teams live in, and it does not yet threaten the power users who build financial models in Excel. The launch also puts Anthropic in sharper competition with Microsoft and Google, which are both Anthropic backers, according to Bloomberg. The company that sells the model now competes with the companies that fund it, and the spreadsheet is where that tension becomes a product instead of a press release.

The 81 billion dollar run rate

The number that matters more than the stock move came in a filing the same day. Anthropic's revenue run rate reached 81 billion dollars as of October 31, up 10 billion dollars from the end of September, and the company is targeting a fully diluted valuation of about 1.4 trillion dollars in an initial public offering later in November, according to Bloomberg. The run rate had passed 65 billion dollars in July, reached 71 billion by the end of September, and 81 billion by October 31. October alone added 10 billion, more than the two months before it combined, and the trajectory is the real story of the launch: the company is adding run rate while it builds the products that justify it.

We audited the IPO filing this morning: 825 million Class A shares at 70 to 78 dollars, gross proceeds of 57.75 to 64.35 billion dollars, and a valuation of about 916 billion to 1.02 trillion dollars on shares outstanding, or about 1.26 to 1.40 trillion dollars fully diluted. The office suite is the product that filing is selling: a company that began as a model lab is now asking public investors to value it as a workplace platform, and the run rate is the evidence that the pivot is working.

What to watch

First, independent reviews of Sheets, because the gap between a beta spreadsheet and Excel is measured in edge cases, and the first users to hit them will say so publicly. Second, whether Microsoft and Google answer with price or with product, since both have their own AI office tools and both are Anthropic backers. Third, whether Meetings expands beyond Google Meet, since the meeting agent is the suite's most distinctive piece and it currently lives inside a competitor's product. And fourth, the IPO pricing later in November, because the 81 billion dollar run rate and the 1.4 trillion dollar target will be judged together, and the suite is the reason the second number is supposed to follow from the first.
