Google made Gemini 4 Carbon broadly available on November 10, 2026, two weeks after announcing it to a small group of cybersecurity defenders. Paying developers and enterprises can use it through the Gemini API, and Google AI Ultra subscribers get it in the Gemini app, Antigravity, and AI Mode in Search. The introductory price doubles on December 1.

Gemini 4 Carbon is available from November 10, 2026, to paying developers and enterprises through the Gemini API and to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, Antigravity, and AI Mode in Search, at 2 dollars per million input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens until November 30, then 4 and 20 dollars from December 1.

Who gets it, and where

Carbon is Google's strongest model, built for long-horizon agentic work across complex knowledge work, software engineering, and scientific reasoning, and for cybersecurity capabilities. The November 10 opening covers paying customers first: developers through the Gemini API in AI Studio and Android Studio, enterprises through Gemini for business, and consumers who pay for Google AI Ultra, who get Carbon in Antigravity, the Gemini app, and AI Mode in Search. Scientists get a separate door: Google is expanding its research program to offer selected current and future participants both Argon and Carbon in Antigravity's highest usage tier free for six months.

The most asked question about the launch has a short answer: no, Gemini 4 Carbon is not free. It is excluded from the Gemini API free tier, and Google's announcement does not list it for free users or for AI Pro subscribers. The free door is the one that stays shut, and the paid doors are the ones that opened.

What changed since October 28

Carbon was announced on October 28, and the announcement came with a limit that defined it: access went first to a set of cybersecurity defenders through Google's Fairwind Program, and everyone else waited. We covered that announcement as it stood: Carbon led the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 59, ahead of Argon at 50, and you could not use it. November 10 removes the limit for paying customers, and the title of that post is now out of date. The cyber-tuned variant stays gated: defenders still apply through the Fairwind Program, with rolling admission. Google also previewed coordinated AI agents in Antigravity, called swarm, alongside the availability announcement. Carbon joins Argon in the Gemini 4 lineup rather than replacing it.

The price, and the deadline

The introductory rate matches Argon's: 2 dollars per million input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens, and the footnote is the part that matters. That rate ends on November 30, and from December 1 the price becomes 4 dollars per million input tokens and 20 dollars per million output tokens. Every processed token is billed, including thinking tokens and specified internal grounding instructions and declarations, so the bill for a long-horizon task is not the input price alone. Cached input is priced at 10 cents per million tokens during the introductory period, then 20 cents.

The price is also low against the competition, on list price alone. Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2, released November 9, costs 8 dollars per million input tokens and 40 dollars per million output tokens, so Carbon's introductory rate is a quarter of Fable's on both counts, and its December rate is half. List price is not task price, and effort settings and token counts change the bill, but the gap is large enough to matter for anyone choosing a frontier model this month. The deadline is what makes it a decision: use Carbon before November 30 and the price is 2 and 10 dollars; wait until December and it is 4 and 20.

The rollout caveat

Availability is staged, not instant. Artificial Analysis notes that Carbon is still being rolled out to selected users, which means the announcement and the access are not the same day for everyone. On the October 2026 model card, Carbon leads Argon by nine points on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index version 5.0, 59 to 50, and by twelve points on the long-context GraphWalks test at 256K to 1M tokens, 96.2 percent to 84.2 percent. In Google's safety review, Carbon stayed below the bar that would have forced a full new critical-capability assessment, so the company did not run one. The model is stronger than Argon on the published tests and, by Google's own safety accounting, not a step change from it. Both of those things can be true, and the honest reading is that Carbon is the better model to use, not a different kind of model.

What to watch

First, whether the December 1 doubling sticks, since the introductory rate was always dated and the real price of Carbon is the December price. Second, independent runs, because the index and GraphWalks numbers are Google's and the model card's, and the first outside measurements will either confirm them or narrow them. Third, whether free-tier access ever comes, since the free door is the one most users are waiting behind. And fourth, the Antigravity swarm preview, because coordinated agents are the feature Carbon was built for, and the preview is the first public sign of what that looks like.
