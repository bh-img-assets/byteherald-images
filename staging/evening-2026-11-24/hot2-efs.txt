<p><a href="https://claude.com/resources/articles/zero-data-retention-with-enterprise-frontier-safeguards">Anthropic opened Enterprise Frontier Safeguards in public beta on November 23, 2026, offering enterprise customers automated safety monitoring that works without Anthropic or their cloud provider keeping a copy of their data</a>. Customers store their own prompts and responses in their own cloud storage, under their own keys, and Anthropic's monitors read that storage for signs of misuse. It is the answer to a fight that started in June, when Anthropic told enterprises it would keep their traffic for 30 days.</p>

<h2>What zero data retention actually means</h2>
<p>Zero data retention, or ZDR, is a promise about custody, not about deletion alone. In a normal enterprise contract, the AI vendor stores your prompts and responses for some window, often 30 days, so it can investigate abuse. Under ZDR, the vendor keeps nothing: your data lives only in storage you control. The catch, until now, was safety. A vendor that keeps nothing cannot look across many requests for a slow attack, because each request looks harmless on its own. Anthropic's answer is to split the two jobs: the data stays in your cloud, and the safety monitoring comes to the data. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-zero-data-retention-audit-who-keeps.html">We audited ten providers' retention policies last week, and Anthropic was one of the vendors still holding enterprise traffic for 30 days; this launch is the change that audit was waiting for</a>.</p>
<h2>How EFS works</h2>
<p>The system has two layers. The first is the one every zero-retention customer already had: real-time classifiers that screen each request as it arrives. The second is new: automated safety monitoring that looks for patterns across many requests. Anthropic's September threat intelligence report describes why that matters: an operation against roughly fifty organizations divided an attack among many parallel agents, and the agents saved harvested credentials between sessions so the campaign could resume. Viewed one request at a time, each piece looked benign. EFS is built to catch the pattern, not the single request.</p>
<p>When the monitoring finds a risk, it creates a Safety Case for the customer's own security team to review. The case can be read from the customer's cloud storage or through an API, and it can feed into the company's security information and event management system. The case does not include inputs or outputs. Anthropic sees only the case ID, the harm category, the status, and the flagged transcript IDs. Inputs and outputs are deleted promptly after they are written to the customer's storage, encrypted with the customer's own keys.</p>

<p>OpenAI announced its own zero-retention safety system in August, according to Engadget, which makes EFS the second vendor answer to the same enterprise demand. The difference is the mechanism: Anthropic published how the monitoring works without keeping the data, down to what its systems can and cannot see. That detail is the point. Enterprises that refused a 30-day retention policy were not asking for a promise; they were asking for an architecture they could audit.</p>
<h2>Who is using it, and what it does not cover</h2>
<p>EFS was developed with more than 100 customers, including a quarter of the Fortune 100, and companies in healthcare, financial services, and law. Bridgewater, Salesforce, and Stripe have been evaluating it in private beta. Wells Fargo's Sharma said the bank's logs stay in a Wells-managed environment under Wells-managed keys, and that the bank keeps custody of its data while Anthropic operates the detection. Stripe's Kemelhar said the system lets Stripe retain conversation logs in its own AWS environment, with access governed by Stripe's security controls. The system runs on the Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock, and Claude on Google Cloud, with Microsoft Foundry support coming soon. Launch coverage reports one exclusion: organizations with HIPAA readiness enabled cannot use EFS yet, so healthcare customers on that configuration wait. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/ai-agents-need-id-agent-identity-stack.html">EFS watches what agents do; the other half of enterprise agent trust is knowing which agent is acting, which is the identity problem we compared in October</a>.</p>
