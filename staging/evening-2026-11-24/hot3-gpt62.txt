<p><a href="https://the-decoder.com/openai-updates-chatgpt-to-handle-complex-everyday-questions-web-searches-and-images-more-reliably/">OpenAI is rolling out a chat-tuned version of GPT-6.2 Sol to ChatGPT's Chat tab, starting November 23, 2026, for Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users</a>. It is the first time the new model family reaches everyday chat, six days after Astra and Sol launched in Work and Codex. OpenAI also set a retirement date for the models it replaces: GPT-5.6 Sol leaves Chat, Work, and Codex on December 23, and GPT-5.6 Terra leaves Work and Codex the same day.</p>

<h2>What changes in the Chat tab</h2>
<p>The Chat tab gets a version of Sol tuned for everyday conversation, not the Work and Codex models, which stay the same. OpenAI says the chat model handles complex requests and web searches better than GPT-6, understands images better, follows detailed instructions more often, and asks more relevant follow-up questions. The rollout is global, and Enterprise availability depends on workplace admin settings. Free and Go plans do not get GPT-6.2 in this release. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/gpt-62-astra-first-model-rated-high-for.html">GPT-6.2 launched on November 17, 2026, as two models: Astra for complex professional work and Sol for speed, with Astra priced at $8 per million input tokens and Sol at $2</a>.</p>
<h2>The numbers OpenAI published</h2>

<p>Every number in that table is OpenAI's own measurement, not an independent test, and the company says the figures apply only to ChatGPT's chat mode. The biggest gains are in youth safety, specifically around emotional dependency, eating disorders, and dangerous activities, according to the system card. That matters because the Chat tab is where the youngest and least technical users meet the model, and it is also the surface where a model change is hardest for a user to notice: the tab looks the same, only the model behind it changes.</p>

<h2>What ends on December 23</h2>

<p>GPT-5.6 launched in July 2026, which makes its Chat career about five and a half months long. The retirement is the other half of the rollout: OpenAI is not adding a model, it is swapping one. Paid users who never open Work or Codex will meet GPT-6.2 only through the Chat tab, and users on Free or Go plans stay on whatever they had. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/frontier-ai-prices-flipped-twice-in.html">Sol's price is $2 per million input tokens, and its promotional pricing ends on November 30, with no successor price announced</a>.</p>
<h2>What this does not mean</h2>
<p>A chat-tab rollout is not a capability launch. GPT-6.2 Astra, the frontier model in the family, is not what is arriving here; the chat version of Sol is the smaller, faster sibling, tuned for conversation. The internal test numbers are the vendor's own, and OpenAI has not published an independent evaluation of the chat-tuned version. What the rollout does mean is that the default model for paying ChatGPT users changes this week, and the model it replaces has a dated end. For users, the practical change is small: the same tab, a model that follows instructions better and answers image questions better, according to OpenAI's tests. For OpenAI, it is the moment GPT-6.2 stops being a Work and Codex announcement and becomes the model most paying users actually talk to.</p>
