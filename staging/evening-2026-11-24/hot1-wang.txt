<p><a href="https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/23/ai-superintelligence-arriving-warns-top-meta-executive">Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang warned on November 22, 2026, that superintelligence is arriving and that humanity may not keep control of it</a>. His case rests on a claimed proof of the Riemann Hypothesis that nobody has verified. The essay passed 13 million views on X within a day, drew a "madness" reply from former Obama speechwriter Jon Favreau, and landed hours after OpenAI's chief scientist proposed shared limits on AI development. The warning is real. The proof it stands on is not settled.</p>

<h2>The case in four steps</h2>
<p>Wang's essay makes its argument in four moves. First, the trigger: Levent Alpöge, a mathematician on Anthropic's technical staff, said he had found a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis working with Claude, and Wang called the proof "clearly marking the beginning of a significant change", done "with the help of an alien mind that we don't understand". Second, the precedent: in December 2024, OpenAI's o3 model performed exceptionally on the American Invitational Mathematics Examination, and Wang argued the hardest problems on that exam demand more abstract reasoning than much of the work engineers, executives, and other professionals do. Third, the trend: "We have seen coding be nearly solved, the emergence of extremely powerful cyber capabilities, and strong agentic capabilities", Wang wrote. Fourth, the conclusion: "The remaining gaps for models to greatly surpass humanity in every field are no longer intelligence; they are domain-specific know-how, plumbing, and experimentation."</p>

<p>The proof question matters because it is the essay's load-bearing fact. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/two-riemann-proofs-in-one-week.html">We audited both Riemann proofs last week: Alpöge's Claude-assisted proof and a second, all-human proof by Stanford postdoc Masullo, and neither was verified as of November 20, 2026</a>. The Lean code has been compiled independently, according to our earlier audit, and TNW reports it has been machine-checked by the Lean and Mathlib communities, but no human-readable version exists yet, which means no mathematician has read the argument in the ordinary sense. Wang's essay treats the proof as the moment the shift began. Until it is verified, it is a claim about a claim.</p>

<h2>The reception crossed party lines</h2>
<p>The essay passed 13 million views on X within a day, according to Dealroom, and reached about 14.5 million views with 47,000 likes by Monday evening Pakistan time, according to aitoolranked. Jon Favreau, the former Obama speechwriter and Pod Save America co-host, quoted the essay to his 1.3 million followers and called it "madness", saying elected officials should do "everything they possibly can to stop the frontier labs from handing over control of humanity to superintelligent machines". His post passed 1.5 million views. Elon Musk had posted earlier that Sunday, before Wang's essay appeared, that "Unequivocal super intelligence has arrived. Welcome to the Singularity". Wang quote-posted Favreau hours later with a softer message: "this path is not inevitable", and pointed to safety work at Meta, including delaying the launch of Muse for months and leading on the SI Accords that peer labs signed.</p>
<h2>The counter-position sits inside Meta</h2>
<p>Wang's warning is striking because it comes from inside the race, and because his own chief executive has taken the opposite tone. In an essay published in August, Mark Zuckerberg lamented that discussions about AI were "filled with doom" and said the technology should lead to "more people sharing greater prosperity, health and freedom", according to Al Jazeera. Dealroom reports that Zuckerberg has rejected calls by Anthropic's Dario Amodei and OpenAI's Sam Altman to slow development, and that Meta expects to spend up to $145 billion on capital expenditures this year, driven largely by AI infrastructure. The essay also landed hours after OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki proposed shared, cross-lab limits on AI development. Wang, who is 29 and co-founded Scale AI before becoming Meta's first chief AI officer, is now the most senior voice inside Meta arguing that the default path leads somewhere humanity should not accept. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-ai-transparency-scoreboard-what-six.html">That argument sits beside Meta's own disclosure record: in our transparency scoreboard, Meta published 2 of the 6 things we checked, ahead of only xAI at zero</a>.</p>
<h2>What recursive self-improvement means</h2>
<p>Recursive self-improvement is the idea that an AI system good enough at AI research can improve its own design, producing a better system that improves itself further, with each cycle faster than the last. Wang's point is that once models can automate AI research, the loop closes and humans stop being the bottleneck. The honest limit is that no lab has demonstrated a closed loop: current systems improve with human-designed training runs, human-written evaluations, and human-chosen data. The debate is about how close that loop is to closing, and Wang's essay is the first time a sitting chief AI officer at a major lab has said, in public, that he finds it hard to imagine humanity keeping control once it does.</p>
<h2>What would change the verdict</h2>
<p>Three things would change the reading of this essay. First, the Riemann proof: if a human-readable version appears and independent mathematicians confirm it, Wang's trigger becomes a fact instead of a claim. Second, Meta's own commitments: the SI Accords Wang cites are a promise, and their text and enforcement will show whether the safety work is real. Third, the next year itself: Wang says how humanity acts over the next year will have great consequence, which makes the essay a dated bet. If the industry takes the different actions he calls for, the essay reads as a turning point. If it does not, it reads as a warning that was heard and ignored.</p>
