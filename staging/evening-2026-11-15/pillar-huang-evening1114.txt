<p>In seventeen days, Nvidia lost the chief executive who ran it for 33 years, its board opened an independent investigation, lawmakers asked its former chief to testify, and the company lost about 500 billion dollars of market value. The story has moved so fast that the claims in it have blurred together: an affair, a suspected spy, a smuggling ring, a financing scheme, a petition to bring him back. This is the complete timeline of the Jensen Huang exit, and a check of every major claim in it, sorted into what is verified, what is reported, what is alleged, and what is only speculation. Fourteen claims in all.</p>

<p>Two rows in that table deserve a closer look, because they are the ones most often overstated. The first is the smuggling estimate. SemiAnalysis, a semiconductor research firm, estimated in its latest newsletter that more than 1.5 gigawatts of restricted Nvidia hardware, which it said could be equivalent to up to 1.5 million GPUs, has reached China despite US export controls. The firm does not disclose the exact number of accelerators shipped illegally or their split between product generations, and Tom's Hardware notes that the report does not explain how it converts accelerator quantities into power figures. The firm also speculates that some members of the US intelligence community sought to remove Huang because he opposed more aggressive restrictions on China's access to American AI hardware, but the report provides no direct evidence that intelligence agencies influenced Nvidia's board or that Huang participated in unauthorized exports. That is speculation, and it should be labeled as such wherever it is repeated.</p>
<p>The second is the charged case, which is different from the estimate and should not be confused with it. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-ai-chip-smuggling-ledger-3-billion.html">Our AI chip smuggling ledger</a> records the charged cases, and the largest is the March 2026 case against Supermicro co-founder Wally Liaw and two others, involving about 2.5 billion dollars of servers sold to a company in Southeast Asia, of which 510 million dollars worth was subsequently diverted to China and obtained by Alibaba. A second case, also cited by Tom's Hardware, accuses nine individuals in Taiwan of arranging unauthorized exports of 130 Nvidia B300-based servers, 74 of which eventually reached China, using shell companies, forged documents, and a declared destination of Japan. Both cases are still before the courts. Charged cases are facts. Estimates are estimates. The ledger keeps them apart, and this audit does the same.</p>

<h2>The money</h2>
<p>The market verdict arrived before any investigation could. Nvidia's shares fell 4.3 percent on Thursday, November 12, closing at 166.19 dollars after falling as much as 14 percent in the session to an intraday low of 148.80 dollars. CoreWeave, the Nvidia-backed cloud firm, fell 8.1 percent. Nebius plunged 18 percent after cutting its 2026 revenue forecast, citing delays in adding third-party capacity. Super Micro Computer dropped 12 percent. The shares are now about 30 percent below their record high of October 6, and more than 1 trillion dollars of market capitalization has been wiped out over the past month, after the valuation briefly topped 5 trillion dollars. The week's loss alone is nearly 500 billion dollars, according to Forbes, and the Journal reports that at the nadir after its report that the board planned to oust Huang, the loss exceeded 500 billion dollars. The market capitalization still stands at about 4.01 trillion dollars.</p>
<p>The selloff spread beyond Nvidia on Friday, November 13. The Nasdaq fell 0.9 percent to 25,136.27. AMD slid 8.8 percent, Broadcom fell 4.8 percent, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 3.4 percent on Friday, after gaining 10.6 percent across the first four days of the week. In China, AI model stocks Z.AI and MiniMax fell 12 percent and 8.4 percent as investors worried that Huang's departure could further restrict Chinese access to advanced US chips.</p>
<p>Huang's own fortune moved with the stock. His stake in Nvidia is estimated at about 140 billion dollars, about 3.6 percent of the company, and Forbes' real-time ranking had him fall from eighth to thirteenth among the world's richest people during the week. The petition to reinstate him, backed by Musk, Palmer Luckey, Travis Kalanick, Joe Gebbia, Changpeng Zhao, and Epic Games chief Tim Sweeney, who called the ouster "Apple firing Steve Jobs level of idiocy," has not moved the board. What moved the market instead was the financing question: as one analyst told MarketWatch, investors are grappling with the idea that Nvidia and Huang served a dual function, not only supplying chips but propping up the AI industry from a financial standpoint, and the circular financing story put that function under review.</p>

<h2>What Asia did</h2>
<p>The governments that depend on Nvidia's chips moved within two days. South Korea's Science Ministry said Nvidia had reaffirmed its plan to supply 260,000 advanced GPUs to the country and to continue joint AI projects, and the government's AI strategy council held an emergency meeting on Friday to assess the impact of Huang's departure. Taiwan's Economy Minister Kung Ming-shin said the departure was unlikely to significantly change the growth of the global AI industry, and Taipei's city government said Nvidia's planned overseas headquarters in the city remained on track. Japan's Industry Ministry said it would closely monitor Nvidia, and Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary Masanao Ozaki said it was too early to assess the impact on the global AI industry.</p>
<p>China's response was the sharpest. Its foreign ministry rejected the spy allegations on November 10, calling them groundless and a smear against Beijing, and a spokesperson on Friday reiterated opposition to export controls and said the ministry was not aware of reports linking restricted Nvidia chips to a Shanghai data center associated with Alice Huang. In Singapore, authorities seized Alice Huang's passport in June 2025 as part of an investigation into possible false declarations regarding server purchases, found insufficient evidence of offences in February 2026, warned her for knowingly giving inaccurate information about her role at Megaspeed, and barred her from re-entering Singapore.</p>
<p>The congressional side is moving just as fast. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/democrats-open-nvidia-inquiry-over-h200.html">Democrats opened an inquiry on November 12</a> into how the Trump administration handled Nvidia's chip sales to China, after Trump was briefed about Huang's relationship with a purported Chinese spy and still granted Nvidia permission in December 2025 to sell its advanced H200 AI chips to China in exchange for a cut of the export sales. Senator Tim Scott requested that Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick testify before the Senate Banking Committee. House committee chairs have required Huang and Nvidia's general counsel to appear for closed-door interviews on December 1 and 2.</p>
<h2>What happens next</h2>
<p>Four dates will decide what this story becomes. The first is Tuesday, November 17, when Nvidia reports third-quarter results, its first earnings since Huang's departure, with Kress presenting. The filing that follows will update the commitments and guarantees at the center of the circular financing story. The second is November 26, Thanksgiving, before which House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast said Huang agreed to testify in a closed-door briefing. The third is December 1, the date House committee chairs requested for a transcribed interview, and December 3, when Senate Banking chairman Tim Scott asked Huang to testify, with a subpoena threatened if he does not accept. The fourth is the board's search for a permanent chief executive, which has no announced timetable, and which will decide whether the financing model Huang built survives him.</p>
<p>The question underneath all four dates is the one the audit table is built to answer, and it has two halves. The first half is personal and is largely settled: Huang admitted the affair, the board accepted his resignation, and he is out. The second half is structural and is not settled at all: whether the demand Nvidia reported was real, whether the chips that reached China got there through channels the company knew about, and whether the financing that held the AI boom together was a bridge or a bubble. The first half ended on November 12. The second half will be decided by filings, testimony, and the November 17 earnings, and this audit will be updated as those answers arrive.</p>
