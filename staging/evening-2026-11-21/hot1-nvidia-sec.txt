<p>Nvidia lost about 491 billion dollars of market value in a single session on Friday, November 20, 2026, after Reuters reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is examining the chipmaker's accounting. The stock fell 14 percent to below 126 dollars, its seventh straight daily fall and its seventh straight weekly drop, the longest weekly losing streak since 2008. The probe is the same investigation the Wall Street Journal first reported on Tuesday, but with a focus the Journal could not establish: the accounting itself.</p>

<h2>What Reuters reported, and what it did not</h2>
<p><a href="https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/exclusive-nvidias-accounting-focus-us-sec-investigation-sources-say">Reuters</a>, citing two people briefed on the matter, reported on November 20 that SEC enforcement staff are scrutinizing potential accounting issues at Nvidia. The report could not determine when the inquiry began or exactly which accounting practices are under review. The SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately respond. A civil probe is a fact-finding exercise, not a verdict, and many such inquiries close without any enforcement action. That distinction matters more than the headline number, because nothing in the reporting establishes wrongdoing.</p>
<p>The Tuesday report from the Wall Street Journal had said the SEC was examining whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors, without being able to say what the probe was about. The Reuters report narrows the focus to accounting, which is a different and more specific question than disclosure in general. Our earlier report on the <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/sec-probes-nvidia-huang-pitches-return.html">SEC probe, Huang's pitch to return, and the board's refusal</a> covered that first, unfocused chapter. This is the second chapter, and it is the one that moved the stock.</p>

<p>The slide did not start on Friday. Nvidia <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/nvidia-fell-43-on-markets-best-day.html">closed at 178.67 dollars on November 9</a>, already 25 percent below its October 6 record close of 239.24 dollars. Friday's fall took it to a <a href="https://decrypt.co/383744/nvidia-shares-sink-52-week-low-sec-probing-accounting">52-week low</a> and roughly 47 percent below that record. The Reuters report turned a slow bleed into a rout: the stock had been falling for six straight days before the accounting news, and the seventh day was the worst of them.</p>
<h2>The three open questions</h2>
<p>Three areas of Nvidia's accounting are drawing attention, and none of them has a confirmed answer yet. The first is Singapore. The Financial Times reported that roughly 104 billion dollars of Nvidia's 2025 revenue was booked through its Singapore unit, and that Singapore sales had soared to almost half of global revenue when the company changed how it reports revenue by geography. Nvidia switched from billing addresses to customer headquarters as the basis, which removed Singapore from its geographic breakdown. Nvidia has disclosed that more than 99 percent of datacenter revenue invoiced to Singapore came from US-based customers. Whether the SEC is examining that change, and what it would need to find for it to matter, is unknown.</p>
<p>The second is the guarantees. On the earnings call on Tuesday, Kress said Nvidia's guarantees totaled 118 billion dollars at the end of the quarter, including 105 billion dollars of credit support tied to a single datacenter campus. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/nvidia-clarifies-when-its-neocloud.html">Our report on when those guarantees actually exist</a> explained the trigger conditions: the guarantee applies only once financing is secured, the facility is built, and the compute is installed. Guarantees of that size are exactly the kind of disclosure the SEC watches closely at AI companies, according to the Motley Fool's report, because the question is whether the risk sits on Nvidia's books or off them.</p>
<p>The third is the receivables. Dealroom's summary of the probe notes that Nvidia's receivables are running at 64 days of sales, a figure that invites questions about how quickly reported revenue turns into cash. A long receivables tail is not evidence of anything on its own, but it is the kind of number an accounting inquiry looks at alongside the revenue recognition behind it. Nvidia's next 10-Q, which Dealroom reports will follow its results in the coming weeks, is the document that will give the market the first hard data on all three questions.</p>
<h2>How a civil SEC probe works</h2>
<p>Most readers hear "SEC investigation" and picture a verdict. The process has three stages, and the first two are invisible. An informal inquiry is fact-finding: staff ask questions and request documents, and the company may not even disclose it. A formal investigation gives staff subpoena power. Only an enforcement action, a filed complaint or a settlement, is a finding. The SEC under the current administration has said it will focus on bread-and-butter securities fraud, including accounting violations, and recently launched a new financial reporting and accounting unit inside its enforcement division, according to Reuters. That is the unit whose work this probe would fall under.</p>
<p>Nvidia has been here before, at a smaller scale. In 2022 it agreed to pay a <a href="https://wccftech.com/nvidia-being-investigated-by-the-sec-for-accounting-issues-says-report/">5.5 million dollar fine</a>, without admitting or denying the SEC's findings, after the regulator found it had failed to disclose crypto-mining revenue. A fine that size is a rounding error for Nvidia. The difference now is the subject: the core revenue and guarantee disclosures of a 3 trillion dollar company, not a single product line. The Justice Department is also reviewing Nvidia's planned 12.9 billion dollar purchase of Hugging Face, according to Reuters, and the board's independent committee is running its own investigation alongside the search for a permanent chief executive after Jensen Huang resigned on November 11.</p>
<p>What to watch next is concrete. The 10-Q will show whether the Singapore figures, the receivables, and the guarantee disclosures changed. The board committee's findings, if disclosed, will show what Nvidia's own investigators found. And any move by the SEC from informal inquiry to formal investigation, or to no action at all, will say more than any single day's share price. Until then, the honest summary is the one Reuters gave: the SEC is looking at the accounting, nobody outside the inquiry knows exactly what, and the market has priced in the worst version of the answer.</p>
