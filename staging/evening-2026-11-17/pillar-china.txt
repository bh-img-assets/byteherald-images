<p>The best Chinese AI model scores 46 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. The world leader scores 65. That 19-point gap is the headline of the November scoreboard, and it comes with a twist: the Chinese field is now cheaper per task than the models it trails, and its cheapest model does each benchmark task for 60 cents. Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro leads China at 46, Alibaba's Qwen4-Max follows at 43, and DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash, at 33, costs about a sixth of what Kimi, the Chinese leader, costs per task.</p>

<h2>The scoreboard</h2>
<p>Artificial Analysis runs the board. Its Intelligence Index, version 5.0.1, combines nine evaluations weighted across knowledge work, coding, science and decision-making, with 60 percent of the weight on private or held-out tests. Scores are at each model's highest reasoning setting. Cost per task is the firm's measured figure at the vendor's list price with caching included. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/meta-vs-xiaomi-vs-mistral-open-weight.html">The last time we ranked open-weight models head to head, in October, the field was Meta, Xiaomi and Mistral</a>; the Chinese models have since taken the top of that board.</p>

<h2>Where each model wins</h2>
<p>The single score hides the splits, and the splits are where the Chinese field actually competes. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, the agentic coding test, Kimi scores 67.2 percent, the best of any model on that test, against 48.5 percent for Qwen4-Max and 59.6 percent for Opus 5.5. On FrontierCode 2.0, the private coding benchmark, Qwen4-Max ties Kimi at 26 percent, and Tencent's Hy4 leads the Chinese field at 31 percent. On DeepSWE 1.1, Qwen4-Max scores 75.7, ahead of Opus 5.5 at 74.2. On the Vals Index, Qwen4-Max leads the Chinese models at 63.20 percent, ahead of Kimi at 62.52 percent and Qwen3.8-Max at 59.05 percent. On AA-Briefcase, the knowledge-work test, Qwen4-Max leads at 1,686 Elo, ahead of Kimi at 1,674. On HLE-Diamond, the hard-knowledge test, Qwen4-Max scores 45 percent, up 21 points on its predecessor. On Agents' Last Exam, Qwen4-Max scores 46.7, the best result in the comparison group. The pattern is consistent: Kimi wins the long-horizon agent work, Qwen wins the knowledge work and the coding tests, and neither wins everywhere.</p>
<p>Human preference tells a similar story with different margins. On LMArena's Agent Arena, which grades models on real agent sessions, Qwen4-Max debuted 14th overall with a 7.5 percent net improvement, the top Chinese model, while Kimi sits 21st at 3.95 percent across 22,291 sessions. Kimi was 13th on November 5, and the rank moved as sessions accumulated, which is how a live board behaves. On the text arena, Kimi ranks 24th at 1,488.0, a preliminary result on fewer than 4,000 votes. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/kimi-k31-pro-657-on-terminal-bench-open.html">We covered Kimi's launch in October, when Moonshot reported 65.7 percent on Terminal-Bench</a>; the independent measurement now puts it at 67.2 percent. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/qwen4-max-1m-context-150-pricing-and.html">Qwen4-Max launched on November 16, and our launch report covers the 1 million token context window, the 1.50 dollar input price, and the closed weights.</a></p>
<h2>The cost story</h2>
<p>Cost per task is where the Chinese field stops trailing and starts leading. DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash does each Index task for 60 cents, about a sixth of Kimi's 3.68 dollars and about a forty-fourth of Fable 5.2's 26.54 dollars, by ByteHerald's arithmetic. Qwen4-Max costs 3.27 dollars per task, about a third of its predecessor's 10.02 dollars. The worst value on the board is GLM-5.3: 5.01 dollars per task for the lowest score in the field, 32, about eight times DeepSeek's cost for one point less.</p>

<h2>What November changed</h2>
<p>The field reshaped itself in the last month. Kimi K3.1 Pro launched on October 23, and Moonshot released its weights in early November, making it the top open-weights model on the board at 46. Tencent's Hy4 reached its full release on November 4, with computer use added to the August preview. Qwen4-Max launched on November 16 with a 1 million token context window and a 1.50 dollar input price, 25 percent below its predecessor, and with its weights closed, a departure for the Qwen line, which Bloomberg has described as the most downloaded open-weight family in the world. Kimi, Qwen4-Max and Hy4 all ship a 1 million token window. The open-versus-closed split is the new fault line: five of the six models in the table ship open weights, and the one that does not, Qwen4-Max, is the newest.</p>
<h2>How to read the board</h2>
<p>Four checks keep the scoreboard honest. First, one composite score hides splits, so check the per-benchmark table before choosing a model for a specific job. Second, cost per task is a measured figure and list price is not the bill, so check cache rates and peak hours; DeepSeek's price doubles on weekday peak hours. Third, ranks on live boards move, so treat a rank as a snapshot with a date, not a permanent position. Fourth, weights status decides whether you can run a model yourself or only rent it through an API, and Qwen4-Max's closed weights are the clearest example of the difference.</p>
