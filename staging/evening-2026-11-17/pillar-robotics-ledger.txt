<p>Seven humanoid robot builders have now raised or priced about 3.5 billion dollars in their latest funding events, led by Figure's 39 billion dollar valuation and closed this week by Genesis, a French startup that raised 500 million dollars at a 3.5 billion dollar valuation on November 17, 2026. The money is real. The robots are not yet shipping at anything like the scale the valuations imply, and the ledger below is the first table that puts all seven on the same page, with dates, amounts, valuations and what each round actually was.</p>

<p>By ByteHerald's arithmetic, the closed rounds in the table total about 3.0 billion dollars: Genesis at 500 million, Figure at over 1 billion, Apptronik's February extension at 520 million, 1X at 100 million, and Unitree's IPO at about 900 million. Counting Agility's closed SPAC gross of about 465 million dollars takes the seven latest funding events to about 3.5 billion dollars. An eighth builder sits outside the table: AI2 Robotics, a Shenzhen-based maker of wheeled humanoids, reportedly raised about 735 million dollars at a nearly 3 billion dollar valuation in July 2026.</p>

<h2>Genesis, the newest row</h2>
<p>Genesis is the round that makes this ledger current. Reuters reported on November 17, 2026, that the French robotics startup raised 500 million dollars in a funding round valuing it at 3.5 billion dollars, one of the biggest funding rounds in Europe's robotics sector. Khosla Ventures, Premji Invest and Eclipse co-led the Series A. The backers include former Google chief executive Eric Schmidt, French tech entrepreneur Xavier Niel, Nvidia and the French state-backed investor Bpifrance. The company was co-founded by Theophile Gervet, a former Mistral AI researcher.</p>
<p>Chief executive Zhou Xian said Genesis will use the money to increase production of Eno, its flagship general-purpose robot, to expand its data collection and AI training, and to improve the robot's ability to manipulate objects with its hands. Eno's design features three body segments resembling oversized playing cards, mounted on a rotating wheeled base. Genesis has a partnership with South Korea's LG CNS to test Eno for manufacturing and logistics, and it expects a wider customer rollout in 2027. The round prices a company that has not yet shipped at scale, which is the pattern of the whole ledger, not the exception.</p>
<h2>Figure is the outlier by an order of magnitude</h2>
<p>Figure's valuation is more than four times the next builder's. Its Series C, announced on September 16, 2025, brought in more than 1 billion dollars in committed capital at a 39 billion dollar post-money valuation, led by Parkway Venture Capital with Nvidia, Microsoft, Intel Capital, Brookfield, the OpenAI Startup Fund and Bezos Expeditions participating. Nineteen months earlier, its Series B raised 675 million dollars at a 2.6 billion dollar valuation. The company has raised about 1.75 billion dollars in three equity rounds, and it has told investors it is targeting roughly 9 billion dollars in revenue by 2029, according to industry trackers reviewing its fundraising materials. No new priced round has closed since September 2025, though The Information reported in October 2026 that Nvidia discussed investing another 1 billion dollars. Those are talks, not a round, and no terms or valuation were reported.</p>
<p>Figure is also the builder with the most deployment to show. Its BotQ factory is producing one Figure 03 robot per hour, and more than 350 units have been delivered. That is still a pilot-scale number against a 39 billion dollar valuation, and it is the honest frame for the whole ledger: the money arrived before the shipments did.</p>
<h2>What the money has not bought yet</h2>
<p>The gap between funding and deployment is the second half of the ledger. Unitree is the volume leader and the cheapest robot in the table: it shipped more than 5,500 humanoids in 2025, and it listed on Shanghai's STAR Market on August 19, 2026, at an offer valuation of about 9 billion dollars. Its shares surged as much as 629 percent on their trading debut, briefly implying a valuation near 66 billion dollars, before giving back part of the gain. A public listing prices the company every day, which is a discipline none of the private builders face.</p>
<p>Agility's Digit has the most commercial hours of any humanoid in the table: more than 65,000 hours of documented operation across nine enterprise customer facilities, as reported in July 2026. But Agility is also the row that shows why announced numbers need checking against filings. The merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI closed on November 4, 2026, and the company began trading on Nasdaq as AGLT the next day. It raised about 465 million dollars in gross proceeds, not the more than 620 million dollars projected in July: about 61.5 percent of Churchill XI's shares were redeemed for cash, shrinking the trust from about 424 million dollars to about 164 million dollars, and the balance came from a 201 million dollar private placement led by Foxconn and about 100 million dollars raised after the deal was announced. The filings behind the listing are harsher still. Agility reported 2025 revenue of 1.78 million dollars against a net loss of 138.1 million dollars, and revenue of 361,000 dollars in the nine months to September 2026, down 79 percent from a year earlier, with a net loss of 458.7 million dollars in the same period. Against the 2.5 billion dollar valuation, 2025 revenue implies a price-to-sales multiple near 1,400. The company says it holds more than 300 million dollars in committed multi-year orders for its next robot, Digit 5, but those orders cover about 1,000 robots on a single three-year contract with a related-party customer, depend on contractual milestones, and Digit 5 is not yet on sale.</p>
<p>Apptronik is still pre-commercial by its own description. Its Series A kept growing instead of becoming a Series B: 350 million dollars in February 2025, extended in March 2025 and again by 520 million dollars in February 2026, bringing the total past 935 million dollars at about 5.3 billion dollars by TechCrunch's reporting and more than 5.5 billion dollars by Bloomberg's. Its Apollo 3, described by the chief executive as the first true commercial model, is expected in 2027. 1X took the opposite bet from the factory builders and went straight for the home, pricing its NEO robot at 20,000 dollars. Its last announced funding was a 100 million dollar Series B in January 2024. The Information reported in August 2026 that 1X had been seeking about 1 billion dollars at a 10 billion dollar valuation, but that SoftBank was instead negotiating to buy a majority stake at close to 6 billion dollars, a discount of roughly 40 percent to that target. Those talks had not been confirmed as of September 2026. Tesla began early production of Optimus in Fremont in October 2026, funded from inside a company worth about 1.5 trillion dollars, which is why it has no row for a raise at all.</p>

<h2>How to read a robot funding round</h2>
<p>Four distinctions keep the ledger honest, and they apply to any funding story in this sector. First, announced is not closed: Agility projected more than 620 million dollars in July and closed with about 465 million in November, after redemptions took about 155 million dollars off the total. Second, talks are not a round: a reported discussion of a 1 billion dollar investment, with no terms, is not a valuation. Third, a target is not a price: a company seeking 1 billion dollars at a 10 billion dollar valuation that is offered a majority sale at 6 billion dollars has been marked down, not up. Fourth, a valuation is not cash: Figure's 39 billion dollars is the price its Series C investors agreed to in September 2025, and secondary-market quotes since then have run slightly below it. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/robotics-has-no-chatgpt-moment-data.html">The shipment data behind this check comes from our October robotics pillar</a>, which counted first-half 2026 shipments at about 25,000 units across the industry, and <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/who-trains-robots-inside-physical-ais.html">the training-data pipeline that feeds those robots is mapped in our robot-training pillar</a>. Funding buys factories, data collection and time. It does not buy deployment, and the builders that ship the most robots are not the ones valued the highest.</p>
