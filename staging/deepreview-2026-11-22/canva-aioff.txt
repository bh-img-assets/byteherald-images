Canva added an AI off switch on November 19, 2026, for users on its Free and Pro plans. The design platform now lets anyone hide its generative AI features across the app, and separately show less AI-made content in its library of templates and graphics. Both controls are free, reversible, and rolling out gradually. The switch does not turn off AI entirely: two popular tools, Background Remover and Magic Grab, keep running, and the library control only shows less AI-made content, it does not remove it.

The two controls, and where they live

ControlWhere it isWhat it does

Generative AI feature controlProfile, Settings, Permissions, then switch off Enable generative AI featuresHides supported generative AI features across Canva; they can be switched back on at any time

AI-generated content controlProfile, Your account, AI personalization, then show less AI-generated contentReduces how much AI-made content appears in search results and recommendations, without changing existing designs

The two switches are independent. A user can hide the generative tools and still see AI-made library content, or the reverse. Team accounts work differently: Canva has offered per-feature AI permissions for team administrators for some time, and a regular team member needs an administrator to change them.

What disappears, and what stays on

Switching off generative AI hides Magic Write, Magic Design, Magic Layers, Magic Edit, Dream Lab, Canva Code, AI Shapes, Magic Expand, Canva Craft, and the chatbot's image generation. Anything already created with those tools stays where it is: turning the setting off does not delete existing designs or remove content already generated.

Two tools stay on. Background Remover and Magic Grab, which picks an object out of a picture to move it somewhere else in the design, keep working when the broader AI tools are disabled. Canva's line is that these are assistive editing tools, not generative ones: they edit or manipulate an existing image rather than generate something new. Using them does not add an AI label to the resulting image's metadata. That distinction is the whole design of the switch: Canva draws the line at whether a tool generates something new.

The opt-out wave Canva is joining

Canva is not the first product to give users a way out of AI this week. A day after Canva's announcement, Signal said it would block screenshots by default so that Siri AI and Google Gemini cannot read private chats, calling operating-system AI agents an existential threat to its privacy protections. Our report on Signal's move covers what it blocks and the gap it leaves on Macs. The two announcements are different in kind, one hides features, the other blocks surveillance, but they share a cause: users are asking for control over AI in products they already use, and the companies are answering with switches instead of arguments.

Canva's users have been asking since its big push into AI in 2023, according to The Verge, and the company says the new controls are a temporary solution, not the final form. Canva tied the launch to the public mood, citing Pew Research Center figures: roughly 6 in 10 Americans say they want more control over how artificial intelligence is used in their lives. In the same announcement, Canva said its community has used its AI tools more than 38 billion times, which is why the company is still investing to make them more powerful even as it adds the off switch.

Fast Company called the first switch mostly theater, on the grounds that a user who does not want AI tools can simply not press the button, and the second control more useful, because it helps users avoid being drowned by AI-made material while browsing the libraries that made the suite popular. That judgment is fair as far as it goes, and it misses the part that matters: a switch that is easy to find and easy to reverse changes who decides. Before, AI was the default you worked around. Now, turning it off is a setting, not a workaround.

What the switch does not do

The controls govern what a user sees and uses, not what Canva does with user data. Nothing in the announcement changes training-data policy, and the library control only reduces the likelihood of AI-made images appearing in search results, it does not remove them. A user who wants no AI-made templates at all will still see some. The honest way to describe the change is narrower than the headline: Canva made its AI optional to use and less visible to browse, for individuals on Free and Pro, with the two most-used assistive tools exempt. That is a real concession to users who have asked for one since 2023, and it is not the same as an AI-free Canva.

The how-to is simple enough to be worth the two minutes. To hide the generative tools, open the profile menu, select Settings, then Permissions, and switch off Enable generative AI features. To see less AI-made content, open the profile menu, select Your account, then AI personalization, and switch on showing less AI-generated content. If neither option is visible yet, the rollout has not reached the account, and checking back later is the only fix.
