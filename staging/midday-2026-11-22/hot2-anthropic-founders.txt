<p>Seven people are worth about 141 billion dollars on paper after Anthropic's record initial public offering, and each of them owns only about 1.3 percent of the company. <a href="https://www.moneycontrol.com/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-founders-fortunes-soar-to-140-billion-in-record-ipo-article-14085064.html">Anthropic priced its shares at 83 dollars on November 18, 2026, and at that price each co-founder's holding is worth about 20 billion dollars</a>, according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index. The seven holdings are nearly identical, the control is not shared equally with the ownership, and almost none of the money is cash. The IPO is the largest in history, and the way its wealth is distributed says more about how AI labs are governed than about how rich their founders are.</p>

<h2>The equal split</h2>
<p>The seven co-founders hold between 240,637,980 and 244,173,040 shares each, according to the principal stockholder table in Anthropic's S-1 filing, for a combined 1,697,109,240 shares. At the 83 dollar IPO price, that comes to 140.9 billion dollars combined, which rounds to the 141 billion dollars Business Insider reported. The spread between the richest and the least rich founder is about 300 million dollars, or roughly 1.5 percent of any one holding. Tom Brown, the chief compute officer, holds the most at 244,173,040 shares, worth about 20.27 billion dollars, ahead of chief executive Dario Amodei at 240,637,980 shares, worth about 19.97 billion dollars.</p>

<p>The group became billionaires less than two years ago, after Anthropic raised 3.5 billion dollars at a 61.5 billion dollar valuation. A further funding round in May 2026, at a 965 billion dollar valuation, put all seven on Bloomberg's list of the 500 richest people. The IPO priced the company at about 1.1 trillion dollars, and the shares opened at 111.50 dollars before closing the first week lower. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/anthropics-ipo-pop-is-gone-nasdaq-lost.html">Our report on the IPO's first week</a> tracked the pop fading as the Nasdaq gave back its 2026 gains, which is the first lesson of the fortune: it is the share price, and it moves every day the exchange is open.</p>

<h2>Control without ownership</h2>
<p>Each founder holds about 1.3 percent of the company's fully diluted economic stake, and together they own about 10 percent. Yet the founders retain 50.1 percent of Anthropic's voting power through Founder LLC, an entity that owns a single Class F share carrying that majority vote. They keep those voting rights as long as they collectively hold at least 2 percent of the Class A shares they owned at the time of the public debut. The structure is the point: a group with a tenth of the economics controls a majority of the votes, and the gap between the two is deliberate. It is the same separation of money and control that <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/who-owns-ai-labs-2026-money-and.html">our map of who owns the AI labs</a> found across the industry, and Anthropic's IPO now puts real, public numbers on its row of that map.</p>
<h2>The cash question</h2>
<p>Almost none of the 141 billion dollars is spendable. Each co-founder sold 3,378,378 shares in the IPO, about 1.4 percent of each holding, for about 280.4 million dollars before costs and taxes. The rest is locked up for up to 365 days, and taxes and option exercise costs sit between the paper value and anything a founder can spend. That is normal for an IPO, but the scale makes the distinction matter: 280 million dollars is real money, and it is also less than 1.5 percent of what the headlines count. The wealth will be retold with every move in the share price, because the wealth is the share price. At the first week's close near 97 dollars, the seven holdings were worth roughly 165 billion dollars; at the Friday low near 83 dollars, they were back near 141 billion.</p>
<h2>The 80 percent pledge</h2>
<p>All seven co-founders have pledged to dedicate 80 percent of their personal company equity to philanthropic causes. The prospectus specifies that these commitments are not legally binding and are voluntary and revocable. That distinction is the whole story of the pledge: it is a statement of intent from people who control the company through Founder LLC, not a trust, not a foundation, and not a contract. Whether it becomes real money for charity depends on decisions the founders have not yet made, in amounts they have not yet specified, on a timetable they have not yet set. The pledge is worth reporting exactly as written, and worth not reporting as a donation.</p>
<h2>Who else got rich</h2>
<p>The founders are the headline, but they are not the only winners. Early backers gained with them: Skype co-founder Jaan Tallinn, who led the 124 million dollar Series A in 2021; former Google chief Eric Schmidt, with about a 1 percent stake worth nearly 10 billion dollars at the 965 billion dollar private valuation; and venture firms including Ashton Kutcher's former firm Sound Ventures, Menlo Ventures, and Spark Capital, each holding stakes worth billions at the IPO price, according to Forbes estimates. Employees hold the largest overlooked pool: option holders carry about 119 billion dollars in paper gains, of which about 73 billion dollars is vested, according to the filing as reported by Reuters. The IPO also matters beyond Anthropic. Bloomberg Businessweek estimated that founders and employees at San Francisco AI startups together hold more than 1.2 trillion dollars in company equity, and Anthropic's listing is the first large piece of that wealth to become tradable. For comparison, Elon Musk's fortune sits at roughly 900 billion dollars, more than six times what Anthropic's entire founding group holds.</p>
