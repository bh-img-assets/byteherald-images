<p><a href="https://www.geekwire.com/2026/beyond-nvidia-convoy-co-founder-and-former-microsoft-leader-raise-277m-to-rethink-ai-infrastructure/">Marlowe, a six-month-old startup founded by two former Microsoft vice presidents, raised 277 million dollars in seed funding at a 1.15 billion dollar valuation</a> to build AI data centers that run on chips other than Nvidia's. The round, led by Accel and first reported by Bloomberg, is outsized by any seed standard: 277 million dollars for a company founded in May 2026 with about 30 employees. It buys a bet, not a business, because Marlowe's first site does not go live until December 2026.</p>

<h2>Who is Marlowe, and what is it building</h2>
<p>Marlowe was founded in May 2026 by Dan Lewis, the Convoy co-founder and former Microsoft vice president, and Kutta Srinivasan, also a former Microsoft vice president. The company is based in Bellevue, Washington, and Redwood City, California, and has about 30 employees. It designs and operates computing systems for inference, the work of running AI models in everyday products, rather than training them. Its first chips come from AMD and Etched, and it is not using Nvidia chips for now, though it has not ruled them out.</p>
<p>The idea grew out of what the founders saw at Microsoft: demand was surging for inference, but data centers were still built mainly for training models in the first place. Srinivasan told GeekWire that at the big technology companies where he has worked, chips from vendors other than Nvidia made up a small slice of the fleet, and there were times when a significant portion of that non-Nvidia fleet sat idle, was grossly underused, or did not support the latest models and workloads. Marlowe's answer is to work directly on customers' workloads to get other chips performing at their full potential, giving those companies a way to reduce dependence on a single supplier. Its first site, built around Etched's chips, is scheduled to go live in December 2026 and will primarily serve a large AI coding company. A second, larger site using AMD's new Helios systems is on track for the second quarter of 2027.</p>

<h2>The 2 to 3 times claim, checked</h2>
<p>Marlowe says gains of 10 to 20 percent at each layer of the system, from chips and networking to power management and software, add up to two to three times more output per watt and per dollar than conventional GPU clouds, measured in tokens. That is the company's own measurement, on its own workloads, before its first site is live, and no independent test exists yet. The arithmetic deserves a note: four layers compounding at 10 to 20 percent each multiply to about 1.5 to 2.1 times, by our calculation, so a 2 to 3 times claim implies either more layers than the four named, larger gains somewhere in the stack, or both. The December site is where the claim meets a paying customer, and that is the number to watch.</p>

<p>The round includes a smaller earlier investment led by Eclipse, and the backers include Bessemer, Altimeter, Atreides, and Valor, alongside angels such as KKR co-founder Henry Kravis and Uber chief executive Dara Khosrowshahi. Marlowe is the newest name on a list we track: <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-nvidia-challenger-ledger-10-chip.html">our Nvidia challenger ledger counted 10 chip startups that had raised 15 billion dollars between them</a>, and Marlowe now adds a 277 million dollar operator bet on top of the chipmakers themselves.</p>
<h2>What 277 million dollars buys</h2>
<p>Not much, by the standards of AI infrastructure. GeekWire puts it plainly: by those standards, 277 million dollars does not go far. Most of the funding will go toward chips, networking equipment, and the support needed to run the sites, along with hiring, and Marlowe plans to prove its approach at sites of tens of megawatts first, then finance more capacity site by site. The scale gap is the point: five large technology companies carry about 3.2 trillion dollars in signed future AI obligations, and <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-32-trillion-dollar-ai-debt-nobody.html">our ledger of that off-balance-sheet debt shows how far the buildout has already run ahead of the companies paying for it</a>. Marlowe is a proof of concept at tens of megawatts against a buildout measured in gigawatts.</p>
<p>Marlowe is also not the first inference bet. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/general-compute-raised-100m-as.html">General Compute raised 100 million dollars in October 2026 for inference cloud infrastructure</a>, less than half of Marlowe's seed, which says something about how fast the inference opportunity is repricing. The launch also lands on the day Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after his resignation on November 12, 2026, in a unanimous vote reported by Reuters on November 23, 2026. The timing is pointed: on the same day Marlowe announced its non-Nvidia bet, the chipmaker at the center of the trade got its chief executive back, and its shares rose by about a third in early trading. The honest verdict is simple: a 1.15 billion dollar valuation for a six-month-old company with no live site is a bet that inference, not training, is where the next AI infrastructure money gets made, and that non-Nvidia chips can finally be run well enough to matter. December will start answering it.</p>
