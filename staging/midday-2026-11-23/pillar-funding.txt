<p>Eight AI funding announcements landed on November 23, 2026, and they add up to $2,238.1 million. Seven of them are startup rounds totaling $2,126.1 million, and one round took more than four fifths of that money: Sierra's $1.75 billion Series F, which is 82.3 percent of the seven-round total and, on its own, about 7.5 times the $232.3 million six-round ledger we compiled for October. The eighth announcement is different in kind, a $112 million backing for AIVC, a venture studio that turns enterprise AI projects into companies, so it sits outside the rounds total. A single day of AI funding was both concentrated and scattered at once: concentrated by check size, with one round taking more than four fifths of the money, and scattered by sector, with seven rounds in seven different businesses and no two in the same one.</p>

<h2>One round took more than four fifths</h2>
<p>The concentration is the story. Sierra's $1.75 billion is 82.3 percent of the seven-round total, and the top two rounds together, Sierra and Marlowe, are 95.3 percent of it. The other five rounds combined are $99.2 million, or 4.7 percent. Marlowe's $277 million seed, <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/marlowe-raised-277m-to-ditch-nvidia-it.html">which we covered in detail this morning</a>, is 13.0 percent of the seven-round total on its own. By stage, Series F money is 82.3 percent of the total, seed is 13.0 percent, Series A is 3.3 percent, pre-Series A is 0.6 percent, pre-seed is 0.4 percent, and the one round whose stage was not disclosed, Playbook, is 0.4 percent. The October ledger we compiled a month ago totaled $232.3 million across six rounds, so this single day is more than nine times that whole ledger, and Sierra alone is about 7.5 times it. The comparison is honest only with its limit stated: both ledgers are the rounds we verified, not every round that happened, so the finding is about the verified flow, not the whole market.</p>

<h2>Seven rounds, seven different bets</h2>
<p>What the money is for is as spread out as the check sizes are concentrated. Sierra is building AI agents that answer customer questions and carry out tasks for large companies, and says it passed $400 million in annual recurring revenue. Marlowe is building AI infrastructure on non-Nvidia chips. Agon is building AI simulations for autonomous military systems. Matters.AI is securing enterprise AI data. Playbook is making AI follow protocol in pharmaceutical manufacturing, with more than 50 customers including two of the world's 20 largest drug makers. coprod is standardizing robotics hardware components so builders stop losing two to three years to integration work. Acorda is letting banks share scam risk signals before money moves, founded a month ago by three former Monzo scam-prevention staff and already trialing with three major global payment companies. No sector repeats. That spread is the opposite of the pattern in <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/indias-ai-funding-hit-16b-ten-deals.html">India's funding story this morning, where ten rounds took 90 percent of a $1.6 billion nine-month total</a>: this single day's $2,126.1 million is larger than India's whole nine-month total, and here the money concentrates by check while spreading by sector. The day's other big number puts the ledger in perspective without joining it: Amazon's reported $7.5 billion Decart deal is about 3.5 times the seven-round total, and it is an acquisition of software, not a funding round, so it stays out of the sums.</p>
<p>AIVC deserves its own paragraph because it is not a startup round. The firm emerged from stealth on November 23 with $112 million in strategic backing anchored by an undisclosed family office, led by a founder who previously ran AI in private equity at Boston Consulting Group and a studio head who previously led AI at Citadel. It plans to form three to five independent companies a year from its enterprise AI engagements. Its claim that existing engagements are on pace to create more than $1 billion in enterprise value is a company projection, not booked revenue, and it is recorded here as a projection, not a fact.</p>
<h2>How to read this ledger</h2>
<p>Three questions separate a useful reading from a credulous one. First, is the valuation pre-money or post-money? None of the November 23 reports disclosed that, so no dilution math is offered here, and the ratios that can be computed, Marlowe's round at about 24 percent of its stated $1.15 billion valuation, Agon's at about 16 percent of its stated $450 million, and Sierra's at about 6 percent of its stated $30 billion valuation, are only meaningful once that disclosure exists. Second, is the money for something that ships, or something that opens later? Marlowe's first site opens in December 2026, coprod's full component line is not due until the end of 2027, and Acorda is still in trials, so most of this money is a bet on delivery, not a reward for it. Third, does the round's sector match the investor's thesis, or is it a headline bet? Heal Capital backing pharma compliance, Founderful and PROTOTYPE backing robotics hardware, and Concept Ventures backing ex-Monzo fraud fighters all fit the investors' known theses, which is a better sign than a famous name on an unfamiliar sector. The ledger itself is a snapshot of rounds announced on one day, November 23, 2026, converted at the stated exchange rates, the same method as <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/six-ai-raises-added-up-232m-for-ai-that.html">the October ledger</a>. Sierra's own announcement says it is raising $1.75 billion; TechCrunch reports the round as raised. The ledger records announcements, so Sierra counts from its announcement date. It will be wrong the moment a round reprices or is corrected, and that is why every figure carries its source.</p>
