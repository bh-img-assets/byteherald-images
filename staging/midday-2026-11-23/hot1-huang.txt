<p><a href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director">Nvidia's board voted unanimously on November 23, 2026, to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive officer, and board director, effective immediately</a>, twelve days after he resigned. The board said it was satisfied about Huang's candor and ability to lead, that it had received input from senior levels of the United States government, and that it is confident his reinstatement poses no national security risk. Nvidia shares rose 33 percent in early trading, at one point reaching 35 percent, the biggest intraday jump since 2000, adding roughly $1 trillion in market value. The board reversed itself in twelve days, and the market treated the return as the end of the uncertainty that had battered AI stocks for a week.</p>

<h2>The 12-day reversal</h2>
<p>Huang resigned on November 11, 2026, after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman suspected by United States authorities of spying for China. Six days later, <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/sec-probes-nvidia-huang-pitches-return.html">we reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission had opened a civil probe into Nvidia's disclosures, that Huang had told associates he would be honored to return, and that the board was not considering reinstatement</a>, with the stock down more than 10 percent since his departure. On November 23 the board did the opposite of what it signaled on November 17: it voted unanimously to bring him back, thanked interim chief executive Colette Kress for her service, and said Kress continues as chief financial officer. Board chair Harvey C. Jones said the board believes Huang is the right person to lead Nvidia and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of stockholders, employees, partners, and customers.</p>

<h2>Monday's scoreboard</h2>
<p>The market reaction was the largest single-day AI rally of the year. Nvidia's 33 percent early gain, peaking at 35 percent, was its biggest intraday jump since 2000, and the stock added roughly $1 trillion in market value during the session. The Nasdaq rose 6 percent and the S&P 500 rose 4 percent in early trading, according to Reuters, and by late morning Bloomberg had the S&P up 5 percent and the Nasdaq 100 up 7 percent. The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 13 percent, and the Nasdaq 100 was on track for a more than $2 trillion surge in market value, its biggest one-day percentage gain since April 2025. By late morning, the rally was broad: Micron rose 19.4 percent, Intel rose about 14 percent, Qualcomm rose 9.2 percent, AMD rose about 8 percent, and Microsoft and Amazon each added close to 4 percent.</p>

<p>The scale only makes sense against the week before it. The Nasdaq had fallen 10 percent the previous week, its biggest decline since the March 2020 pandemic selloff, and Wall Street's fear gauge had pushed above 40 for the first time since April 2025. Taiwan's chipmaker-heavy benchmark posted its biggest weekly fall since 1990. One widely quoted investor line captured the turn: Glen Smith of GDS Wealth Management said the reinstatement removes a key level of uncertainty from the AI trade, which had been suffering for weeks. Reuters put the same point in market terms, noting that Huang's return removes a great deal of uncertainty from the more highly leveraged part of the market, the neocloud companies that rent out AI compute on borrowed money, which is the corner of the trade <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-32-trillion-dollar-ai-debt-nobody.html">we tracked in our $3.2 trillion off-balance-sheet debt ledger</a>.</p>
<h2>What the board actually decided</h2>
<p>The reinstatement came with governance changes, not just a personnel change. The board said an independent-directors committee formed after the reporting that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort will proceed to its conclusion, with independent counsel assisting, and that Huang fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating. The board also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee, made up solely of independent directors, to oversee trade compliance and national security risks, and Nvidia has begun a search for a chief compliance officer, a new position that will report to the board's nominating and corporate governance committee. The company said it will continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries. President Donald Trump welcomed the board's decision, according to Reuters. One succession subplot closed the same day: Arm chief executive Rene Haas pledged to stay in his role through 2033 after rebuffing approaches to succeed Huang, according to Bloomberg.</p>
<h2>What is still open</h2>
<p>A reinstatement removes the leadership question. It does not close the investigations. The independent committee's probe is still running, the SEC's civil probe into Nvidia's disclosures is still open, and a House interview is set for December 1, as we reported last week. Kress, who ran the company for twelve days as interim chief executive, had already ended H200 chip sales in China, a decision that stands. The market question is narrower and harder: the 33 percent jump repriced Nvidia on the assumption that the governance crisis is over, but the committee's conclusion has not been published, and the export-control exposure that sits behind the whole episode has not changed. What to watch next is the committee's conclusion, the SEC probe's outcome, and whether the rally holds past the first week, when the news is no longer the story and the numbers have to carry it.</p>
<p>The return also narrows the window for the companies betting against Nvidia's dominance. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/marlowe-raised-277m-to-ditch-nvidia-it.html">Marlowe raised $277 million this morning to build AI infrastructure on non-Nvidia chips</a>, and its pitch was, in part, that the AI trade needed an alternative while Nvidia was in turmoil. That turmoil lasted twelve days. The challengers still have their money and their sites, with Marlowe's first Etched site due in December 2026, but the urgency that made their case has faded, and the incumbent they were built to route around is back at full strength.</p>
