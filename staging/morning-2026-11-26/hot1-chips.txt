<p><a href="https://www.reuters.com/world/china/us-chip-curbs-distillation-crackdown-threaten-chinese-ai-model-development-2026-11-26/">Reuters reported on November 26, 2026 that Z.AI may push its GLM-5.5 flagship into the first quarter of 2027 or later, and that Moonshot AI expects its release cycle to stretch from six months to 12 months or longer if overseas computing access is cut off</a>. Both delays rest on anonymous sourcing. Neither company confirmed the new timing to Reuters. The rule behind the compute squeeze is public, and its text sets a clear threshold.</p>

<h2>What slipped, and what did not</h2>
<p>Z.AI, also known as Zhipu AI, planned to release GLM-5.5 in November 2026, according to two people familiar with the matter who spoke to Reuters on condition of anonymity. The model is designed with roughly 3 trillion parameters. One source said stronger safeguards against distillation held up training, and that new United States computing restrictions could move the launch from a hoped-for year-end release into early 2027. Z.AI did not immediately respond to a Reuters request for comment.</p>
<p>Moonshot faces a different exposure. The company rents computing power and prioritises overseas providers, according to a person with knowledge of its operations. That person estimated that Kimi K4 could arrive around the middle of 2027. Moonshot released Kimi K2 in July 2025, K2.5 in January 2026, and K3 in July 2026. Those dates give two six-month intervals. A mid-2027 K4 would make the next interval about 12 months, twice as long. Moonshot did not immediately respond to Reuters either.</p>

<p>One release did land. Z.AI shipped GLM-5.4 on November 23, 2026, built on an existing 750 billion parameter model. The company says a new design matches models with 2 trillion to 3 trillion parameters. Reuters reports the claim without endorsing it, and no independent benchmark in the Reuters report settles it. A smaller model shipping on time does not cancel a flagship delay. It shows a fallback exists while the larger training run waits.</p>

<h2>The rule behind the compute squeeze</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-11-19/pdf/2026-23529.pdf">The Bureau of Industry and Security interim final rule took effect on November 19, 2026</a>. It requires a license for advanced semiconductor exports, reexports, and transfers tied to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant computing power to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau, including through an ultimate parent in those places. The rule sets the significant-volume line at 12,000,000 aggregate TPP. TPP measures processing performance. Reuters puts that line at roughly 750 Nvidia H100 chips. Data centers already providing covered services before the rule have 90 days to obtain licenses, which runs to February 17, 2027. Commercial recommender systems sit outside the license requirement.</p>
<p>The rule also adds license requirements for advanced semiconductor shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Brazil, with an exception for companies headquartered in the United States that meet reporting requirements. Remote access matters because renting time on chips abroad can train a model without importing the chips. The rule targets that rental path directly. It does not ban all overseas computing. It licenses large volumes for covered companies and five diversion-risk destinations.</p>
<p>Reuters reports two adaptation moves. Moonshot stepped up efforts to rent computing power in Europe and plans to approach Chinese chipmakers about training processors, according to a separate source. The Information reported last week that Moonshot scrapped a 150 million dollar deal for Nvidia Blackwell access through an Indonesian data center, and Moonshot declined to comment to that publication. Europe rentals and domestic chips may soften the squeeze. The Reuters sources say computing capacity inside China could not make up the shortfall if overseas access ended entirely. That capacity claim comes from one source and carries no public capacity table behind it.</p>
<h2>Distillation got harder at the same time</h2>
<p>Distillation trains a smaller or cheaper model on answers from a stronger model. Done without authorization at industrial scale, it lets a lab copy capability without paying the full training cost. Reuters reports that Anthropic said in September 2026 it had observed nearly 200 million exchanges with Claude models linked to suspected distillation campaigns, attributed to Chinese developers including Alibaba, Moonshot, and Z.AI. Those attributions are Anthropic allegations. Reuters also reports that Moonshot previously rejected suggestions that Kimi K3 performance came from distillation, telling National Business Daily in July 2026 that gains came from original architecture changes.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.anthropic.com/news/distillation-defenses">Anthropic published its defense results on October 12, 2026</a>. The company says early evidence suggests harvested data fell by around 90 to 99 percent, and that reasoning tokens extracted by distillers ran 10 to 100 times lower after its classifier shipped. Anthropic measured those gains on its own platform. No outside auditor verified them in the post. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/openai-cut-model-theft-from-54-to-02.html">Our November 20 audit of OpenAI distillation defenses found the same pattern</a>: large self-measured drops, with attack success falling faster than any proof about student model quality. Readers weighing the Reuters delay claim should hold both facts at once. Defenses tightened, and the size of the tightening rests on lab measurements.</p>
<p>The cost point is the strategic core. Reuters frames the combined measures as a threat to the lower-cost path that helped Chinese labs compete under export controls. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/chinas-best-ai-model-is-19-points.html">Our November 16 scoreboard put China's best model 19 points behind the world leader on Artificial Analysis</a>, with far lower cost per task. Slower flagship cycles would widen a capability gap only if rivals keep their pace. The November 26 report does not measure rival pace, training budgets, or domestic chip supply. It establishes reported delays and a public rule. It does not establish that Chinese frontier development stops.</p>
