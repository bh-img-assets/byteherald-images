<p>Alibaba launched Qwen4-Max on November 16, 2026, a flagship model with a 1-million-token context window priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens, less than a quarter of what Anthropic charges for output tokens on Claude Opus 5.5. It debuts at No. 14 on the Agent Arena board, the top Chinese model there, and Alibaba ranks as the No. 5 lab overall. The catch is in the fine print: the weights are closed, the headline efficiency numbers come from Alibaba's own tests, and no outside lab has checked the model yet.</p>

<h2>The scoreboard, in numbers</h2>
<p>The independent boards moved fast. LMArena's Agent Arena puts Qwen4-Max at No. 14 overall on its debut, up from No. 39 for Qwen3.8-Max, a net improvement of 7.5 percent across about 14,700 votes, and names it the top Chinese model on that board. On Code Arena's WebDev board it lands at No. 6 with a preliminary 1,750 points, 74 points above Qwen3.8-Max at 1,676. Artificial Analysis scores it 43 on the Intelligence Index at extra-high reasoning effort, 9 points above Qwen3.8-Max and 3 points behind Kimi K3.1 Pro at about 11 percent lower cost per task.</p>

<p>Two warnings belong with the table. Arena scores are preliminary and move as more votes come in, so the WebDev 1,750 is a snapshot, not a final ranking. And the Artificial Analysis figure is at extra-high reasoning effort, the most expensive way to run the model, so it says nothing about what the same score costs at a normal setting.</p>

<h2>What the price buys</h2>
<p>Qwen4-Max runs on a new mixture-of-experts architecture with a 2.4-trillion-parameter backbone, 71 billion parameters active per token, and a further 328 billion N-gram embedding parameters, according to the Qwen team's announcement. It reads text, images and video natively, takes up to 1 million tokens of context, and caps thinking plus the final answer at 262,144 tokens. Alibaba says it beats Qwen3.8-Max while using up to about 50 percent fewer tokens, and that the average cost per task on its own Work360 evaluation fell from $4.43 to $1.67, a 62 percent drop, with the Work360 score itself at 52.69, about 10 percent higher than its predecessor. The API is live on QwenCloud, and the model is also in Qwen Studio, QwenWork and Qoder. Smaller Qwen4 models will get open weights later, Alibaba says, without giving a date.</p>
<p>The price is the story. At $1.50 in and $4.50 out, Qwen4-Max undercuts every frontier flagship on the list: Google's Gemini 4 Carbon costs $2 and $10 through November 30, Claude Opus 5.5 costs $4 and $20, Claude Fable 5.2 costs $8 and $40, and GPT-6 Astra costs $10 and $50. Cached input is $0.075 per million tokens, against $0.10 for Carbon and $0.20 for Opus 5.5. The gap is widest where it matters most for agent work, on output tokens, where Qwen charges less than a quarter of Anthropic's flagship rate. That is the same pricing pressure <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/gemini-4-carbon-leads-index-but-you.html">Gemini 4 Carbon</a> brought when it opened to paying developers on November 10, and it fits the pattern <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/ai-got-75-cheaper-discounts-are-expiring.html">the October price cuts</a> set: the frontier is getting cheaper, and the cuts now arrive with dates attached. Qwen's price carries no stated expiry, which is the one way it differs from Carbon's $2 rate, which doubles on December 1.</p>
<h2>The safety pitch, and what it does not prove</h2>
<p>Alibaba is selling safety as hard as price. The company says it filtered Qwen4-Max's training data by purpose, keeping material for defensive work such as finding and fixing vulnerabilities while removing data meant to improve attack capabilities. Internal experts then reviewed long-running task sessions that included the models' full chains of thought. The company concludes that "no capabilities posing large-scale cybersecurity risks have been observed." Its pass rate on the Last Secure Code Bench, for code that both works and is secure, rose from 22 percent to 31.8 percent. The company says it did dedicated safety training for chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear risks.</p>
<p>Every one of those claims is Alibaba's own. No external red team has tested the model yet, and the safety numbers come from the same team that built it. That does not make them false. It makes them unverified, and the difference matters for a model whose selling point is that it was trained to avoid offensive hacking skills. The safeguards disclosure itself is real and dated: Alibaba detailed the new safeguards on November 16, the same day as the launch, and Reuters reported the disclosure as part of a wider step-up in test disclosures by Chinese developers.</p>
<h2>The catch</h2>
<p>The launch-day excitement deserves a cooler read. First, the weights are closed. Qwen built its reputation on open-weight models, and Max breaks that pattern: you can rent it, you cannot download it, and the open-weight Qwen4 models have no date. Second, the efficiency case rests on Work360, Alibaba's own benchmark, and the 62 percent cost drop compares Qwen4-Max with Alibaba's own older model, not with its rivals. Third, the preview that opened on November 11 ran on the Token Plan subscription only, and that preview build retires on November 25. The November 16 launch is the full model on the API, not the preview, and early arena scores are the first independent signal, not a verdict.</p>
<p>None of that makes Qwen4-Max a bad model. It makes it a model to judge the way every flagship should be judged: on independent boards rather than vendor charts, on the price you actually pay per finished task rather than the price per token, and on whether the safety claims survive outside testing. The boards so far say it is the strongest Chinese model and the fifth-ranked lab's best work. The price says it is the cheapest frontier ticket on the list. What is missing is the part no launch post can supply, which is time.</p>
<h2>What to watch</h2>
<p>Three dates and numbers decide whether this launch sticks. The first is the arena boards over the next two weeks, as the preliminary WebDev and Agent Arena scores settle with more votes. The second is the open-weight Qwen4 schedule, which Alibaba has not given: a dated release would tell developers whether Max is a closed flagship with an open family behind it, or a closed turn for the Qwen line. The third is the price itself. Qwen's $1.50 and $4.50 carry no expiry date in the announcement, unlike Carbon's introductory rate, so the question is whether Alibaba holds the rate once usage grows, or follows the pattern of its rivals and reprices on a calendar.</p>
