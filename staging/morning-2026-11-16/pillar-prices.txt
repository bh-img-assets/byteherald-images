<p>Between November 9 and December 1, 2026, the cheapest frontier AI ticket changes hands twice. Anthropic cut Claude Fable 5.2 to $8 per million input tokens on November 9, Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon to paying developers at $2 on November 10, and both Google models double to $4 on December 1, landing exactly on Claude Opus 5.5's price. A buyer who locks a workload to one model without reading the expiry dates pays double on December 1 for the same tokens. This is the dated calendar that decides what AI costs, and every date on it is already published on a vendor pricing page.</p>

<p>Two models sit just outside the table and matter to the same buyer. GPT-6.1 Sol, OpenAI's balanced default, costs $2 in and $10 out with cached input at $0.10, the same as Carbon's introductory rate, and carries no dated change. GPT-5.6 Sol, the older model it replaced, sits on a promotional $4 and $20 that OpenAI says is available until November 30, 2026, with no successor price published. And Carbon is not free at any tier: Google lists every Carbon line as not available on the free tier, and consumer access in the Gemini app needs a Google AI Ultra subscription. The question people ask most about Gemini 4, whether it is cheaper than ChatGPT, has a per-token answer and a per-task answer. Per token, Carbon at $2 and $10 through November 30 undercuts GPT-6 Astra at $10 and $50 fivefold on input and output, and matches GPT-6.1 Sol exactly. Per finished task, the comparison turns on how many tokens each model needs, how much thinking it bills, and how often it retries, none of which a price list shows.</p>

<h2>Why the calendar matters</h2>
<p>The price war did not end with the October cuts. It went calendar-driven, and the calendar is the story now. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/ai-got-75-cheaper-discounts-are-expiring.html">The October chapter</a> found that the cuts had already happened and the next moves were up, with Argon doubling on December 1 and Flash doubling on January 1 flagged as the rises to watch. November added a second round of evidence, and it cut both ways: a new cut from Anthropic on November 9, a new entrant at a quarter of the frontier price on November 10, and the same December 1 step-up, now priced to the dollar. The honest caveat from October still holds: these are list prices, and batch processing, cache rates and context-size thresholds move real bills away from the table. Batch halves the standard rate on all three vendors, cached input runs from $0.075 to $1.00 depending on the model, and prompts over 272,000 tokens on OpenAI's flagships bill at double the input rate and 1.5 times the output rate. A workload that fits the table's assumptions pays the table's prices. One that does not, does not.</p>
<p>The three vendors are not moving together. Anthropic cut its top model by 20 percent and posted no dated change, so its side of the calendar is the stable one. Google opened its cheapest frontier model at half of Anthropic's mid-tier price and posted the doubling in the same announcement. OpenAI left the one open question on the calendar: GPT-5.6 Sol's promotional price ends November 30 and the page does not say what replaces it. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/gemini-4-carbon-leads-index-but-you.html">Carbon's launch audit</a> in October covered the model when it was gated and unusable; the November change is that it opened to paying developers with a price and an expiry on that price. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/us-vs-china-ai-revenue-105-billion.html">The revenue gap</a> we tracked in November showed the same pricing pressure from the Chinese open models; the Big Three frontier is now running the same play with dated rates instead of open ones.</p>
<h2>What to check before you commit</h2>
<p>First, is the rate an introduction with a date, or a standing price? Carbon's $2 is the first, Opus 5.5's $4 is the second, and the difference is $8 against $16 on the small workload above. Second, does your workload match the assumptions the table uses? A job that leans on cached input pays Carbon's $0.10 against Astra's $1.00, a tenfold gap the headline rates hide, and a job over 272,000 tokens on OpenAI pays the long-context multiplier the table does not show. Third, what does the model cost the day after the date? December 1 turns the cheapest frontier ticket into a tie with Opus 5.5, and January 1 doubles the Flash tier that most high-volume work actually runs on. The calendar rewards the buyer who checks the date before the workload, not after the invoice.</p>
