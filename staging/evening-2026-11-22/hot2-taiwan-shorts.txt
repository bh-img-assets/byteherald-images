<p>Taiwan <a href="https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/taiwan-tightens-short-selling-rules-after-ai-linked-stock-market-losses">banned short selling below the previous day's closing price</a> on Saturday, November 21, 2026, after the Taiex fell 17.5 percent in five sessions, the worst week for the index since 1990. The Financial Supervisory Commission announced five emergency measures that take effect on Monday, November 23, including a rise in the minimum margin for short sales from 130 percent to 150 percent and a suspension of new put-warrant applications. The National Stabilization Fund issued a <a href="https://finance.biggo.com/news/f683c139-d754-4dbf-8da7-bb114228fdd3">rare weekend statement</a> confirming it had entered the market to support prices. The package is the strongest official response yet to a sell-off that started in the United States and hit Taiwan hardest, because Taiwan makes the hardware the AI trade runs on.</p>

<h2>The five measures, and what each one does</h2>
<table><thead><tr><th>Measure</th><th>What it does</th><th>Effective</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Below-close short-sale ban</td><td>Short selling below the previous session's closing price is prohibited for all listed and over-the-counter shares and Taiwan Depositary Receipts, covering 1,986 stocks</td><td>November 23, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Higher short-sale margin</td><td>Minimum collateral for short sales rises from 130 percent to 150 percent of the position, making bearish bets more expensive</td><td>November 23, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Put warrants suspended</td><td>Acceptance of new applications from securities firms to list put warrants, which gain value when prices fall, is temporarily suspended</td><td>November 23, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Securities lending curbed</td><td>Lenders are called on to stop lending shares, recall existing loans early, or avoid renewing them; these are calls, not blanket mandatory bans</td><td>November 21, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Buybacks encouraged</td><td>Listed companies are encouraged to buy back their own shares to support prices</td><td>November 21, 2026</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The package widens curbs the commission had introduced only two days earlier. On Thursday, November 19, it restricted short selling only in stocks that had fallen at least 3.5 percent in the previous session. The new rule removes that threshold entirely: any stock that fell in the prior session is covered. Hedging trades by securities firms and futures commission merchants are exempt, and the commission said its goal was orderly trading and market stability, not rescuing any particular company.</p>
<h2>The week, in numbers</h2>
<p>The Taiex closed on Friday, November 20, at <a href="https://asiadaily.org/news/19397/">36,033.29</a>, down 6.23 percent on the day and down 2,393.60 points, the third-largest single-session point loss on record. Over five sessions the index lost 7,641.83 points, or 17.5 percent, and more than 300 listed and over-the-counter companies hit their daily limit-down levels. The index is now 28.48 percent below its October 6 intraday record. Trading turnover surged to 877.35 billion New Taiwan dollars, about 26.6 billion dollars, as investors rushed to adjust positions.</p>
<p>The losses were concentrated exactly where the AI trade lives. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., the world's largest contract chipmaker, fell 4.76 percent on Friday to 2,000 New Taiwan dollars. Foxconn fell 4.76 percent as well, while MediaTek and Delta Electronics dropped to their daily limits. Retail investors turned to odd-lot trading to buy discounted shares of major firms and exchange-traded funds, a sharp contrast with the institutional selling driving the decline. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/the-correction-scoreboard-6-trillion.html">The correction scoreboard we published on Friday</a> shows the American side of the same week: the S and P 500 down 3.70 percent on Friday and the Nasdaq down 5.28 percent, with both the S and P 500 and the Dow Jones closing the week in correction territory.</p>

<h2>What the curbs do not fix</h2>
<p>A short-selling ban changes who can bet against the market, not why the market is falling. The stated cause in the local coverage is concern about the global AI industry's outlook: whether the returns on massive AI infrastructure spending will ever arrive, and whether the spending itself can continue. That question is decided in the United States, by American earnings and American capital spending, not in Taipei. Taiwan's regulator can slow the selling; it cannot answer the question behind it.</p>
<p>The measures can also cut both ways. Investors who use short positions to hedge long books may find themselves unable to hedge, and some may respond by selling holdings outright to raise cash, adding pressure rather than relieving it. Analysts in Taipei argued the package should still help by squeezing out bearish positioning, with one strategist noting that margin deleveraging has run ahead of the index's decline, a sign the market may be close to a floor. Another put the drop from recent highs at close to 30 percent and called it an overshoot relative to fundamentals. Both readings agree on one thing: the measures buy time, not certainty.</p>
<p>The stabilization fund's statement is the part to watch next. The fund said state funds had formally entered the market after its November 6 emergency committee meeting authorized stabilization work, and that it would continue to take necessary measures as conditions warrant. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/nvidia-fell-14-as-sec-probe-turned-to.html">The American trigger for the week's selling</a> was Nvidia's 14 percent fall on Friday as the SEC probe turned to its accounting, on top of a correction that had already been running for weeks. If American tech steadies, Taiwan's curbs will look like a well-timed brake. If it does not, the ban will be tested on Monday, when it takes effect.</p>
