<p>OpenAI's release notes for November 20, 2026, say ChatGPT Space is rolling out globally to Plus users aged 18 and older. Space replaces Library in the sidebar and turns ChatGPT into a shared workspace for Pages and Slides, where people can build documents and presentations together with ChatGPT inside the workspace. Two things Plus users asked for are not in this rollout: Dots, the agents that would do the work, and Sheets, the spreadsheets.</p>

<h2>What Space is</h2>
<p>Space is where ChatGPT now keeps pages, slides, images, and uploaded files, in one place instead of scattered through chat history. OpenAI introduced it at its DevDay event on September 29 and 30, 2026, for Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans. The November 20 change is the guest list: Plus users, who pay 20 dollars a month, can now create their own Spaces. To start, select Space from the left-hand navigation bar, where Library used to be, click the plus icon, and choose a template or a blank Space, then upload pages, slides, spreadsheets, images, or website links.</p>
<p>Pages are the new document type inside Space, built for work people do with agents: writing, research, coding, and charts. A Page can hold text, images, charts, and files, and teammates can edit and share it. OpenAI says Pages can stay updated from connected tools, so a dashboard or a tracker can refresh itself instead of being rebuilt. Slides are the presentation editor: collaborative slides with editable charts, simultaneous multi-user editing, and export to PowerPoint, PDF, or Google Slides. Space also works with ChatGPT's Scheduled Tasks, so a routine around work that keeps changing can run on a schedule. Mobile editing is part of the package too: Pages can be created and edited on the web, on desktop, and on iOS, and read and shared on other mobile devices.</p>
<h2>What Plus gets, and what it does not</h2>

<p>The table is the whole story of this rollout. Plus gets the workspace and the documents, but not the agents. Dots are the AI agents that handle tasks on their own with little supervision, and OpenAI began testing them with a small group of Plus subscribers on November 19, a day before Space itself arrived for Plus. For now, a Plus user can build a Page with ChatGPT and invite people to edit it, but cannot hand the work to a Dot. Sheets, the spreadsheet piece, is signalled for a future release and is not here yet for any plan.</p>

<h2>The three catches</h2>
<p>The first catch is the missing agent. Space is sold as a place where people, ChatGPT, and each person's Dot all work from the same pile of project knowledge, but the Dot is the one piece Plus does not get. That matters because the agent is what would keep a Page updated and do the routine work. Without it, Plus users get a shared document editor with ChatGPT attached, not the autonomous workspace OpenAI describes.</p>
<p>The second catch is sharing. To open a shared Page or presentation, someone needs permission and must be signed in to a Plus, Pro, Business, or Enterprise account with Space access. Free users cannot open shared pages at all, and public links are not available. For a client or a colleague outside the paid wall, an exported deck is the practical handover, and Slides supports view and edit access but currently has no separate comment-only role.</p>
<p>The third catch is the self-updating Page. OpenAI says Pages can update themselves from connected tools, and its announcement says ChatGPT can keep them updated automatically. One report on the rollout says that self-updating is not live yet, so treat it as announced, not shipped, until OpenAI's own help pages show it working. The gap between the announcement and the feature is the part of this launch to watch, because a Page that refreshes itself is the difference between a document and a dashboard.</p>
<h2>Why OpenAI is doing this</h2>
<p>Space is the second step in a pattern. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/chatgpt-can-now-read-your-icloud-mail.html">In October, ChatGPT gained the ability to read iCloud Mail and Calendar</a>, and <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/googles-universal-cart-ai-shopping.html">Google answered with its own Universal Cart for AI shopping the same month</a>. The direction is the same in all three moves: the chatbot is becoming a place where work happens, not just a box that answers questions. Space makes that explicit by putting the document, the collaborators, and the model in one room. The question for Plus users is narrower and more practical: the workspace is now open to them, but the two pieces that would make it feel like a workspace, the agents and the spreadsheets, are still on the other side of the paywall.</p>
