<p>Amazon will own 14.7 percent of Anthropic after its initial public offering and Google will own 13.7 percent, for 28.4 percent combined, according to an amended filing Anthropic submitted to the United States Securities and Exchange Commission on Tuesday, November 17, 2026. Both stakes sit in non-voting stock, so the two cloud giants will own more than a quarter of the company behind Claude and none of its general vote. Anthropic is marketing its shares at 70 to 78 dollars each, with pricing expected on Wednesday evening, November 18, and trading to begin on Thursday, November 19, on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.</p>

<h2>What the filing says</h2>
<p>The amended filing fills in the ownership table that Anthropic's original October 26 filing left blank. Amazon will hold 1.92 billion Class N non-voting shares, or 34 percent of that share class. Google will hold 1.79 billion, or 32 percent. The seven co-founders will hold 12.8 percent of the shares and 61.3 percent of the voting power. Put the three groups together and Amazon, Google and the founders will own about 41.1 percent of Anthropic, worth about 398.4 billion dollars at the 74 dollar midpoint of the price range.</p>

<p>The combined stake is worth roughly 260 billion to 290 billion dollars depending on where the shares price inside the 70 to 78 dollar range. A separate private placement running alongside the IPO adds a second layer: seven technology companies, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners, agreed to buy 26 billion dollars of the same non-voting stock at the IPO price. Google and Nvidia each agreed to buy 10 billion dollars of it. Google's 10 billion dollars buys 135.1 million non-voting shares. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/11/anthropics-644b-ipo-file-every-number.html">We audited the full IPO file on November 10</a>, when the raise, the valuation and the revenue were the news; the ownership percentages are what the November 17 amendment adds.</p>
<h2>The cap that keeps Amazon at 12 percent</h2>
<p>Amazon's stake could have been much bigger. Most of its investment came as convertible notes, loans that turn into stock, with conversion prices of about 2.09 dollars or 5.05 dollars a share depending on the year of the investment. Amazon still holds 6.203 billion dollars of those notes unconverted, carried on Anthropic's books at 108.6 billion dollars of fair value. If Amazon converted all of them today it would own about 3.48 billion shares, or 23.8 percent of the company, worth roughly 272 billion dollars at the top of the price range.</p>
<p>It is not doing that. The filing says Amazon "has elected not to waive" an ownership cap, and the notes only convert while doing so would not lift Amazon's stake above 12 percent of Anthropic's capitalization. Google has its own cap: shares issued under its 2026 agreement are limited to 14.99 percent of Anthropic on a fully converted basis unless that limit is waived. The caps are the mechanism that lets two of the world's largest technology companies be Anthropic's biggest economic owners while its founders keep control.</p>
<h2>What the money bought</h2>
<p>The stakes are the end of a three-year spending trail. Amazon's cash into Anthropic totals 18 billion dollars as of June 30, 2026: 8 billion dollars of convertible notes bought from late 2023 through late 2025, plus 5 billion dollars of Series G preferred stock in April 2026 and 5 billion dollars of Series H in May 2026. Under an April 2026 agreement Amazon can still provide up to 15 billion dollars more in stock or notes. Google's trail is smaller: 2 billion dollars of notes from 2023 to 2025, about 1.3 billion dollars more in a note from August 2024, and about 10 billion dollars of preferred stock in March 2026, on top of the 10 billion dollars it is buying in the private placement.</p>
<p>The money also bought compute commitments that run in both directions. Anthropic committed to spend more than 100 billion dollars on Amazon Web Services over the next decade, and Google is also one of its cloud providers, selling Claude through its own cloud platform. That two-way flow is the part of the filing that matters most for the independence question: the stakes carry no votes, but the cloud bills are the largest numbers in Anthropic's future. <a href="https://www.byteherald.com/2026/10/michael-burrys-537-billion-anthropic.html">Michael Burry tallied the same commitments in October</a> and put the total at about 537 billion dollars, with hyperscaler minimums alone at 269.4 billion dollars; the filing now shows who sits on the other side of those minimums.</p>

<h2>Non-voting stock, explained</h2>
<p>Anthropic has two classes of common stock. Class A carries the votes. Class N, the class Amazon and Google hold, carries no general vote, but it can receive dividends and it converts into Class A voting shares when it is sold. That is why the filing can say the founders hold 61.3 percent of the voting power while owning 12.8 percent of the shares, and why Amazon and Google together hold 34.8 percent of the shares with the Public Benefit Fund and 0 percent of the general vote. The structure is deliberate: it was designed so that early money from the cloud giants could fund the company without handing them the company. The trade-off is visible in the same filing. A buyer of Class N stock owns the economics of Anthropic's growth and none of its governance, and the only way that changes is a sale, which converts the shares into voting stock in someone else's hands.</p>
<h2>What this does not establish</h2>
<p>The filing does not establish that Amazon or Google control Anthropic, and the numbers point the other way: the founders' 61.3 percent of the vote is the control, and the caps were written to keep it that way. It does not establish the final value of either stake, because the 260 to 290 billion dollar combined figure depends on a price that will not be set until Wednesday evening. Amazon's 23.8 percent is hypothetical, not a holding: the cap blocks it, and Amazon has said it will not waive the cap. And the filing does not settle whether the cloud commitments help or hurt Anthropic's independence, because the same 100 billion dollar AWS commitment is both a guaranteed customer and a guaranteed bill. That question will be answered by the contracts, not by the cap table.</p>
